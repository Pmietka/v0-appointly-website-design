# When Should I Add a Second Crew to My Floor Coating Business?

**Meta Description:** Add a second crew when the install backlog holds month after month and your leads can already feed two crews. Fix show rate first: 80% with no reminders, 95% with.

---

Add a second crew when your install backlog stays long month after month in season, and your lead flow can already feed two crews, not after one busy month. Before you hire, fix the calendar: about 80% of booked estimates show with no reminders and about 95% show with confirmation and reminders, so the capacity you think you lack may already be sitting in empty estimate slots.

## Key takeaways

- A second crew should be hired from steady demand, not from one record month or a spring rush.
- Estimates booking weeks out is usually an owner capacity problem, not an install capacity problem.
- A second crew needs roughly twice the booked estimates of one crew, and the leads to produce them, before the first paycheck.
- Show rate is hidden capacity. Moving from about 80% to about 95% shown gives you more sold floors without a single new hire.
- Quality slips first when the owner is no longer on every slab, so a written prep checklist and a proven crew lead come before crew two.
- Outside Florida, the Gulf Coast, and the Carolinas, demand drops in winter, so a crew hired in fall has to survive the slow months.

## What are the signs you actually need a second crew?

A busy feeling is not a signal. These are:

- **The install backlog stays long every week in season.** Not one month. Month after month, homeowners are waiting longer for an install date than you want them to.
- **You are turning down work you would normally sell.** Not small or out of area jobs. Good floors in your area that you pass on because the crew is full.
- **Lead flow is steady without heroics.** You did not get there by tripling spend for a few weeks. The leads come in at the same pace week after week.
- **Your close rate and average ticket are holding.** A busy crew that is closing well is ready to grow. A busy crew that is discounting to fill days is not.

## Is a long estimate wait a crew problem or an owner problem?

Usually an owner problem. If estimates are booking weeks out but installs are not, the crew is not the bottleneck. You are. Every estimate slot is a time you are not on a slab, and you only have so many.

That changes the fix. A second install crew will not open estimate slots. A crew lead who runs installs without you, or an estimator, will. Our guide to [hiring a floor coating salesperson or estimator](/guides/hire-floor-coating-salesperson-estimator) walks through that choice.

Long estimate waits also hurt directly: estimates that sit weeks out are the ones that slip.

## Add a crew or not yet? A decision table

| Signal | What it usually means | Add a crew or not yet |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Estimates booking weeks out, installs on time | The owner is the sales bottleneck | Not yet. Add estimate capacity first |
| Install backlog long for one month only | A rush, often spring | Not yet. Book out with deposits |
| Install backlog long month after month in season | Real install demand | Add a crew, if lead flow can feed it |
| Turning away good floors in your area | Demand is ahead of capacity | Add a crew, after checking show rate |
| Empty estimate slots from no-shows | Calendar leak, not a demand problem | Not yet. Fix reminders |
| Owner installs all day and sells at night | Owner is doing two jobs | Not yet. Move the owner off the crew first |
| Close rate dropping as volume rises | Rushed estimates or discounting | Not yet. Fix the sales process |
| Busy only in warm months in a cold market | Seasonal demand | Not yet, unless winter work is lined up |

## Why is show rate hidden capacity?

Because every no-show is a sales slot you paid for and could not use. Our [floor coating benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) put show rate at about 80% with no reminders. With a confirmation at booking, reminders 24 hours and 1 hour out, and someone following up when the homeowner goes quiet, it is about 95%.

> **From the field:** The owners who feel out of capacity often have a calendar problem, not a crew problem. Across floor coating accounts, about 80% of booked estimates show with no reminders and about 95% show with confirmation and reminders. Closing that gap fills days with sold floors before anyone new is on payroll, so fix the calendar before you staff up.

## How do you make sure lead flow can feed two crews?

Start from what one crew needs. Our guide to [how many estimates a week one crew needs](/guides/how-many-estimates-a-week-one-crew-floor-coating) has the formula: floors per week, divided by close rate, divided by show rate, gives booked estimates. Then divide by your lead to booked rate, which should be 60 to 70% when leads come from a landing page and are called fast.

These are example numbers, not a client's results. Every step is rounded up.

| Crews | Example floors a week | Example close rate | Estimates to run | Booked, 95% show | Booked, 80% show | Leads needed, 95% show |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| One crew | 3 | 45% | 7 | 8 | 9 | 12 to 14 |
| Two crews | 6 | 45% | 14 | 15 | 18 | 22 to 25 |

Two things to take from it. A second crew does not need a few more estimates. It needs roughly double. And at an 80% show rate, two crews need 18 booked estimates a week instead of 15, which is three wasted trips every week.

Then prove the volume before the hire:

1. **Book the estimates first.** Raise ad spend or widen the service area and see whether you can hold the two crew estimate number for a stretch of weeks. If you cannot, a second crew will sit idle.
2. **Check lead to booked rate.** If leads are not booking at 60 to 70%, more leads will mostly mean more chasing. Our post on [lead to appointment rate](/blog/floor-coating-lead-to-appointment-rate) covers the fixes.
3. **Check your service area.** More volume often means more distance. Two crews driving across the county lose install hours to the road.
4. **Check your season.** Florida, the Gulf Coast, and the Carolinas book year round. Most of the rest of the country is strong spring through fall and drops in winter, so plan how crew two stays busy then. Our [winter guide](/guides/keep-floor-coating-crew-busy-in-winter) has options.

## What are the risks of adding a crew too early?

**Idle crews.** The most common mistake is splitting one crew's estimates between two crews. Both are half busy, the new installers get short weeks, and they leave. Our [crew capacity post](/blog/how-many-floor-coating-jobs-can-one-crew-do) shows why marketing has to be sized to the crew.

**Quality slipping.** When you are not on every slab, prep is the first thing to drift, and the warranty calls land on you. Move your best installer up to lead crew two, and give both crews the same written prep checklist. [Hiring and training a floor coating crew](/blog/how-to-hire-and-train-a-floor-coating-crew) covers the training plan.

**Cash.** A second crew means a second rig before it means a second paycheck. Grinder and dust extraction alone run $6,000 to $15,000 when you start out, plus a trailer, tools, and material on hand. Payroll starts on day one. Revenue starts when crew two finishes floors, and the homeowner pays.

## Frequently asked questions

### When should a garage floor coating company add a second crew?

When the install backlog stays long month after month in season, you are turning down good floors in your area, and your lead flow can already produce roughly double the booked estimates one crew needs. One busy month or a spring rush is not enough. Prove the volume by booking the estimates before you hire.

### Should I add a second epoxy crew or an estimator first?

Look at where the wait is. If estimates are booking weeks out but installs are on time, add estimate capacity first, either a crew lead so you can sell full time or an estimator. If installs are backed up and estimates are not, a second epoxy crew is the answer, provided the leads can feed it.

### How many estimates does a second floor coating crew need?

Roughly double what one crew needs. As an example, one crew installing 3 floors a week at a 45% close rate needs about 8 booked estimates with reminders. Two crews at the same rates need about 15, or 18 without reminders. Use your own close rate and floors per week.

### Can show rate really replace hiring a second concrete coating crew?

Sometimes. About 80% of booked estimates show with no reminders and about 95% with confirmation and reminders. If your concrete coating calendar leaks no-shows, fixing it can fill days and delay a hire. If the calendar is clean and the backlog still grows, it is time for crew two.

### What is the biggest risk of adding a second polyaspartic crew?

Hiring before demand is steady. A second polyaspartic crew splitting one crew's estimates means two half busy crews, unhappy installers, and payroll you cannot cover. The second biggest risk is quality slipping when the owner is not on every slab, which a written prep checklist and a proven crew lead address.
