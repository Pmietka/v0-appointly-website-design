# Why Are My Facebook Leads for Epoxy Flooring So Bad?

**Meta Description:** Bad epoxy flooring leads from Facebook usually trace to the native lead form, stock creative, discount hooks, and a wide service area. Here is the fix for each type.

---

Facebook leads for epoxy flooring usually go bad for reasons you control: the native lead form fills itself in, the creative is stock or generic, the ad sells a discount instead of a floor, the service area is drawn too wide, and nobody qualifies the lead on the first call. On Meta the creative is the targeting, so the fastest fix is an ad that shows a real garage floor to a homeowner, a landing page instead of the native form, and a first call that screens out renters, out of area addresses, and kit shoppers before you drive anywhere.

## Key takeaways

- A "bad" epoxy lead is almost always one of six types: renter, out of area, never answers, price shopper, do it yourselfer, or fake contact details.
- On Meta the creative is the targeting: a real coated garage and a homeowner message attract homeowners, and a discount banner attracts bargain hunters.
- The native Meta lead form pre-fills name and phone, so a tap while scrolling counts as a lead. A landing page produces better leads.
- Sort your last 30 leads into types before you change anything, so you fix the biggest leak first.
- The first phone call is the last filter. Confirm ownership, service area, the project, and a time that fits your route before the estimate goes on the calendar.

## What counts as a "bad" epoxy flooring lead?

"Bad leads" is too vague to fix. Owners usually mean one or more of these:

- **Renters.** They like the idea of a coated garage but cannot approve the work.
- **Out of area.** A real homeowner, a real project, and an address you would never drive to.
- **Never answers.** The phone rings out, the voicemail is full, the text gets no reply.
- **Price shoppers.** They want a number per square foot over the phone and nothing else.
- **Do it yourselfers.** They want to know which product you use so they can buy a kit.
- **Fake or wrong contact details.** A dead number, a made up name, or a lead the person does not remember submitting.

Each one has a different cause, so the first step is sorting, not rebuilding the campaign.

## Why does Facebook send epoxy companies so many bad leads?

Facebook does not decide who fills out your form. Your ad and your form do. Five setup choices produce most of the junk.

**The native lead form.** It pre-fills name and phone from the person's profile, so two taps make a lead who may not remember submitting. A landing page adds one step, and that step filters for people who actually want a floor.

**Generic or stock creative.** A stock showroom floor or a warehouse floor does not say "this is for your garage." It says "pretty floor," and everyone taps.

**Broad promises.** "Any floor, any surface, any budget" invites everyone, including renters and people with a basement floor you do not coat.

**Discount hooks.** An ad that leads with a big percentage off or a "special price" attracts people whose main question is price. You will hear that question on every call.

**A service area drawn too wide.** An ad that runs across a whole region and names no towns brings in homeowners you will never drive to.

## How do you find out which bad lead problem you actually have?

Pull your last 30 leads into a sheet. For each one, write down:

1. Where it came from (native form or landing page).
2. Minutes from form submit to first call attempt.
3. Whether they answered.
4. Which of the six types it was, or "good."

Now count. Usually one or two types dominate. Sometimes the finding is different: the leads were not bad, the calls were late. Our [floor coating benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) put the answer rate at about 90% when the call goes out within one minute, and it falls fast after that. If your "never answers" column is the biggest one and your call times are measured in hours, the ads may be fine. Our guide to [why floor coating leads don't answer](/guides/why-floor-coating-leads-dont-answer) walks through that problem.

If you get plenty of clicks and almost no form fills, that is a different problem. See [floor coating ads that get clicks but no leads](/guides/floor-coating-ads-clicks-but-no-leads).

## How does the first call filter out the rest?

Even a good ad lets some wrong people through. A good first call catches them before they cost you a drive. It does three jobs.

**It qualifies.** Four questions, asked naturally:

- Do you own the home?
- What are you looking to have coated? (Garage, basement, patio, or a kit question in disguise.)
- What town is the house in?
- Which of these two times works for someone to come measure?

**It sets a price range.** Some price shoppers just want to know they are not wasting an hour. Our benchmark for garage floor coating is $5 to $8 per square foot, and a two car garage runs roughly 400 to 500 square feet, so you can tell a homeowner roughly where their garage will land before anyone drives out. The homeowner who hangs up was never going to buy. The one who stays is a better estimate.

**It educates.** The do it yourselfer usually does not know what a full grind is, why a hardware store kit peels, or what a polyaspartic topcoat does. Two minutes on that turns some of them into buyers and lets the rest go politely.

When ads are high intent, send traffic to a landing page, and every lead gets called fast, 60 to 70% of floor coating leads should become a booked estimate. If you are far below that after fixing the ad, the call is the next place to look. Our [lead follow up scripts](/blog/floor-coating-lead-follow-up-scripts) give you the wording.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do my epoxy flooring leads from Facebook never answer the phone?

Usually two reasons together: the native Meta lead form lets people submit with two taps while scrolling, and the first call goes out too late. Homeowners are far more likely to pick up in the first minute than an hour later. Moving epoxy flooring ads to a landing page and calling inside a minute fixes most of it.

### Is the Facebook lead form or a landing page better for garage floor coating leads?

A landing page produces better garage floor coating leads than the native Meta lead form. The native form pre-fills contact details, so volume looks good but many people barely remember submitting. A landing page asks a little more, which filters for people who want a coated floor.

### How do I stop getting renters from my concrete coating ads?

Change what the ad shows and says. On Meta the creative is the targeting, so show an attached garage on a house, coated and with a car parked on it, and say "homeowners" in the first line. Then ask "Do you own the home?" on the first call so any renter who still gets through never reaches your calendar.

### How do I filter out price shoppers on epoxy flooring leads?

Stop leading with a discount in the ad, and give a price range on the first call. Garage floor coating sells for $5 to $8 per square foot, and a two car garage is roughly 400 to 500 square feet, so you can tell a homeowner where most garages land before anyone drives out. Homeowners who stay on the call after hearing the range make far better epoxy estimates.
