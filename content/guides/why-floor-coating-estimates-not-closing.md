# Why Am I Not Closing My Garage Floor Coating Estimates?

**Meta Description:** Floor coating estimates fail before you arrive (lead source, no first call, wrong people at the table) or at the table (price first, one option, no ask). Find which.

---

Garage floor coating estimates usually fail for one of two sets of reasons. Either the estimate was lost before you arrived (a weak lead source, no first call that educated the homeowner, the wrong decision makers at the table, an appointment booked too far out), or it was lost at the table (leading with price per square foot, never explaining the system, offering one option, never asking for the job). Track one month of estimates on a single sheet and the pattern will show you which set you have.

## Key takeaways

- Split the problem in two: before the estimate and at the estimate. The fixes are completely different, and fixing the wrong half wastes a season.
- If every lead source closes about the same, the problem is at the table. If one source closes and another does not, the problem starts before you arrive.
- There is no single close rate benchmark for floor coating. It moves with the product and the estimator, so compare against your own history and your own system.
- Shared marketplace leads typically close at 5 to 12%, and estimates contractors book themselves close roughly 40 to 50%, so know which kind you are running before you judge yourself.
- A premium price does not have to mean a low close rate. How the system is explained matters as much as the number on the sheet.

## Is the problem before the estimate or at the estimate?

Most owners assume it is their pitch. Often it is not.

| Where | Problem | What you notice | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Before | Weak lead source | Homeowners have three other quotes and only want a number | Track close rate by source; move budget to the source that closes |
| Before | No first call education | Homeowner does not know what a grind or a topcoat is, and compares you to a kit | Call fast and explain the system and a price range before booking |
| Before | Wrong decision makers | "I need to talk to my husband" or "my wife decides" at the end | Ask on the booking call who else will want to weigh in, and book when both are home |
| Before | Booked too far out | No shows, cold homeowners, "we went with someone else" | Book within a few days when you can; confirm and remind every estimate |
| At | Leading with price per square foot | Homeowner fixes on the number and starts comparing | Inspect and explain first, price last |
| At | No system explanation | "The other guy was cheaper" with no answer | Explain prep, base, flake, topcoat, and warranty in plain words |
| At | One option only | Yes or no, and no is easier | Present good, better, best, starting at the top |
| At | No ask | "Let me know what you think" and then silence for a week | Ask which option fits, then stop talking |

The "at" half is covered step by step in our guide to [closing more floor coating estimates](/blog/how-to-close-more-floor-coating-estimates), and the common objections are in [floor coating sales objections](/blog/floor-coating-sales-objections). This page is about finding which half costs you jobs.

## How do you figure out which one you have?

Track every estimate for one month. If you run fewer than about 20 in a month, keep going until you have 20. One sheet, one row per estimate, filled in the same day:

- Lead source (Meta, Google, referral, marketplace, repeat customer)
- Days from booking to the visit
- Whether the homeowner had a first call that explained the product
- Who was at the table (one decision maker or all of them)
- What you presented (one option or three)
- Whether you asked for the job before you left
- Outcome: closed, open, lost, and the reason they gave
- Ticket if closed

At the end of the month, sort the sheet and look for a split.

**Sort by lead source.** If referrals close and marketplace leads do not, your table skills are fine. The source is the problem. Shared marketplace leads typically close at 5 to 12%, so a bad month on those is not a verdict on you.

**Sort by days out.** If estimates that sat on the calendar for a week or more close worse than ones you ran within a few days, the gap between booking and visit is costing you. Homeowners cool off and keep shopping.

**Sort by who was at the table.** If you close when both partners are home and rarely when one is, fix the booking call, not the presentation.

**Sort by what you presented and whether you asked.** If the one option estimates and the ones where you did not ask make up most of the losses, the problem is at the table, and it is the cheapest one to fix.

If no split shows up and every kind of estimate closes about the same low rate, look hard at the presentation itself. If someone else runs estimates, track by estimator too. Two people on the same leads can close very differently, which our guide on [hiring a floor coating salesperson](/guides/hire-floor-coating-salesperson-estimator) covers.

## What close rate should you compare yourself against?

Our [floor coating benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) do not set a single close rate benchmark, because close rate moves with the product and the estimator. A premium polyurea system sold by a seasoned owner and an entry level epoxy floor sold by a new hire are not the same job.

Compare against three things instead:

1. **Your own history.** Your last 20 or 40 estimates, by lead source.
2. **Your own product.** If you sell a premium system, your rate will look different than a shop selling the cheapest floor in town.
3. **The kind of estimate.** Shared marketplace leads typically close at 5 to 12%. Estimates contractors book themselves close roughly 40 to 50%. If you run the second kind and close like the first, the sheet will show you why.

> **From the field:** Garage Force of the Inland Northwest closes about 55% of its estimates on a premium $5,000 polyurea system. That is not a cheap floor, and it closes because the homeowner understands the system and why it costs what it does. Close rate depends on how the system is sold, not just the price. ## What fixes the "before the estimate" problems?

- **Call fast.** A homeowner reached while still thinking about their garage is easier to book and to close. Our guide on [why floor coating leads don't answer](/guides/why-floor-coating-leads-dont-answer) covers speed and cadence.
- **Educate on the first call.** Explain what a full grind is, why a professional floor outlasts a kit, and what the topcoat does. Give a range: garage floor coating sells for $5 to $8 per square foot, and a two car garage is roughly 400 to 500 square feet. A homeowner who has heard that range and still books is a real buyer.
- **Get the decision makers.** Ask "Is there anyone else who will want to see the options?" and book when they are home.
- **Book close in.** Offer two times in the next few days, not an open calendar weeks out.
- **Confirm and remind.** About 80% of booked estimates show with no reminders, and about 95% show with a confirmation at booking, reminders 24 hours and 1 hour out, and someone following up when the homeowner goes quiet.

## What fixes the "at the estimate" problems?

Run the same process every time: inspect the slab, explain the system, present three options from the top down, give the numbers in person, and ask. ## Frequently asked questions

### Why are my garage floor coating estimates not closing?

Usually one of two sets of reasons. Before the estimate: a weak lead source, no educating first call, the wrong decision makers, or a visit booked too far out. At the estimate: price per square foot first, no system explanation, one option, or no ask. A month of tracking shows which set is yours.

### What is a good close rate for floor coating estimates?

There is no single close rate benchmark for floor coating, because it moves with the product and the estimator. Compare against your own history and your own system. For context, shared marketplace leads typically close at 5 to 12%, and estimates contractors book themselves close roughly 40 to 50%.

### Does a higher price lower my epoxy flooring close rate?

Not by itself. Garage Force of the Inland Northwest closes about 55% on a premium $5,000 polyurea system. When the homeowner understands the prep, the system, and the warranty, a higher priced epoxy or polyaspartic floor can close as well as a cheap one. Leading with price per square foot before explaining anything is what makes price the whole conversation.

### Should I present one price or three options on a concrete coating estimate?

Three. A single option turns the estimate into yes or no, and no is easier to say. Present good, better, best on a concrete coating estimate, starting with the premium system, and ask which fits before you leave.

### How long should I track estimates before deciding what is wrong?

One month, or about 20 estimates if a month gives you fewer. Record lead source, days from booking to visit, who was at the table, what you presented, whether you asked, and the outcome. Sorting by each column shows whether your polyaspartic or epoxy jobs are lost before you arrive or at the table.
