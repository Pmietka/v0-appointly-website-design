# Why Don't My Floor Coating Leads Answer the Phone?

**Meta Description:** Most floor coating leads go silent because the first call came too late. About 90% answer within a minute. Then check the lead form, the ad, and your caller ID.

---

The most common reason floor coating leads do not answer is that the first call came too late: about 90% of homeowners answer when called within one minute of submitting the form, and the answer rate falls fast after that. After speed, the usual causes are low intent leads from the native Meta lead form, an ad that promised something the call does not deliver, an unknown or spam labeled number, bad call times, and no text before the call. Log the time to first call on every lead for two weeks and you will know which one is yours.

## Key takeaways

- About 90% of homeowners answer when called within one minute of submitting the form, and the answer rate drops fast after that.
- Native Meta lead forms fill themselves in with a tap, so many of those homeowners do not remember asking and will not pick up.
- If the ad promised a price or a free floor and the call opens with "we need to come out first," homeowners stop answering.
- An unknown number, or one your carrier labels as spam, gets ignored even by homeowners who want the estimate.
- A text that goes out the second the lead arrives tells the homeowner who is about to call and why.
- Track the minutes from form to first call on every lead. It explains more silent leads than anything else.

## What are the most common reasons floor coating leads go silent?

Here are the causes in the order they usually matter, starting with the one that explains the most.

### 1. The first call came too late

This is the big one. A homeowner fills out your form while they are standing in the garage or sitting on the couch with the phone in their hand. Call in the next minute and they are still holding it. Call an hour later and they are at dinner, at work, or on the phone with another coating company. Our [floor coating benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) put the answer rate at about 90% within one minute, falling fast after that. Our post on [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-wins-floor-coating-jobs) covers why the first contractor to call usually wins the job.

### 2. The lead came from the native Meta lead form

The native lead form inside Facebook and Instagram fills in the name, email, and phone number for the homeowner. One tap and it is sent. That is easy for curious scrollers, which means more of those leads never planned to talk to anyone. A landing page produces better leads than the native form, because the homeowner had to leave the app and choose to fill it out. If you are tired of junk leads in general, our guide on [low quality Facebook leads for epoxy flooring](/guides/low-quality-facebook-leads-epoxy-flooring) goes deeper.

### 3. The ad promised something the call does not deliver

If the ad said "get your price instantly" and your first call says you need to come measure, the homeowner feels baited. If the ad pushed a big discount and the call never mentions it, same result. They answer once, feel misled, and ignore every call after. Your ad, your page, and your first call should all promise the same thing: a free estimate, a clear explanation of the system, and a real range for the job.

### 4. You are calling from an unknown or unlabeled number

Homeowners screen calls. A random number from another area code, or a number their phone flags as possible spam, gets ignored even by someone who wants the estimate. Call and text from one consistent local number. Ask your phone provider how to register it so it shows your business name. Then call your own cell from it and see what shows up.

### 5. You call at times the homeowner cannot answer

Calling every lead at 7 pm because that is when you get off the floor means you reach people during dinner and bedtime routines. The first call should go out right away, whatever time it is. Later attempts should try a different part of the day than the one that failed.

### 6. No text went out first

A text that arrives the moment the lead comes in, with your name, your company, and "I'm going to call you in a minute about your garage floor," turns your unknown number into an expected call. Skip it, and the call looks like spam.

## How do you tell which cause is yours?

Match what you see to the likely cause. This table is the quick version.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Leads answer when you call right away, not when you call later | Time to first call | Call within one minute; have someone cover the phone when you are on a floor |
| Many leads say "I didn't fill anything out" | Native Meta lead form | Send ads to a landing page instead |
| Leads answer once, then go silent after you explain the estimate | Ad promise does not match the call | Make the ad, page, and call say the same thing |
| Almost nobody answers, even fresh leads | Unknown or spam labeled number | One local number, registered with your business name, used for calls and texts |
| Texts get replies but calls do not | Bad call times, or the homeowner prefers text | Let them book by text; try calls at a different time of day |
| Leads answer, then no show at the estimate | Not an answer problem; a confirmation problem | Confirm at booking and remind 24 hours and 1 hour out |

To find out for sure, keep a log for two weeks. For every lead, write down the source (landing page or native form), the time it came in, the time of your first call, whether they answered, and whether they booked. Then sort it. If your answered leads were mostly called within minutes and your silent leads were called hours later, you have your answer, and it is not the leads. If native form leads stay silent even when called fast, it is the form. What changed is that the moment passed, and they are now busy, distracted, or already talking to someone else.

## What call and text cadence should you use?

You do not need a complicated system. You need the first touch to be instant and the rest to be steady, then stop. Our [follow up scripts](/blog/floor-coating-lead-follow-up-scripts) post has the exact words, so this is just the timing.

| When | What to do | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The second the lead arrives | Text: your name, company, and that you will call in a minute | Turns an unknown number into an expected call |
| Within one minute | Call | This is the window where most homeowners answer |
| No answer | Short voicemail, then a text offering two estimate times | Lets them book without picking up |
| Later the same day, different time | Call again | Catches the homeowner at a better moment |
| Day 2 | One call and one text | Gives a busy homeowner one more easy chance |
| Day 3 | Final, friendly text | Leaves the door open without nagging |
| After day 3 | Stop calling; add to a seasonal check in list | More calls now hurt your number's reputation and your brand |

Two rules make this work. Every touch should offer a specific time, not "let me know when works." And whoever runs this cadence needs to be free to call within a minute, which is hard when you are grinding a slab. ## Frequently asked questions

### Why do my garage floor coating leads not answer when I call back?

Usually because the call came too late. About 90% of homeowners answer when called within one minute of submitting the form, and the answer rate falls fast after that. A garage floor coating lead called that evening is often busy or already talking to another contractor.

### Are native Facebook lead forms bad for epoxy flooring leads?

They produce more leads, but lower intent ones. The form fills itself in with a tap, so many epoxy leads do not remember asking for an estimate and will not answer. A landing page produces better leads because the homeowner had to choose to fill it out.

### Should I text or call a new floor coating lead first?

Text first, then call within a minute. The text tells the homeowner who is calling and why, so your floor coating number does not look like spam. If they do not answer, follow with a voicemail and a text that offers two estimate times.

### How many times should I call a concrete coating lead before giving up?

A steady three day cadence is enough: a call within a minute, another call later the same day, one call and text on day 2, and a final text on day 3. After that, move the concrete coating lead to a seasonal check in list instead of calling again.

### Could my phone number be the reason polyaspartic leads don't answer?

Yes. If your number is unknown, from a different area code, or flagged as possible spam, homeowners will ignore it even if they want a polyaspartic floor. Use one local number for calls and texts, register it with your business name, and test it by calling your own cell.
