# How Do I Expand My Floor Coating Business Into a New Town?

**Meta Description:** Pick a town inside a drive you can repeat, install a few floors there first, run separate ads with local photos, and group installs by area before you go wider.

---

Expand into a new town in four steps: pick an area inside a drive your crew can repeat for installs and callbacks, install your first few floors there and photograph them, run ads for that town separately from your home market using those local photos, and group installs by area so the drive does not eat your margin. Treat the new town as a test with its own numbers, not as a bigger circle on your existing ads.

## Key takeaways

- The best next town is one your crew can reach for an install, an estimate, and a warranty callback without losing a day.
- Look at four things before you commit: drive time, how many homeowners live there, how crowded the market is, and whether you can service it after the job.
- Build proof in the new town before you spend real money there: a few installed floors, local photos, and reviews from homeowners in that area.
- Run the new town as its own campaign so you can see its leads, booked estimates, and closes apart from your home market.
- On Meta, the creative is the targeting, so photos of floors installed in the new town beat photos from your home market.
- Schedule installs and estimates by area. A route that crosses the county twice a day costs more than most owners count.

## How do you pick the next town to expand into?

Start with the map, not the ad account. Score each candidate on four questions.

**Can the crew drive it?** Count the whole job: the estimate, the install, the material run, and the callback. If any of those trips turns into a lost half day, the town costs more than it pays.

**Are there enough homeowners with garages?** Floor coating sells to people who own the property. A town of rentals and apartments produces leads that cannot buy.

**How crowded is it?** Several coating companies advertising every day means a higher cost per estimate and pressure on price. Our post on [winning in a saturated floor coating market](/blog/saturated-floor-coating-market) covers what changes when the market is crowded. Our [benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) put garage floor coating at $5 to $8 per square foot, with saturated markets near the bottom and wealthier, less saturated metros near the top, so two towns an hour apart can price very differently.

**Can you service it in a year?** Your warranty does not end at the town line. If you would not drive there for a callback in the middle of a busy week, do not sell floors there. Our [floor coating warranty guide](/blog/floor-coating-warranty-guide) covers what you are promising.

## How do you compare two towns side by side?

| Factor | Good sign | Warning sign | How to check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drive time | Crew can do an install and be home the same day | Every install becomes a long day or an overnight | Drive it at the time your crew would leave |
| Homeowners | Neighborhoods of owner occupied homes with attached garages | Mostly rentals, apartments, or no garages | Public census data, plus a drive through |
| Competition | Few coating companies advertising there | Several companies running ads every week | Search the town name with "garage floor coating" and check the Meta Ad Library |
| Price the market will pay | Similar to or above your home market | Below what you need to cover the drive | Ask suppliers and look at what homeowners say they were quoted |
| Callbacks and warranty | You can get there inside a normal workday | You would put off a callback because of the drive | Be honest with yourself |
| Existing customers | You already have a floor or two there | Nobody in town has heard of you | Check your past jobs list |

## How do you build proof in a town where nobody knows you?

Homeowners in the new town want to see floors in their town, not yours. So the first job is to put a few there.

- **Start with the customers you already have.** If you have done even one floor in the new town, ask that homeowner for a review that mentions the town, and ask if you can photograph the floor again.
- **Book your first floors there on purpose.** When a lead comes in from the new area, give it priority. The first three or four floors are worth more than their ticket because they become your ads.
- **Photograph every one.** Before, during the grind, the flake broadcast, and finished with the garage door open. Get the street view if the homeowner is fine with it.
- **Ask for the review on the spot.** A review that names the town does more for you there than ten reviews from your home market. Our post on [Google reviews for floor coating contractors](/blog/google-reviews-floor-coating-contractors) covers how to ask.
- **Update your Google Business Profile.** Add the new town to your service area so you show up when homeowners there search.

Do not tag home market photos with the new town's name. Homeowners recognize their own neighborhoods.

## Should you run separate ads for the new town?

Yes. Run the new town as its own campaign or ad set, with its own budget and its own creative.

If you just widen the radius on your existing campaign, the new town's leads mix in with your home market, so you cannot tell whether it is working. Meta also tends to spend where results come cheapest, usually the market where you already have proof. Keep the setup simple:

- **Separate targeting.** Draw the new town's area so it does not overlap your home market.
- **Local creative.** Real floors from the new town as soon as you have them. Until then, use your best real floors and say plainly that you now serve the new town.
- **A landing page that names the area.** A landing page produces better leads than the native Meta lead form, and a page that names the town tells the homeowner you actually work there.
- **Track it on its own.** Leads, booked estimates, shows, and closes for the new town, in a separate column from your home market.

Judge it on real numbers: leads with no booked estimates is a different problem from booked estimates that did not close. Our guide to [Meta ads for floor coating contractors](/blog/meta-ads-for-floor-coating-contractors) covers the setup.

> **From the field:** On Meta the creative is the targeting. Across accounts, the ads that pull in a new area are the ones showing floors installed in that area, because a homeowner stops scrolling when the garage looks like the ones on their street. Photos from the home market still work, but the first local floors in a new town usually become the strongest ads that town has.

## How do you schedule installs across two areas?

Group by area. Pick days for the new town and days for your home market, and book both estimates and installs to fit.

- **Zone days.** For example, the new town gets estimates and installs on set days of the week, and the home market gets the rest. Adjust the split as the new town grows.
- **Stack estimates on install days.** If the crew is installing in the new town, run estimates there that afternoon, and offer homeowners two specific times rather than an open calendar.
- **Know your crew's real capacity first.** Adding drive time cuts install days. Our post on [how many floor coating jobs one crew can do](/blog/how-many-floor-coating-jobs-can-one-crew-do) covers the math.

## When should you not expand yet?

Expansion is not always the answer to a slow calendar.

- **Your home market is not full.** If you are not booked in the town you already serve, a new town usually adds drive time before it adds work. Fix lead flow and the first call at home first.
- **Your close rate is low.** More estimates in a new town will not fix an estimate that does not close.
- **The crew is already stretched.** If quality is slipping or callbacks are piling up, a longer drive makes both worse.
- **You are competing for a bigger crowd, not a better one.** If the new town is crowded, read up on [competing with floor coating franchises](/guides/compete-with-floor-coating-franchises) before you commit.

## Frequently asked questions

### How far should a floor coating company travel for a job?

As far as your crew can go for the estimate, the install, and a warranty callback without losing a day. The distance matters less than whether you would actually make the callback trip in a busy week. If you would not, the floor coating job is outside your real service area.

### Should I widen my Facebook ad radius or create a new campaign for a new town?

Create a new campaign or ad set. Widening the radius mixes the new town's leads with your home market, so you cannot see whether it is working, and Meta tends to spend where you already have proof. A separate epoxy flooring campaign with local creative and its own landing page gives the new town a fair test.

### How do I get reviews in a town where I have never installed a garage floor?

Book the first few garage floor coating jobs there on purpose, photograph each one, and ask every homeowner for a Google review that mentions the town before you leave. Add the town to your Google Business Profile service area. Never relabel home market photos as the new town.

### Can I charge the same price for polyaspartic or concrete coating in a new town?

Not always. Garage floor coating generally sells for $5 to $8 per square foot, with saturated markets near $5 and wealthier, less saturated metros near $8. Check what the new town will pay and whether it covers the extra drive before you set a price.
