# I'm Working 70 Hours a Week Running My Floor Coating Business. What Do I Hand Off First?

**Meta Description:** Hand off calling and booking new leads first. About 90% of homeowners answer a call within one minute, and you can't make it from a grinder. Estimates go last.

---

Hand off calling and booking new leads first. It is the most time sensitive job in your week, about 90% of homeowners answer when called within one minute of submitting the form, and you cannot make that call with a grinder in your hands. After that, hand off reminders and admin, and keep the estimate and your install standards until the very end.

## Key takeaways

- The first job to hand off is the one that is both time sensitive and interrupts installs: calling and booking new floor coating leads.
- Reminders and confirmations go second, because booked estimates show at about 80% with no reminders and about 95% with them.
- Admin (invoicing, ordering, install scheduling) goes third. It eats hours but rarely costs you a job if it waits until tonight.
- The estimate is the last thing a coating owner should hand off, and only once a written estimate process exists.
- Never hand off your prep standards, your pricing, or your weekly numbers early.
- A handoff worked when you stop making first calls from a job site, not when you feel less busy.

## Where do 70 hours a week actually go?

Before you hand anything off, write down one normal week in season, job by job. The table below is an example week, not a client's numbers. The columns that matter are how time sensitive each job is, how hard it is to hand off, and who could take it.

| Job | Example hours a week | Time sensitive? | How hard to hand off | Who could do it |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Installing floors | 35 | Yes, on the schedule | Hard. Needs a trained crew lead | Crew lead you trained |
| Running estimates | 10 | Yes, booked times | Hardest. Needs a sales process | You, later an estimator |
| Calling and booking new leads | 8 | Extremely. Minutes matter | Easy to hand off, hard to do well | Setter, office person, or outside booking service |
| Texting confirmations and reminders | 3 | Yes, 24 hours and 1 hour out | Easy | Office person, setter, or automated texts with a person watching replies |
| Install scheduling and customer calls | 4 | Somewhat | Medium | Office person |
| Running ads | 3 | No, weekly | Medium | Agency or a trained office person |
| Invoicing and collecting | 3 | No, end of day is fine | Easy | Bookkeeper or office person |
| Ordering material | 2 | Only when you are about to run out | Easy with a written list | Crew lead or office person |
| Social posts and job photos | 2 | No | Easy | Crew lead takes photos, anyone posts |
| **Total** | **70** | | | |

The install hours are not the problem. That is the work that pays you. The jobs that wreck your week are the small, constant ones: the phone buzzing on a slab, the reminder you forgot, the invoice you write at night.

## What should a floor coating owner hand off first?

Calling and booking. Not because it takes the most hours, but because it is the only job where waiting even an hour changes the outcome.

Our [floor coating benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) put the answer rate at about 90% when a homeowner is called within one minute of submitting the form, and it falls fast after that. When you are the installer, you call at lunch or after the job. By then the homeowner is at dinner, has forgotten the form, or has already talked to someone who called first. The full case for speed is in [speed to lead wins floor coating jobs](/blog/speed-to-lead-wins-floor-coating-jobs).

Calling also breaks the install day. Every time you step off the slab for a lead call, the crew waits and the mix sits.

You have three ways to hand it off: hire a setter, give it to an office person, or use an outside service that books estimates for you. Our guide to [getting floor coating jobs without chasing leads](/guides/floor-coating-leads-without-chasing) compares those three, and [in house appointment setter vs outsourced](/blog/in-house-appointment-setter-vs-outsourced) runs the volume math. Whoever takes it needs to call in minutes, know floor coating well enough to answer basic product questions, and book times that fit your route.

## What comes after calling and booking?

Work down the list in this order. Each step should be running cleanly before you start the next one.

| Order | Job to hand off | Why it goes here | Sign it is working |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Calling and booking new leads | Most time sensitive, interrupts installs | You stop making first calls from a job site |
| 2 | Confirmations and reminders | Protects every estimate you booked | Show rate moves toward 95% |
| 3 | Install scheduling and customer calls | Takes your phone out of your pocket on the slab | Customers get answers without you |
| 4 | Invoicing, collecting, ordering | Hours back at night, low risk | Nothing is late and you did not touch it |
| 5 | Ads and social posts | Weekly, not hourly, so it can wait | Someone reports booked estimates, not clicks |
| 6 | Running estimates | Hardest to replace you | A second person closes near your own rate |

Reminders sit second because about 80% of booked estimates show with no reminders. About 95% show when the estimate is confirmed at booking, reminded 24 hours and 1 hour out, and someone follows up when the homeowner goes quiet. The [no-show post](/blog/floor-coating-estimate-no-shows) has the reminder sequence.

Admin comes before ads because it is easier to check. When you do hand off ads, judge them only on booked estimates, not on clicks or leads.

## Why should estimates be the last thing you hand off?

Because the estimate is where your money is made, and nobody sells your floor the way you do yet. Owners who hand off estimates too early hand off a feeling, not a process. The new person improvises, the close rate drops, and the estimates come back to the owner.

Hand off estimates only when three things are true: you have a written estimate process you run the same way every time, you track your own close rate and average ticket, and you have enough estimates to keep a second person busy. Our guide on [hiring a floor coating salesperson or estimator](/guides/hire-floor-coating-salesperson-estimator) covers that decision.

> **From the field:** Across the floor coating owners we work with, the ones who grow fastest are the ones who only have to show up and run the estimate. The calling, the qualifying, and the booking happen without them, so their day is installs and estimates and nothing else. The owners who stay stuck are usually the ones still returning lead calls from the truck.

## What should you never hand off early?

Keep these until the company is much bigger:

- **Your prep standards.** The crew lead follows the checklist, but you own it, because coatings fail at prep. Our post on [hiring and training a floor coating crew](/blog/how-to-hire-and-train-a-floor-coating-crew) covers passing it down.
- **Your prices.** Someone else can send the quote. You decide what the systems cost.
- **Your weekly numbers.** If you stop looking, you will not notice when a handoff breaks. The [weekly KPI scoreboard](/blog/floor-coating-contractor-kpis) is the list to watch.
- **The review ask.** Ask while you are standing in the finished garage.

## How do you know the handoff actually worked?

Check two things after each handoff. Did the hours come back? If you handed off calling but still call back every lead yourself, you did not hand it off. Did the numbers hold? Leads should still become booked estimates at 60 to 70% when they come from a landing page and are called fast, and show rate should move toward 95%. If a number dropped, fix that handoff before starting the next.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should a floor coating owner delegate first?

Calling and booking new leads. It is the job where minutes matter most, since about 90% of homeowners answer when called within one minute, and it constantly pulls a garage floor coating owner off the slab. Hours spent on invoicing can wait until tonight. A lead call cannot.

### Should I hire an office person or a crew lead first?

It depends on where your hours go. If you keep stepping off installs to take calls and send reminders, an office person or a setter frees you faster. If you want to move into estimates full time, a trained crew lead comes first.

### When should an epoxy flooring owner stop installing?

When you have a crew lead who follows your prep checklist without you, and enough booked estimates to fill your days with selling. Many epoxy owners step off the crew in stages, a few days a week at first.

### Can I hand off running my concrete coating estimates?

Yes, but last. Hand off estimates only when you have a written process, you know your own close rate and average ticket, and there are enough estimates to keep a second person busy. Otherwise your concrete coating close rate usually drops and the estimates come back to you.
