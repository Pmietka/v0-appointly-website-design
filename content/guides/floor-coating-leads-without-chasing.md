# How Do I Get Floor Coating Jobs Without Chasing Leads All Day?

**Meta Description:** Chasing comes from slow calls, low intent sources, and native lead forms. Fix the source, hire a setter, or buy booked estimates. Someone still calls in 1 minute.

---

You stop chasing floor coating leads by fixing why they go cold: a first call that comes too late, a lead source with low intent, or a native lead form the homeowner barely remembers filling out. There are three ways out: fix the source and call within one minute yourself, hire a setter to make that call, or buy booked appointments so someone else does it. No option removes the fast call. About 90% of homeowners answer when called within a minute, so the only real question is who makes that call.

## Key takeaways

- Chasing is a symptom. The causes are slow first contact, low intent lead sources, and native lead forms.
- About 90% of homeowners answer when called within one minute of submitting the form, and the answer rate falls fast after that.
- A landing page produces better floor coating leads than the native Meta lead form, and better leads need less chasing.
- The three ways to stop chasing are: fix the source, hire a setter, or buy booked appointments. Each moves the calling to a different person.
- Whatever you choose, keep closing and follow up on unsold estimates yourself. Those are not chasing. That is selling.

## Why do floor coating leads need so much chasing?

Chasing means calling, texting, and calling again, for days, to reach a homeowner who once raised a hand. It almost always traces back to one of three causes.

**The first call came too late.** A homeowner fills out the form while looking at their garage. If the call goes out within a minute, they are still holding the phone. Our [floor coating benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) show about 90% answer at that speed, and the answer rate falls fast after that. Call back that evening and you are now the one doing the chasing. The full case is in [speed to lead wins floor coating jobs](/blog/speed-to-lead-wins-floor-coating-jobs).

**The source had low intent.** Shared marketplace leads go to several contractors at once, and they typically close at 5 to 12%. You are chasing a homeowner who is also dodging three other calls. Ads that sell a cheap price attract people who want a cheap price. On Meta, the creative is the targeting, so the ad decides who fills out the form.

**The form was too easy.** The native Meta lead form fills in a name and phone number with one tap. Some of those homeowners do not remember submitting anything when you call. A landing page asks for a little more effort, and it produces better leads.

| What you hear when you call | Likely cause | First fix |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No answer, then no callback | Call went out too late | Call within one minute, every time, including evenings and weekends |
| "I didn't fill anything out" | Native lead form, one tap submit | Send ad traffic to a landing page |
| "I already have three quotes" | Shared lead source | Move budget to an exclusive source |
| "Just text me a price" | Creative selling price, not the result | Show finished local floors, not discounts |
| Booked, then a no-show | No confirmation or reminders | Confirm at booking, remind 24 hours and 1 hour out |

## What are the three ways to stop chasing?

### 1. Fix the source and keep calling yourself

Run your own Meta ads to a landing page, refresh the creative often, show real finished floors, and call every lead within a minute. When all of that is in place, the benchmark is 60 to 70% of leads becoming a booked estimate. At that rate you are not chasing. You are booking.

The catch is the minute. You are grinding a slab at 2pm and a lead comes in. You are at dinner at 7pm and a lead comes in. Most owner operators cannot hit the one minute window on install days, and that is when the chase starts again. Use tested [follow-up scripts](/blog/floor-coating-lead-follow-up-scripts) for the leads you do miss.

**Fits:** owners with a small lead volume, a predictable schedule, and someone who can pick up the phone fast when they cannot.

### 2. Hire a setter

Put someone else on the phone. An in-house setter or an office person can make the first call, educate the homeowner, qualify them, and book the estimate. You still own the ads and the lead source, so a bad source is still a bad source.

The hard parts are coverage and product knowledge. Homeowners fill out forms at night and on weekends, and a setter who cannot explain prep, cure time, or epoxy versus polyaspartic just confirms a time. The math on cost and volume is in [in house appointment setter vs outsourced](/blog/in-house-appointment-setter-vs-outsourced).

**Fits:** owners with steady lead volume, a good source already working, and the time to train and manage a person.

### 3. Buy booked appointments

Pay a service to run the ads, call every lead fast, qualify, and put a confirmed estimate on your calendar. You pay per booked estimate, not per lead, so the leads that never book are the service's cost, not yours. You show up and run the estimate.

The tradeoff is control and cost per appointment. You do not run the ads. You pay a set price for every booked estimate, so your close rate decides whether the math works. And if the service only works with one contractor per market, your market may already be taken.

**Fits:** owners who want to spend their day installing and selling, not dialing, and who close well enough to make a per appointment price pay off.

## How do the three options compare?

Your hours depend on your lead volume. Track your own for two weeks before you decide.

| | Fix the source, call yourself | Hire a setter | Buy booked appointments |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who makes the first call | You | Your setter | The service |
| Owner hours per week on leads | Most: every call, text, and follow up | Some: managing, training, and reviewing the setter | Least: running estimates and closing |
| Cost structure | Ad spend plus your time | Ad spend plus payroll | A set fee per booked estimate |
| Who pays for leads that never book | You | You | The service |
| Coverage on nights and weekends | Only if you answer | Only if you staff it | Built in, if the service does it |
| Biggest risk | Missing the one minute window on install days | A setter who confirms times but cannot sell the product | A close rate too low for the per appointment price |

When the person on that first call educates the homeowner on the product, the estimate stops being a pitch, and the contractor walks in to a homeowner who already wants the floor.

## What should you never hand off?

Stopping the chase does not mean stopping the selling. Keep these yourself:

- **The estimate and the close.** Nobody sells your floor like you or your best estimator.
- **Follow up on unsold estimates.** A homeowner who saw you, liked you, and did not sign yet is not a cold lead. Our post on [following up unsold floor coating estimates](/blog/follow-up-unsold-floor-coating-estimates) covers what to send and when.
- **Reviews.** Ask while you are still standing in the garage.

## How do you know the chasing has stopped?

Check three numbers every week. Your leads should become booked estimates at 60 to 70%. Booked estimates should show at about 95% with reminders. And you should not be making first calls from a job site. If the first two are below benchmark, fix the source or the speed. If the third is still true, the calling is in the wrong hands.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why do my floor coating leads never answer the phone?

Usually because the first call went out too late. About 90% of homeowners answer when called within one minute of submitting the form, and the answer rate falls fast after that. Native Meta lead forms make it worse, because some homeowners submit with one tap and forget. Call within a minute and send ad traffic to a landing page.

### Can I stop chasing epoxy leads without hiring anyone?

Yes, if you can call every lead within a minute and your source is good. Send Meta traffic to a landing page, use creative that shows real local epoxy and flake floors, and set up reminders. The weak spot is install days, when most owners cannot answer fast. That is when an epoxy contractor without help starts chasing again.

### Is buying booked appointments better than hiring a setter?

It depends on volume and your close rate. A setter makes sense when you have steady garage floor coating leads and time to manage someone. Booked appointments make sense when you would rather not run ads or manage a caller and you close well enough to make a per appointment price pay off. In both cases, someone has to call within a minute.
