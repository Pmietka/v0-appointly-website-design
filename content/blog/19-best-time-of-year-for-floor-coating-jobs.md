# The Best Time of Year to Book Floor Coating Jobs (and How to Stay Booked Year-Round)

**Meta Description:** Spring through fall is peak season for floor coating jobs. See the seasonal demand curve, why the South books year round, and how to stay booked in winter.

---

Spring through fall is the best time of year for floor coating jobs in most of the country. Demand surges from March through May, holds through summer, and gets a second push in September and October before winter cuts it off. Florida, the Gulf Coast, and the Carolinas book year round. Everywhere else, winter work depends on whether you can coat in cold conditions.

## Key takeaways

- Spring (March through May) is the busiest stretch of the year for residential garage floors, and fall brings a second, smaller surge before winter.
- Florida, the Gulf Coast, and the Carolinas book coating jobs year round; every other region drops in winter unless the crew can coat in cold conditions.
- Standard epoxy needs a slab and air temperature of roughly 50 to 90 degrees, while many polyaspartic systems cure near freezing and extend the season by weeks on each end.
- Keep booking estimates during peak season, because the backlog you build in August is what fills December.
- Fill winter with heated garages, basements, and commercial floors, where slab temperature is not a problem.

## When is demand for floor coating highest?

Demand peaks in spring and stays strong through summer, with a second, smaller surge in fall. Coating demand follows a predictable rhythm driven by homeowners and weather, and in most of the country it looks like this.

**Spring (March through May): the surge.** This is your busiest stretch. The garage got cleaned out, the snow is gone, and the homeowner who stared at a cracked, oil-stained slab all winter is finally ready to do something about it. Garage floors, basements, and patios all spike here. If you only set up your booking system in March, you are already behind the contractors who set up in January.

**Summer (June through August): sustained high demand.** Cure conditions are ideal, homeowners are doing home projects, and patio and pool-deck coatings come into play. By mid-summer you may be booked far enough out that impatient homeowners go to whoever can start sooner.

**Fall (September through November): the pre-winter push.** Homeowners want the garage done before they park the car inside for winter. "Before it gets cold" and "before the holidays" are real motivators, and this is the last easy residential demand before the slow season.

**Winter (December through February): the slowdown.** Residential demand drops, cold slabs make exterior work impractical in many regions, and homeowners stop thinking about garage floors. This is where most contractors go quiet, and where the prepared ones stay booked.

Region changes the curve. Our [floor coating benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) show that Florida, the Gulf Coast, and the Carolinas book year round. Everywhere else is strong spring through fall and drops in winter unless the contractor can coat in cold conditions.

## Why does temperature control your calendar?

Coating is seasonal because of chemistry. Your product cannot cure properly outside a temperature and humidity window, which is not true of most trades.

- **Standard epoxy** typically wants a slab and ambient temperature in the 50 to 90 degree range, with the concrete at least 5 degrees above the dew point. Pour it on a 40 degree slab and it gets thick, won't self-level, and can cure soft or hazy.
- **Cold slabs kill cure times.** Even when the air feels fine, the concrete holds cold. A garage slab in February can sit 15 degrees below the air temperature, which wrecks the cure even when your thermometer says you are fine.
- **Humidity and dew point** cause blushing, fish-eyes, and adhesion failures. High humidity is a summer problem. Condensation on a cold slab is a winter problem.

This is why exterior work grinds to a halt in cold climates. You cannot reliably coat an unheated garage or an outdoor patio when the slab is near freezing.

## Polyaspartic widens your winter window

Chemistry choice is the lever most contractors underuse. [Polyaspartic and polyurea coatings](/blog/polyaspartic-vs-epoxy-garage-floors) cure across a much wider temperature range than standard epoxy. Many polyaspartic systems cure in temperatures down near freezing, and some formulations are rated lower. They also cure fast, often returning a floor to foot traffic the same day and vehicle traffic in 24 hours.

For your business that means two things:

1. **You can keep installing later into the fall and earlier in the spring** than an epoxy-only contractor.
2. **You can take cold-weather jobs** an epoxy contractor has to turn down, as long as the slab is prepped and within the product's rated range.

If your winters are dead and you only run epoxy systems, adding a polyaspartic or polyurea option is the simplest way to extend your installable season by weeks on each end.

## Where does winter work come from?

The slow season is not empty. The demand shifts indoors. Contractors who stay booked in January are usually working these jobs:

- **Heated garages.** Plenty of homeowners run a heated, insulated garage or shop. The slab stays warm enough to coat year round, and these customers exist in every market.
- **Commercial and industrial floors.** Warehouses, showrooms, auto shops, breweries, and retail spaces are climate controlled, so slab temperature is not an issue. Commercial buyers also prefer winter installs because their slow season is your slow season.
- **Basements.** Conditioned basement floors hold temperature and are ideal winter work, especially while homeowners are stuck inside finishing them.
- **Interior repairs and recoats.** Flake recoats, chip repairs, and top-coat refreshes on existing interior floors keep crews moving.

A contractor who markets to commercial buyers and heated-space owners in November does not have a dead winter. They have a different mix of jobs. Our [winter marketing plan for floor coating contractors](/blog/winter-marketing-plan-floor-coating-contractors) lays out that mix month by month.

## The dry spell starts when you stop booking in spring

Almost every contractor falls into the same trap: they stop generating booked estimates the moment they get busy. In April you are slammed, so you quit chasing new work. Then July ends, the backlog runs out, and there is nothing in the pipeline because you spent three months not feeding it. The fall and winter dry spell is usually not a demand problem. It is a pipeline problem created in spring.

Booked estimates have a lead time. A homeowner who books an estimate today might not sign for a week and might not want the install for a month. The work you book in late summer and early fall is what carries you into winter. **If you turn off lead generation during peak season, you are guaranteeing the slow season will hurt.**

The fix is to keep the machine running all year. When you are slammed, book the estimates further out instead of stopping the flow. A full backlog in August is what a busy December looks like.

## How to plan crew capacity around the curve

Once you understand the curve, you can staff to it instead of getting whipped around by it.

**Build backlog ahead of the surge.** Going into spring, you want a wall of booked estimates already scheduled so your crews hit the ground running in March. Our [spring marketing plan](/blog/spring-marketing-plan-floor-coating-contractors) covers the timing.

**Don't overhire for the peak.** Staffing up hard for the spring rush and then carrying that payroll through a dead winter is a common mistake. Run a lean core crew you can keep busy 12 months and lean on the wider polyaspartic window plus commercial work to fill the shoulders. Our guide to [how many jobs one crew can do in a month](/blog/how-many-floor-coating-jobs-can-one-crew-do) shows how to size the pipeline to the crew.

**Use the slow months for what you can't do when slammed.** Equipment maintenance, training, sample boards, photographing finished jobs, and following up with past customers about additional spaces all happen better in January than July.

**Schedule by chemistry.** Hold weather-sensitive epoxy exterior jobs for the warm window, and route cold-weather and commercial work to your polyaspartic systems. That alone smooths the calendar.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best month to start marketing for floor coating jobs?

January is the best month to start marketing for floor coating jobs in most regions. Homeowner demand surges in March, and a booked estimate takes a week or more to become a signed job and often a month to become an install. Contractors who start ads and follow-up in January enter spring with a backlog. Contractors who start in March spend the surge catching up.

### Can you coat a garage floor in winter?

Yes, you can coat a garage floor in winter if the slab is warm enough for the product you are using. Standard epoxy generally needs the concrete in the 50 to 90 degree range and above the dew point, which rules out most unheated garages in cold climates. Many polyaspartic and polyurea systems cure near freezing, so heated garages, basements, and commercial interiors stay coatable all winter.

### Why does floor coating business drop off in the fall and winter?

Floor coating business drops in fall and winter for two reasons. Cold slabs push standard epoxy outside its cure window, so exterior and unheated garage work stops in most regions. Homeowners also stop thinking about garage floors once the car is parked inside for the season. Contractors who kept booking estimates through summer feel a slower season. Contractors who stopped in spring feel a dead one.

### Do floor coating contractors in Florida have a slow season?

Floor coating contractors in Florida, on the Gulf Coast, and in the Carolinas book jobs year round. Slabs in those markets rarely drop below the cure window, so garage floors, patios, and pool decks stay installable in January. Demand still moves a little around holidays and the hottest weeks of summer, but the deep winter drop that contractors in the Midwest and Northeast plan around does not happen there.

### Should I hire more crew for the spring rush?

Hire for the spring rush only if you have winter work lined up to keep the extra crew busy. Most coating contractors do better running a lean core crew all 12 months, booking spring estimates further out instead of adding payroll, and using polyaspartic systems plus commercial and basement work to fill the shoulder months. Add a crew when backlog stays past three weeks for several months.
