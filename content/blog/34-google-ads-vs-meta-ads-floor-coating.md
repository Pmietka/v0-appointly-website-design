# Google Ads vs Meta Ads for Floor Coating Contractors: Which Fills the Calendar?

**Meta Description:** Google Search captures homeowners already looking for a coating. Meta creates demand with before-and-afters. How each fills a floor coating calendar.

---

Google Search ads capture homeowners who already want a coating, so they convert at higher intent but are limited by how many people search each month. Meta ads create demand by putting a before-and-after in front of homeowners who were not searching, so they produce more volume but need faster follow-up. Most floor coating contractors get the fullest calendar running both, with one calling process behind them.

## Key takeaways

- Google Search ads capture existing demand: the homeowner typed "garage floor coating near me" and is ready to compare quotes today.
- Meta ads create demand: on Meta the creative is the targeting, and a good before-and-after reaches homeowners who never searched.
- Meta volume is capped by your budget; Google volume is capped by how many people in your market search each month, which is a small number in small metros.
- Both platforms need a call within one minute, because the answer rate is 90% at one minute and Meta leads cool faster than Google leads.
- Run Google for the searchers, Meta for volume, and one calling process behind both, then judge each by booked estimates rather than lead count.

## What is the difference between Google Ads and Meta Ads for a floor coating contractor?

The difference is who sees the ad and why. Google Search shows your ad to a homeowner who typed a query about floor coating. Meta shows your ad to a homeowner who was scrolling and matches the kind of person your creative pulls in. One captures demand that already exists. The other creates it.

That single difference explains almost everything else: intent, volume, creative burden, how costs behave when a market fills up, how each responds to the seasons, and what kind of follow-up each lead needs. The table below lays it out, and the sections after it go through each row.

## How intent differs between Google and Meta leads

A Google lead has higher intent because the homeowner started the conversation. Someone who searched "epoxy garage floor cost" or "polyaspartic coating near me" has a floor, a problem, and a plan. They also, almost always, clicked two or three other ads. You are one of the quotes.

A Meta lead has lower intent at the moment of the click and higher intent a minute later if you call. The homeowner did not wake up planning to coat the garage. They saw a flake floor with a glossy topcoat, remembered their own stained slab, and tapped. That interest is real but it is fresh, and it fades fast if nobody calls. This is why speed to lead matters more on Meta than anywhere else.

Neither intent is better. A Google lead is shopping and needs a reason to pick you. A Meta lead is curious and needs a fast, friendly call that turns curiosity into an estimate on the calendar.

## How volume differs between the two platforms

Meta produces more volume than Google in almost every floor coating market, because Meta is limited by your budget and Google is limited by how many people search. In a metro of a few hundred thousand people, the number of homeowners searching for a garage floor coating in a given week is small. Once you have captured them, more Google budget buys nothing.

Meta has no such ceiling in practice. The audience is every homeowner in your service area, and the creative decides which of them stop scrolling. More budget means more homeowners see the before-and-after, and more of them fill out the form. The [Meta ads guide for floor coating contractors](/blog/meta-ads-for-floor-coating-contractors) covers the campaign structure that turns that reach into leads.

The practical result: Google fills a few slots a week. Meta fills the calendar. If your problem is "I need more estimates," Google alone rarely solves it.

## How much creative work each platform needs

Google needs little creative and Meta needs a steady supply of it. A Google Search ad is a few headlines and descriptions plus a landing page. Write it once, test a few variations, and it runs for months.

Meta is the opposite. On Meta the creative is the targeting, which is one of our [published benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks). The photo or video decides who stops, and a floor coating ad that shows a real garage, a real flake broadcast, and a real finished floor pulls homeowners. Stock photos pull renters and tire kickers. Ads also wear out, so you need new before-and-afters every few weeks.

If you hate shooting video on the job site, Meta will be harder for you. If your crew already films every grind and every pour, you have the raw material most contractors lack.

## How costs behave in a saturated market

In a saturated market, Google costs rise faster than Meta costs, because Google is an auction on a fixed number of searches. When five more coating contractors open in your metro, all of them bid on the same "garage floor coating" queries. The searches do not multiply. The bids do.

Meta costs rise too when a market fills up, but more slowly, because a better ad can still reach homeowners the other contractors' ads bored. The contractor with the strongest creative keeps finding new people while the others fight over the same feed. That does not make Meta immune; it makes creative the lever. Our post on [running ads in a saturated floor coating market](/blog/saturated-floor-coating-market) goes deeper.

Judge both platforms on one number instead: what a booked estimate costs you, and what it turns into. ## How each platform responds to the seasons

Google follows the season and Meta can be run against it. In a northern market, searches for garage floor coating drop in December and climb in March. Google spend in January mostly buys nothing, because the searches are not there.

Meta reaches homeowners whether or not they are searching, which means you can put a "book now, install in April" ad in front of them in February and fill the spring calendar before the first warm day. In Florida, the Gulf Coast, and the Carolinas, both platforms run year round.

## How each platform handles a small market

Meta works in small markets and Google often does not. A town of 40,000 with a rural service area might produce a handful of floor coating searches a month, not enough to spend any real budget on Google. The same town has thousands of homeowners with a garage, and a Meta ad can reach every one of them.

The trade-off is that a small Meta audience wears out faster, so creative has to rotate more often, and the service radius may need to be wider. The contractors who do well in small markets treat Meta as the main engine and use Google as a small net for the few people who search.

## What follow-up each platform needs

Both platforms need a call within one minute, and Meta needs it more. Our [phone answer rate benchmark](/floor-coating-benchmarks) is 90% when the lead is called within one minute of submitting the form. On Meta, a landing page beats the native lead form on lead quality, and with speed to lead under a minute 60 to 70% of leads should become a booked estimate.

A Google lead is already comparing, so the first call needs to book the estimate before the other two contractors call back. A Meta lead is fresh, so the first call needs to happen while the homeowner still has the ad in mind. Either way, the follow-up is the same process: call within a minute, text if no answer, try again, and book on the calendar, not "we'll call you back."

The mistake contractors make is running two platforms with two follow-up habits. Google leads get called because "those are the good ones." Meta leads sit because "they're just curious." Then Meta looks like it does not work, when the calling did not work.

## When to use Google, when to use Meta, and how to run both

Use Google to catch the homeowners already searching, use Meta to fill the calendar, and put one calling process behind both. Here is how that breaks down.

1. **Start with Meta if you need volume.** If the calendar has gaps, Meta is the platform that closes them, because it is not capped by search volume.
2. **Add Google to stop losing the searchers.** A small Search campaign on a tight set of coating keywords in your service area catches high-intent homeowners who would otherwise call a competitor.
3. **Send both to the same landing page and the same calling process.** One form, one phone number, one rule: called within a minute, booked on the calendar.
4. **Measure both on booked estimates.** Not clicks, not leads. Which platform put more estimates on the calendar this month, and what did those estimates close into?
5. **Shift budget with the season.** In cold markets, lean on Meta in late winter to pre-book spring and let Google grow as searches return.

## Frequently asked questions

### Are Google Ads or Meta Ads better for a floor coating contractor?

Neither platform is better on its own for a floor coating contractor. Google Search captures homeowners who are already searching for a coating, so it converts at higher intent but runs out of volume in most markets. Meta creates demand with before-and-after creative and produces far more volume. The fullest calendars come from running both with one fast calling process behind them.

### Why do Meta leads for floor coating need faster follow-up than Google leads?

Meta leads need faster follow-up because the homeowner was not searching. They saw a finished garage floor, got interested, and filled out a form on impulse. That interest fades within minutes if nobody calls. Called within one minute, about 90% of leads answer. A Google lead was already comparing quotes, so it holds its intent longer, though the first caller still usually wins.

### Do Google Ads work for floor coating in a small market?

Google Ads often struggle for floor coating in a small market because there are not enough monthly searches to spend a meaningful budget. A town with a few searches a week cannot fill a crew's calendar through Search alone. Meta works better in small markets because it reaches every homeowner in the area, not only the ones who happen to be searching that week.

### Should a floor coating contractor use a landing page or a lead form on Meta?

A floor coating contractor should send Meta traffic to a landing page rather than the native lead form when lead quality matters more than lead count. A landing page beats the native lead form on lead quality because the homeowner takes an extra step to reach it. Pair the landing page with a call within one minute and the extra step costs you very little volume.
