# Speed to Lead: Why the First Floor Coating Contractor to Call Wins the Job

**Meta Description:** Homeowners book whoever calls back first. What slow follow-up costs floor coating contractors, and how instant contact wins the jobs you paid to generate.

---

The first floor coating contractor to call a homeowner usually wins the job, because the homeowner submits three or four forms at once and books the first estimate offered. Call within one minute and about 90% of homeowners answer. Wait an hour and most have already scheduled with someone else. Speed to lead is the single biggest lever on how many of your paid leads become booked estimates.

## Key takeaways

- About 90% of homeowners answer when the call comes within one minute of the form submission, and the answer rate falls every minute after that.
- With fast contact, 60 to 70% of leads should become a booked estimate; slow follow-up drops that share because the homeowner books whoever called first.
- Show rate moves with speed too: a homeowner booked while intent is high, then reminded 24 hours and one hour out, shows about 95% of the time.
- The goal of the first call is a specific day and time on the calendar, not a friendly chat or a phone quote.
- A contractor on a job site can't call within a minute, so someone whose whole job is answering leads has to run the front end.

## Why does the first call win the job?

When a homeowner submits a form, intent is at its peak. They're sitting there with the phone in hand, thinking about the garage floor, and they want to talk to someone now. And because that's how people shop, they've also filled out two or three more forms on the next contractors that came up. By evening, four coating contractors have the same name and number.

The first contractor to call gets a warm homeowner who is happy to hear from them. That contractor:

- Answers questions while the homeowner is still excited
- Builds rapport before anyone else has dialed
- Books the in-person estimate on the spot
- Becomes the contractor the homeowner mentally chose before the others call

By the time contractor two calls, the homeowner already has an estimate scheduled, so that call is an interruption. Contractors three and four get voicemail or a curt "we already have someone coming out."

The first caller isn't better. The first caller got there while the door was still open.

## What do the numbers say about response time?

Our [benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) put the phone answer rate at about 90% when the lead is called within one minute of submitting the form. From there the drop-off is steep and it happens fast.

| Response time | What happens |
|---|---|
| Within 1 minute | About 90% answer. The homeowner is still holding the phone they used to submit the form. |
| 5 to 30 minutes | Answer rate falls. Some have started dinner, some have already talked to a competitor. |
| 1 hour or more | Most have moved on or booked an estimate with whoever called first. |
| Next day | The lead is usually gone, or won't answer a number they don't recognize. |

Those answer rates flow straight into booking rate. With contact inside a minute, 60 to 70% of leads should become a booked estimate, which is the [lead to appointment rate](/blog/floor-coating-lead-to-appointment-rate) a coating contractor should hold. Reach half your leads because you called late and that share drops with it.

Speed moves show rate too. A homeowner booked while intent is high, then confirmed at booking and reminded 24 hours and one hour out, shows about 95% of the time. Book them two days later, after they've cooled off, and you're the appointment they're most likely to skip.

## Why do busy coating contractors lose the jobs they paid for?

The contractors who most need new work, the ones grinding slabs and pulling squeegees all day, are the worst positioned to win on speed.

You're on a job site at 10 a.m. with a respirator on, a grinder running, and your phone in the truck across the driveway. A lead comes in. You see it at lunch, and you don't get a quiet minute to call until cleanup at 5. By then the homeowner submitted the form six hours ago and three other coaters already called.

You did everything right. You paid for the lead through [Meta ads](/blog/meta-ads-for-floor-coating-contractors), you do excellent work, your reviews are strong. You still lost the job, because you were doing the job you already had.

This is the trap:

- **You can't run an estimate and answer the phone at the same time.** The work that pays today blocks the work that pays next month.
- **Voicemail and "I'll call them tonight" are too late.** Tonight is hours behind the first caller.
- **An office person is expensive** and still can't cover nights and weekends, which is exactly when homeowners fill out forms.

So the leads you paid for quietly leak out the bottom of the bucket. Not because they were bad leads. Because nobody got to them in time.

## Two contractors, one lead: what four minutes is worth

Say a homeowner named Dave fills out a form Saturday at 2 p.m. wanting his two-car garage coated. The same lead goes to two contractors.

**Contractor A** is on a job site. He sees the notification at 6 p.m., decides not to bug anyone on a weekend, and calls Monday at 9 a.m. Dave is at work and doesn't pick up. Contractor A leaves a voicemail. Dave never calls back, because by Sunday he'd already booked an estimate with someone else.

**Contractor B** has a system that contacts every new lead instantly. At 2:04 p.m. Saturday, Dave gets a call while he's still at the kitchen table looking at coating photos. They talk for four minutes about flake colors and what a polyaspartic floor runs. Contractor B books an estimate for Tuesday evening and texts a confirmation. Tuesday, Contractor B measures the garage, shows samples, and writes a $4,200 job. Dave signs.

Same homeowner. Same intent. Same job. Contractor A blamed bad leads. Contractor B was four minutes faster, and those four minutes were worth $4,200. Run that across a month of leads and the gap is the difference between a full calendar and a slow season.

## Why does instant contact plus booking beat better follow-up?

Many contractors think the answer is to follow up better: set reminders, build a call list, try each lead three or four times over the week. That helps a little, but follow-up that starts hours later is already racing from behind.

What wins is two things working together:

1. **Instant contact.** The lead gets a call the moment they raise their hand, while intent is at its peak and before any competitor has dialed.
2. **A booked time slot.** The first call isn't a friendly chat. It ends with an in-person estimate locked to a specific day and time. A booked appointment is a commitment; "we'll be in touch" is a coin flip.

Combine speed with a booked slot and you take the homeowner off the market before your competitors know the lead existed. The [follow-up scripts](/blog/floor-coating-lead-follow-up-scripts) post has the exact words for the first text, the first call, and the three days after. Then run the confirmation sequence so the booked estimate actually happens; [no-shows](/blog/floor-coating-estimate-no-shows) drop from about 20% to about 5% with it.

## Why is this nearly impossible to do alone?

No working contractor can do this himself. You can't grind a floor with a respirator on and also call a fresh lead within a minute, on a Saturday, at 8 p.m., or from a ladder. Speed to lead needs someone whose entire job is to answer every lead the instant it arrives, every day, including the nights and weekends when homeowners actually fill out forms.

That isn't something you squeeze in between estimates. It's a separate function that has to run while you work. Whether that's a hire or a service is the question in [in-house appointment setter versus outsourced](/blog/in-house-appointment-setter-vs-outsourced), but either way the phone can't be in the truck.

## Frequently asked questions

### How fast should a floor coating contractor call a new lead?

Call within one minute of the form submission. At that speed about 90% of homeowners answer, because they're still holding the phone they used to send the request. The answer rate falls with every minute after that, and by the next day most leads have booked an estimate with a competitor or stopped answering numbers they don't recognize.

### What percentage of floor coating leads should become booked estimates?

With contact inside a minute and a call that ends in a specific time slot, 60 to 70% of floor coating leads should become a booked estimate. If your booking rate sits well under that, the usual cause is speed: leads called an hour or a day later never get reached, and the ones you do reach have already scheduled elsewhere.

### Does speed to lead affect show rate on floor coating estimates?

Yes. A homeowner booked in the first minutes, while they're still excited about the garage floor, is more committed than one booked days later after cooling off. Pair fast booking with a confirmation text, a reminder 24 hours out, and a reminder one hour out, and show rate runs about 95% instead of about 80% with no system.

### Should a floor coating contractor call leads on nights and weekends?

Yes, because that's when homeowners fill out forms. A Saturday afternoon or 8 p.m. lead is at peak intent right then, and waiting until Monday morning hands the job to whoever called over the weekend. If you can't cover those hours yourself, that's the strongest case for a setter or a service that answers every lead within a minute.
