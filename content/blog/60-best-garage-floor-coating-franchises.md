## Is a manufacturer dealer program the same as a franchise?

No, and the difference matters for your numbers. Some coating manufacturers run dealer or certified installer programs. [Penntek's dealer page](https://penntek.com/become-a-dealer/) says its dealers operate independently and do not pay franchise fees or royalties, that startup costs vary with your existing equipment and team, and that every new dealer completes a two day, hands on certified installation training through Penntek Academy. The page does not address territory or exclusivity.

So a dealer program gets you a product line, training, and some manufacturer support without a revenue share. It does not get you a brand homeowners recognize, a protected territory, or a marketing program. For an installer who already knows prep, that can be a middle path between buying a franchise and going fully independent.

## What does a garage floor coating franchise really cost?

The franchise fee is the smallest part of the long term cost. Garage floor coating franchise cost has three layers:

1. **The franchise fee** (FDD Item 5), paid once at signing. On the sites above, $50,000 to $74,950.
2. **The rest of the startup investment** (Item 7): equipment, vehicle, initial product, training travel, opening advertising, and working capital. On the sites above, the full Item 7 ranges run from about $136,000 to $284,000, fee included.
3. **Ongoing fees** (Item 6): royalty, brand fund, technology, and any minimums. These come out of every job for the life of the agreement.

Here is what the third layer looks like with the one brand that publishes its rates. Granite Garage Floors lists a 6.5% royalty and a 2% brand fund, or 8.5% of revenue. Take a single crew doing an illustrative $378,000 a year, which is the full time one crew example in [how much a floor coating business makes](/blog/how-much-does-a-floor-coating-business-make):

| Illustrative annual revenue | Royalty plus fund at 8.5% | Over a 10 year term at flat revenue |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $250,000 | $21,250 a year | $212,500 |
| $378,000 | $32,130 a year | $321,300 |
| $700,000 | $59,500 a year | $595,000 |

The revenue figures are illustrative, not Granite's results, and term length varies by agreement. The point is the scale: over the life of the agreement, ongoing fees usually dwarf the franchise fee. The right question is whether the brand, training, and lead program produce more than that in extra profit. ## How do you read FDD Item 7 and Item 19?

The FDD is the disclosure document every franchisor has to give you under the FTC's [Franchise Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/franchise-rule). The FTC's [Consumer's Guide to Buying a Franchise](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/consumers-guide-buying-franchise) says you must receive it at least 14 days before you are asked to sign any contract. Two items do most of the work when you compare coating brands.

**Item 7: Estimated initial investment.** This is the low to high range of what it costs to open, line by line: franchise fee, equipment, vehicle, initial inventory, training travel and wages, opening advertising, insurance, and working capital for the first months. Read the notes under the table, not just the totals. They tell you what is excluded. GarageExperts, for example, notes that its range does not include royalties or advertising fees and assumes two people trained for five days. Plan on the high end of the range, because coating businesses in cold markets need to carry costs through a slow winter.

**Item 19: Financial performance representations.** The FTC guide describes Item 19 as the claims the franchisor chooses to make about the sales or earnings of its franchises, and notes that no other financial performance claim may be made if it does not appear there. Three things to check:

- **Is it revenue or profit?** Both Item 19 figures quoted above are revenue. A coating job typically spends 40 to 50% of its price on materials, crew labor, and consumables before overhead, marketing, and royalty.
- **Who is in the average?** Find out whether it includes every location or only those open a full year, and how many fell below the average.
- **What is missing?** If Item 19 is blank, the franchisor has chosen not to make an earnings claim. Ask current owners instead.

Then read Item 20, which the FTC guide says shows growth and owner turnover in the system, and call owners from that list, including ones who left. The SBA's [planning resources](https://www.sba.gov/counseling/plan-your-business/) also include a section on buying an existing business or franchise, and a franchise attorney review is worth its cost next to a ten year agreement.

## What should you ask about leads before you buy?

The brand, the coating system, and the training are the easy parts to evaluate. Lead flow is the hard part, and it is where most franchise locations either thrive or stall. Ask the franchisor and at least five current owners:

1. How many booked estimates a week does the brand fund produce in a territory like mine?
2. Who calls the homeowner, and how fast? Our [benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) show about 90% of homeowners answer when called within one minute, and the rate falls fast after that.
3. Am I allowed to run my own local marketing, and with which vendors?
4. Do most owners spend their own money on local ads on top of the fund?

If you already own a location and the calendar is thin, our guide for a [garage coating franchise owner without enough leads](/guides/garage-coating-franchise-owner-not-enough-leads) walks through whether it is a volume, speed, or booking problem. ## Franchise or independent: which makes more sense?

| | Franchise (sites above) | Independent |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Startup cost | About $136,000 to $284,000 published ranges | Roughly $20,000 to $45,000 for most owner operators |
| Ongoing fees | Royalty and fund on every job (8.5% where published) | None |
| Training | Included | Manufacturer training or an experienced installer |
| Brand | Established name | Built one review at a time |
| Territory | Protected, defined by the agreement | Anywhere you can drive |
| Lead flow | Shared program, plus your own spend in many cases | Entirely on you or a partner |

The independent startup range comes from our guide on [how to start a floor coating business](/blog/how-to-start-a-floor-coating-business). The full comparison, including who each path suits, is in [garage coating franchise vs independent](/blog/garage-coating-franchise-vs-independent).

The short version: a franchise makes the most sense for someone new to concrete who wants a system, a brand, and structure, and is willing to share revenue for it. Going independent makes more sense for an experienced installer who would rather put the royalty money into marketing they control. ## Frequently asked questions

### What is the cheapest garage floor coating franchise?

Of the brands whose franchise sites we could verify in October 2026, GarageExperts published the lowest starting investment on its investment page at $136,300, and Granite Garage Floors the lowest franchise fee at $50,000. The cheapest to open is not always the cheapest to own, because royalties and fund fees run for the life of the agreement. Compare Item 6 alongside Item 7 in each current FDD.

### How much does a garage floor coating franchise cost?

On the franchise sites we checked in October 2026, published investment ranges ran from about $136,000 to $284,000, with franchise fees from $50,000 to $74,950. On top of that come ongoing royalties and fund fees. The one brand that publishes them lists 6.5% plus 2% of revenue. Confirm every figure in the current FDD, since sites and disclosure documents change every year.

### Do garage coating franchises publish their royalty rates?

Most do not publish them on their websites. Of the four franchise brands in our table, only Granite Garage Floors publishes its royalty and brand fund rates on its site. The rest disclose them in Item 6 of the FDD, which you receive at least 14 days before signing.

### Is Item 19 revenue the same as what a franchise owner makes?

No. The Item 19 figures quoted on the coating franchise sites we checked are revenue. Owner income is what is left after materials, crew labor, royalty, brand fund, insurance, vehicles, equipment payments, and marketing. Ask the franchisor whether the FDD includes any expense or profit data, and ask current owners what they keep.

### Is a Penntek dealer the same as a franchise owner?

According to Penntek's own dealer page, no. Penntek dealers operate independently and do not pay franchise fees or royalties, and new dealers complete two days of certified installation training. A dealer gets a product line and training but not a franchise brand or protected territory.
