# Best Floor Coating Marketing and Lead Generation Companies in 2026

**Meta Description:** An honest roundup of floor coating marketing and lead generation companies in 2026: what each one does, what triggers the charge, real client results, and who each is best for.

---

Floor coating marketing and lead generation companies split into three groups: niche agencies that serve concrete and epoxy coating contractors and bill for the marketing work, shared lead marketplaces that resell the same homeowner to several contractors, and pay per appointment providers that charge a fee per booked estimate. The best choice depends less on the logo and more on which pricing model matches who answers your phone.

**Disclosure:** Appointly Solutions publishes this page and appears on this list. We have tried to describe every other company by what it publicly says about itself rather than by what would flatter us, and we have not invented prices for anyone. Details reflect each company's public marketing as of 2026 and change over time, so confirm specifics with them directly.

## Key takeaways

- Most coating-specific agencies sell marketing services, which means you are billed for the work whether or not estimates land on your calendar.
- Shared lead marketplaces are the cheapest per unit and the most expensive per installed floor, closing at 5 to 12%.
- Pay per appointment providers bill their fee per booked estimate and do the calling, qualifying, and booking themselves.
- Whoever you pick, get four things in writing: exclusivity at the market level, the qualification definition, the credit policy, and who owns the ad account.
- The best provider for a shop with a full-time appointment setter is usually not the best provider for an owner-operator on a grinder all day.

## How to judge a floor coating lead generation company

Before the list, the five questions that actually separate these companies. Ask every one of them on every sales call.

1. **What triggers a charge?** A retainer bills monthly for the work. Pay per lead bills per form fill. Pay per appointment bills per confirmed estimate. These are three completely different risk profiles wearing the same "lead generation" label.
2. **How many coating contractors do you work with in my market?** Lead-level exclusivity is not market-level exclusivity. If the same provider runs campaigns for a competitor two towns over, you are bidding against a company using the same creative and the same targeting.
3. **Who calls the homeowner, and how fast?** Speed to lead decides everything. Our [benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) show about 90% of homeowners answer when the call goes out inside a minute and 60 to 70% of leads become booked estimates. If the provider hands you a lead and expects you to dial, that is your job now.
4. **What happens when it does not work?** Ask what is credited, what is refunded, and what the exit looks like. A provider whose revenue is unaffected by your results will tell you it is a learning phase.
5. **Who owns the ad account and the assets?** Some providers build inside your Business Manager. Some run campaigns from their own. Neither is wrong, but you should know which, and what happens to the campaigns if you part ways.

## Shared lead marketplaces

**What they are:** Angi, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack, Networx, and similar platforms that sell homeowner form fills to contractors.

**Pricing model:** Per lead, typically $30 to $120 for coating work, sometimes with a setup fee. The same lead is commonly sold to three to five contractors.

**Best for:** Shops with a dedicated person who calls back within two minutes every time, including evenings, and who are comfortable competing on speed and price.

**Honest assessment:** Contractors who track it report closing 5 to 12% of shared leads. At $50 a lead and an 8% close rate, that is roughly $625 in lead cost per installed floor, before counting the hours spent chasing homeowners who already booked somebody else. It is the cheapest lead and usually the most expensive floor.

## So which one should you choose?

**If you employ a real appointment setter,** somebody whose actual job is answering inside a minute through evenings and Saturdays, a coating-specific agency running strong campaigns is likely your best value. You supply the speed to lead that turns their leads into estimates, and you keep the lower per-unit cost.

**If your people are in the field all day,** the honest answer is that most agency engagements will underperform for you, not because the campaigns are bad but because leads decay in minutes and nobody is there. Either hire the setter or buy confirmed appointments.

**If you want the widest long-term moat,** organic search, maps, and reviews compound in a way paid campaigns never do. Pair that with something that fills the calendar this month, because SEO will not.

**If you want to buy outcomes rather than activity,** pay per appointment is the only model on this list where the provider earns no fee when estimates do not materialize. That is the structural argument for it, and it is the reason we built Appointly the way we did.
