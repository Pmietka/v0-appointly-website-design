# How to Follow Up on Floor Coating Estimates That Did Not Close

**Meta Description:** A 30 day follow-up plan for floor coating estimates that did not close: what to set up before you leave, what to text and when, and how to keep unsold homeowners for next season.

---

Most floor coating estimates that do not close on the spot are not a no. They are a not yet. The homeowner needs to talk to a spouse, wants to see one more quote, or is waiting on timing or money. The contractors who win those jobs set a clear next step before leaving the driveway, send a written quote the same day, follow a simple 30 day sequence of calls and texts that each add something useful, and then keep the homeowner on a seasonal list instead of deleting them.

## Key takeaways

- An unsold estimate is the cheapest job you will ever book. The lead, the phone call, and the drive are already paid for.
- Most homeowners who do not sign are waiting on a spouse, another quote, timing, or budget. Very few have decided no.
- Before you leave, agree on a specific next step and a time for the follow-up call.
- Send the written quote the same day, with photos of their garage and a finished job like it.
- Follow up over 30 days with a mix of calls and texts, and make every message useful. "Just checking in" is not a follow-up.
- Never chase with discounts. It tells the homeowner your first price was padded.
- Record why each estimate did not close. The pattern tells you what to fix.

## Why are unsold estimates worth so much?

Because everything expensive about the job has already happened.

You paid for the ad. Someone answered the lead, qualified the homeowner, and booked the time. You drove out, walked the garage, showed the samples, and measured. The homeowner has met you, seen your work, and has a price in hand.

Now compare that to a brand new lead. Starting over costs the full [cost per qualified estimate](/blog/cost-per-qualified-floor-coating-estimate) again. Following up on the estimate you already ran costs a few texts and a phone call.

Yet most contractors send one "let me know if you have questions" text and move on. That is where a lot of profit leaks out of a floor coating business.

## Why do homeowners not sign at the estimate?

When we ask contractors why their open quotes did not close, the same four reasons come up almost every time.

| Reason | What it usually means | What helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| "I need to talk to my spouse" | The other decision maker was not there | Send a clear written quote they can share, and book a call when both can be on |
| "I am getting other quotes" | They are comparing on price, or on trust | Show what is included that the cheaper quote leaves out |
| "Not the right time" | Money, a move, or a busy season | Ask when would be, and put that date in your calendar |
| "Let me think about it" | Something was not answered | Ask what is holding them back before you leave |

The best answers to these at the table are in [the three objections every floor coating estimator hears](/blog/floor-coating-sales-objections). Follow-up is what you do when the answer at the table was not enough.

## What should you do before you leave the driveway?

The best follow-up starts before the estimate ends.

1. **Ask the question directly.** "Is there anything that would keep you from moving forward today?" You will hear the real objection more often than you think.
2. **Set the next step.** Not "I will follow up." Say "Can I give you a call Thursday evening after you and your wife have looked it over?" Get a day and a time.
3. **Get the right contact method.** Ask whether they prefer a call or a text. Some homeowners will never answer an unknown number.
4. **Tell them what happens next.** "I will text you the full quote tonight with the photos, so you have everything in one place."
5. **Only use a deadline if it is real.** "Our November schedule fills up around the 15th" is fine if it is true. A made up expiration date costs trust.

If the spouse was not at the estimate, see if you can confirm both decision makers when the appointment is booked next time. That is part of what a good qualifying call does, and it is one of the reasons the [lead to appointment process](/blog/floor-coating-lead-to-appointment-rate) matters so much.

## What does a 30 day follow-up sequence look like?

Here is the sequence we recommend. Every touch has a purpose, and each one gives the homeowner something new.

| Day | Channel | What to send |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 (same evening) | Text and email | Written quote, photos of their garage, photo of a finished garage like theirs |
| 2 or the agreed day | Call | The follow-up call you scheduled at the estimate |
| 5 | Text | A recent review or a finished job in their neighborhood |
| 10 | Call | Answer questions, confirm where they are in the decision |
| 20 | Text | Your install schedule for the coming weeks |
| 30 | Text | A polite close out message |

### Sample texts

**Day 0:**

> Hi Sarah, it was great meeting you and Mike today. Here is the full quote we went over, plus the photos of your garage and the Johnson job on Maple that is the same size. Everything is in there for when you two sit down. I will call Thursday around 6 like we talked about.

**Day 5:**

> Hi Sarah, we finished a floor a few streets over from you this week. Thought you might like to see how it came out. [photo]

**Day 20:**

> Hi Sarah, we are filling our install schedule for the next few weeks. If you want your floor done before [real date], let me know and I will hold a day for you.

**Day 30:**

> Hi Sarah, I do not want to keep bugging you, so this is my last message for now. If the garage floor comes back up, just reply here and I will take care of you. Thanks again for having me out.

The day 30 message gets more replies than you would expect. Telling someone you are closing the file often gets them to answer.

## What should you never do in follow-up?

- **Do not chase with discounts.** "I can take 10% off if you sign this week" tells the homeowner your first number was padded, and they will ask for more.
- **Do not text every day.** Six touches over 30 days is persistent. Twelve in a week is a reason to block you.
- **Do not send "just checking in."** It asks the homeowner to do the work. Send a photo, a review, a scheduling update, or an answer.
- **Do not talk down the other quote.** Show what is included in yours instead. Homeowners notice when a contractor attacks a competitor.
- **Do not delete them after 30 days.** A homeowner who did not buy this fall is a strong lead next spring.

## What happens to homeowners after 30 days?

They go on a seasonal list. This is where a lot of easy work comes from.

A homeowner who got a quote in October and said "not this year" often means exactly that. Send one message at the start of each busy season:

- **Early spring:** "We are opening up our spring install schedule. Want me to hold a spot for your garage?"
- **Early fall:** "We have install days left before the cold. Want to get your floor done before winter?"

Keep it to a few messages a year. The goal is to be the name they remember when they are ready, not to wear them down. The [spring marketing plan](/blog/spring-marketing-plan-floor-coating-contractors) and the [fall marketing plan](/blog/fall-marketing-plan-floor-coating-contractors) both work better when this list is part of them.

## Which numbers tell you if follow-up is working?

We do not publish a benchmark for how many unsold estimates come back, because it depends heavily on market and on how the estimate went. Track your own:

1. **Revived within 30 days.** Of the estimates that did not close on the spot, how many signed inside the follow-up sequence?
2. **Revived from the seasonal list.** How many past quotes signed the next season?
3. **Reason lost.** A simple tag on every open quote: spouse, other quote, timing, budget, or unknown.

If "unknown" is the biggest bucket, the estimator is not asking the direct question before leaving. If "other quote" is the biggest, your presentation is not showing what is different about your system. Those numbers belong next to the rest of your [floor coating contractor KPIs](/blog/floor-coating-contractor-kpis).

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should you follow up on a floor coating estimate?

Run an active sequence for about 30 days with around six touches, mixing calls and texts. After that, move the homeowner to a seasonal list and send one message at the start of each busy season. Many homeowners who say "not this year" are ready the following season.

### What should you text after a floor coating estimate?

Send the written quote the same evening, with photos of the homeowner's garage and a finished garage like theirs. After that, every text should add something useful: a nearby finished job, a recent review, or a real update on your install schedule. Avoid "just checking in" messages.

### Should you offer a discount to close an open floor coating quote?

No. A discount after the estimate tells the homeowner your first price was padded, and it invites them to negotiate further. If price is the real issue, offer a different system or a smaller scope from your quote rather than cutting the price on the same job.

### What do you do when a homeowner goes with a cheaper quote?

Thank them, ask what made the difference, and keep them on your seasonal list. Record the reason. If you keep losing to cheaper quotes, the fix is usually in how the estimate explains the system and what is included, not in your price.

### How do you stop estimates from going unsold in the first place?

Book estimates with homeowners who already understand the product and have both decision makers available, ask directly what would keep them from moving forward before you leave, and set a specific next step. A good first phone call and a clear estimate process prevent most open quotes.
