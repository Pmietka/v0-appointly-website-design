# Floor Coating Warranty Guide: What to Cover, What to Exclude, and How to Use It to Close

**Meta Description:** What a garage floor coating warranty should cover, what it must exclude, how long to offer, and how to use it to close estimates without eating callbacks.

---

A residential floor coating warranty should cover adhesion and workmanship for five to ten years on a slab you ground and moisture tested, pass through the manufacturer's warranty on materials, and exclude moisture vapor, structural cracking, impact damage, UV yellowing on epoxy, and hot tire pickup on epoxy-only systems. Present it mid-pitch, right after you explain prep, and it becomes the reason your quote is higher.

## Key takeaways

- Cover three things only: adhesion, workmanship, and the manufacturer's pass-through warranty on materials.
- Moisture test every slab, write the reading on the estimate, and have the homeowner initial the exclusions.
- Offer five to ten years on a full flake polyaspartic system, and make anything labeled lifetime pro-rated, non-transferable, and limited to adhesion.
- Present the warranty right after you explain grinding and moisture testing, so it becomes the reason your price is higher than the one-day quote.
- Set aside one to two percent of revenue for warranty work and respond to every claim the same day.

## Why is the warranty both a sales tool and a liability document?

A clear warranty closes jobs, and a vague one costs you callbacks, refunds, and one-star reviews. Every homeowner has read a horror story about a peeling garage floor, and the warranty answers the question they are not asking out loud: "What happens if this fails?"

## What actually fails on a coated floor, and whose fault is it?

Most coating failures trace back to prep, moisture, or product choice, and your warranty should say which ones are on you.

| Failure | Usual cause | Who owns it |
|---|---|---|
| Delamination and peeling | Slab not ground to profile, old sealer left behind, or base coat over dust or oil | You |
| Moisture blistering | Vapor through a slab that needed a moisture test and a mitigating primer | You, if you skipped the test |
| Hot tire pickup | Cheap epoxy softens under a hot tire and pulls up | Product choice |
| Yellowing | Epoxy ambers in sunlight; homeowners treat it as a failure | Product choice |
| Cracks telegraphing through | Concrete moves; a coating bridges hairline cracks, not structural ones | The slab |

Polyaspartic topcoats largely eliminate hot tire pickup and yellowing, which is one reason to sell [polyaspartic over epoxy](/blog/polyaspartic-vs-epoxy-garage-floors). The first two rows are prep, and prep is why you can offer a warranty at all.

## What should a floor coating warranty cover?

Keep the covered list to three items:

1. **Adhesion.** The coating will not peel, flake, or delaminate from a properly prepared slab.
2. **Workmanship.** Application defects such as roller marks, holidays, uneven flake, or missed edges will be corrected.
3. **Product defects.** Pass through the manufacturer's warranty for the materials themselves.

Everything else is either an exclusion or a maintenance item.

## What should a floor coating warranty exclude?

Exclusions are where most contractors get burned, so write them plainly and have the homeowner initial them:

- **Moisture vapor transmission** on slabs that were not moisture tested, or that tested above the manufacturer's limit and the homeowner declined vapor mitigation.
- **Structural cracking, heaving, or settling** of the concrete.
- **Damage from impact, gouging, dragging equipment, or chemical spills** beyond the product's rating.
- **Discoloration from UV exposure** on epoxy systems, stated in writing on every epoxy job.
- **Hot tire pickup on epoxy-only systems**, unless you are selling a polyaspartic topcoat.
- **Normal wear**, including gloss loss in traffic lanes.
- **Any coating applied over an existing coating** at the homeowner's request.
- **Work by others**, including anyone who repairs or recoats the floor after you.

The moisture exclusion only holds up if you test. Buy a meter, test every slab, and write the reading on the estimate. A number on paper ends most arguments before they start.

## How long should a floor coating warranty be?

Five to ten years is the right term for a full flake polyaspartic system with proper prep.

| Term | What it signals | Advice |
|---|---|---|
| One to two years | You are not confident, and homeowners notice | Avoid |
| Five to ten years | Serious enough to win the job, short enough to honor | Standard |
| Fifteen years to lifetime | A marketing device | Only if pro-rated, non-transferable, and limited to adhesion on a tested slab |

For a new coating business, a straightforward ten-year adhesion and workmanship warranty is easy to explain and easy to honor. Your supplier's warranty on the product itself is separate, so understand it before you promise anything.

## Which conditions should the warranty depend on?

Attach conditions that protect the floor at no cost to the homeowner:

- **Cure time.** No foot traffic for the stated hours and no vehicles until the product's full cure. Write the exact hours on the invoice.
- **Cleaning.** pH-neutral cleaners only. No harsh solvents, no pressure washing at close range.
- **Mats under hot tires** are optional on polyaspartic and required on epoxy.
- **Report defects within 30 days** of noticing them.

Conditions like these signal that you know the product.

## How do you present the warranty at the estimate?

Use the warranty mid-pitch, right after you explain prep, never as fine print at the end.

> Here's why we grind instead of acid etch, and why we test the slab for moisture before we start. Your floor comes with a ten-year warranty on adhesion and workmanship, and I can only offer that because of the prep. The companies that skip the grind can't warranty the floor, and that's usually why their quote is cheaper.

You have just turned your warranty into the reason your price is higher. ## How do you handle a warranty claim without losing the customer?

Respond the same day, look before you decide, and fix covered items without an argument. Handled that way, callbacks turn into reviews:

1. **Respond the same day.** Even if you cannot get there for a week, the homeowner needs to know you are not dodging.
2. **Go look before you decide.** Most claims are smaller than they sound on the phone.
3. **Fix covered items without an argument.** A half-day repair is cheaper than a one-star review, and a happy homeowner leaves the [Google review](/blog/google-reviews-floor-coating-contractors) that closes your next three jobs.
4. **Explain excluded items with the paperwork.** Show the moisture reading, the initialed exclusion, and the cure-time instructions, then offer a paid repair at a fair price.

Budget one to two percent of revenue for warranty work.

## What goes in a simple warranty template?

Your attorney should review the final document, but the structure is standard:

- Company name, customer name, address, install date, system installed
- Coverage: adhesion and workmanship for a stated term, non-transferable by default
- Exclusions: the list above
- Conditions: cure, cleaning, reporting
- Remedy: repair or recoat the affected area at the company's discretion; no cash refunds
- Moisture test result and prep method recorded on the document
- Homeowner signature and initials beside the exclusions

Keep a signed copy with the job photos. Together they are your defense in any dispute and your proof for the next homeowner who asks.

## Frequently asked questions

### How long should a garage floor coating warranty be?

A garage floor coating warranty should run five to ten years on a full flake polyaspartic system installed over a ground and moisture-tested slab. One to two years signals doubt, and anything labeled lifetime is a marketing device that only works if it is pro-rated, non-transferable, and limited to adhesion.

### Does a floor coating warranty cover moisture problems?

A floor coating warranty should not cover moisture vapor transmission unless you tested the slab, it passed, and the failure still happened. Exclude slabs that were not tested, and slabs that tested above the manufacturer's limit where the homeowner declined vapor mitigation. Record the reading on the estimate; a written number ends most moisture disputes.

### Should a floor coating warranty transfer to a new homeowner?

Make a floor coating warranty non-transferable by default. Transferability is a small selling point for a homeowner planning to move, but it exposes you to claims from a buyer who never heard your care instructions. If you offer it, charge a modest fee, re-inspect the floor at the sale, and keep the same exclusions.

### How much should a floor coating contractor budget for warranty work?

Set aside one to two percent of revenue for warranty repairs. Say your coating business does $500,000 a year: that is $5,000 to $10,000 for callbacks, enough for a few half-day repairs and their materials. A crew that grinds every slab and moisture tests before coating should stay at the low end.
