# Why New Build Homeowners Are the Best Garage Floor Coating Customers in 2026

**Meta Description:** Why new construction homeowners book garage floor coatings faster, the concrete cure catch that burns contractors, and how to find new build neighborhoods in your market.

---

New construction homeowners are the best garage floor coating customers in 2026 because they have an empty garage, a move in deadline, and a clean slab. At Appointly, we have booked over 1,000 floor coating appointments, and when one of our markets started outperforming every other account, most of the homeowners booking were in brand new builds.

This guide covers why new build homeowners buy, the concrete catch that burns contractors, and how to find new build neighborhoods in your market.

## Why new build homeowners book faster

### 1. The garage is empty

There are no cars, shelves or boxes to move. The homeowner is looking at the bare gray slab the builder left behind, and it is the easiest time they will ever have to get it coated.

### 2. They are on a clock

Most new build homeowners want the floor done before the moving truck shows up, not after oil stains and tire marks do. That timeline creates urgency that a homeowner who has lived somewhere for 15 years rarely has.

### 3. The price feels small

They just signed for the biggest purchase of their life. Next to a new home, a garage floor coating feels like a finishing touch, not a major expense.

### 4. The slab is clean

There is no old coating to strip and no years of stains to fight. That usually means a cleaner, more predictable install.

## Why speed matters more with new build homeowners

A homeowner on a deadline does not wait around. Many fill out a few quote forms in one night and book whoever calls back first. If you are on a job site when the lead comes in, the estimate is often gone before you can call.

## The catch: new concrete needs time to cure

The biggest mistake contractors make on new builds is coating the slab too soon. Fresh concrete releases moisture as it cures, and moisture coming up through the slab is one of the most common causes of coatings lifting or failing.

- **Wait for the cure.** Most industry guidance says to wait at least 28 days after the concrete is poured. Always follow your coating manufacturer's spec.
- **Test for moisture.** Use a plastic sheet test (ASTM D4263) or a calcium chloride test (ASTM F1869) before you install.
- **Still grind the slab.** New concrete often has laitance and curing compounds on the surface. Diamond grinding is still needed for the coating to bond.
- **Ask about the builder warranty.** Have the homeowner check their builder's warranty terms before any work on the slab.

The good news is that the cure window does not stop you from booking the job. Book the estimate now, lock in the install date after the cure window, and schedule it around the closing or move in date.

## How to find new build neighborhoods in your market

1. **Check building permit data.** The U.S. Census Bureau's Building Permits Survey shows new residential permits by metro and county, and many counties publish permit data online.
2. **Look at builder websites.** National builders like D.R. Horton, Lennar and PulteGroup list their active communities by city.
3. **Drive the subdivisions.** Look for model homes, "Now Selling" signs and streets with new driveways.
4. **Target those ZIP codes with ads.** Run Meta ads on Facebook and Instagram to the ZIP codes around active communities, with creative showing finished garages in homes that look like theirs.
5. **Talk to the people at closing.** Real estate agents, builder sales reps and closing coordinators all talk to new homeowners right when the garage is empty.

## How to sell a garage floor to a new build homeowner

- **Lead with timing.** "Get it done before you move in" is the strongest line you have.
- **Show homes like theirs.** Photos of finished garages in similar new construction homes beat generic before and after shots.
- **Explain the cure window up front.** Homeowners trust the contractor who tells them to wait, not the one who rushes it.
- **Schedule around their closing.** Offer an install date that lines up with their move in.

## FAQ

### How long after a new home is built can you coat the garage floor?

Most industry guidance says to wait at least 28 days after the concrete is poured, then test for moisture before installing. Always follow your coating manufacturer's spec.

### Is it better to coat a garage floor before moving in?

Yes, for most homeowners. The garage is empty, the slab is clean, and the install is faster and easier before cars and storage move in.

### Do new concrete garage floors still need grinding?

Yes. New concrete often has laitance and curing compounds on the surface, so diamond grinding is still needed for the coating to bond properly.

They have an empty garage, a move in deadline, and a clean slab, and they are already investing in their new home. That combination usually means faster decisions.
