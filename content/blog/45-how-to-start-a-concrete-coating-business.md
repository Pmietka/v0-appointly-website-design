# How to Start a Concrete Coating Business: Pick Your Niche, Your Model, and Your First 90 Days

**Meta Description:** How to start a concrete coating business: which niche to start in, independent vs franchise vs dealer, what to buy first, and a 90 day plan to your first paying floors.

---

To start a concrete coating business, pick one niche to launch in (for most people that is residential garage floors), choose how you will operate (independent, franchise, or as a manufacturer's certified installer), learn prep under an experienced installer, buy the grinder and dust extraction first, and set up a way to book estimates before you finish your first floor. Most owner operators start for $20,000 to $45,000. The businesses that make it past year one are the ones that solve the calendar early.

## Key takeaways

- "Concrete coating" covers garage floors, basements, patios and pool decks, decorative metallic floors, and commercial or industrial floors. Start in one.
- Residential garage floors are the best starting niche for most new owners: one day installs, a $2,000 to $4,000 ticket, and demand you can create with ads.
- You can start independent, buy a franchise, or become a certified installer for a coating manufacturer. Each trades cost against control.
- Coatings fail because of prep. Install several floors under someone experienced before you sell your first one.
- Buy the grinder and dust extraction first. Almost everything else can be rented, bought used, or added later.
- Plan how you will get estimates before you plan anything else. A great installer with an empty calendar is not a business.

## What counts as a concrete coating business?

Concrete coating is a broad trade. Most businesses start in one of these and add others later:

| Niche | Typical customer | Why start here | Why wait |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Residential garage floors | Homeowners | One day installs, strong demand, easy to show in photos | Competitive in saturated markets |
| Basements and interior floors | Homeowners | Year round work, no weather risk | Moisture issues require more experience |
| Patios, porches, pool decks | Homeowners | Pairs well with garage jobs | UV and weather exposure need the right system |
| Decorative metallic epoxy | Homeowners, retail, offices | High ticket, visual portfolio | Skill heavy, easy to get wrong early |
| Commercial and industrial | Businesses, warehouses, shops | Large jobs, repeat work | Longer sales cycles, bids, bigger crews |

For a first year business, residential garage floors are the easiest place to start. The job is repeatable, the homeowner decides quickly, and a before and after photo does most of the selling. Once your prep and install are dialed in, you can add basements, patios, and small [commercial floor coating jobs](/blog/commercial-floor-coating-jobs-for-residential-contractors) as off season work.

## Which business model should you choose?

There are three common ways into the trade.

### Independent

You form an LLC, choose your coating system, buy your equipment, and build the brand yourself. You keep every dollar of margin and can change products, pricing, and territory whenever you want. You also carry all of the risk of building a lead flow from nothing.

### Franchise

You pay an upfront fee and an ongoing royalty for a brand, a system, training, and a territory. It is the most structured path and the most expensive over time. We break down the tradeoffs, including which parts of the Franchise Disclosure Document to read, in [garage coating franchise vs going independent](/blog/garage-coating-franchise-vs-independent).

### Certified installer for a manufacturer

Many coating manufacturers run training programs and certify independent installers on their system. You stay independent and own your business, but you get hands on training, product support, and sometimes warranty backing from the manufacturer. For someone new to concrete who does not want to pay a royalty, this is often the best middle path.

| | Independent | Franchise | Certified installer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Upfront cost | Lowest | Highest | Low to moderate |
| Ongoing fees | None | Royalty and marketing fund | Usually none, you buy their product |
| Training | Find it yourself | Included | Included or low cost |
| Product choice | Any | Chosen for you | Usually tied to that manufacturer |
| Brand | Build your own | Theirs | Your own |

## What do you need to buy first?

The equipment list for a concrete coating business is long, but only a few items actually decide whether your floors hold up.

1. **A planetary grinder with dust extraction.** This is the purchase that matters most. Proper surface profile is the difference between a coating that lasts and one that peels in a year. Rent for your first few jobs if you need to, but budget for your own.
2. **Edge grinder and prep tools.** For the edges, cracks, and control joints the big grinder cannot reach.
3. **A moisture meter.** Moisture coming up through the slab is one of the most common causes of coating failure. Test every floor.
4. **Mixing, spreading, and safety gear.** Drills and paddles, squeegees, rollers, spiked shoes, respirators, and gloves.
5. **A way to haul it all.** A used enclosed trailer or van works. Material should stay out of big temperature swings.

## How do you learn the trade before you sell?

Prep is where every new coating business gets humbled. The slab looks fine, the coating goes down beautifully, and three months later it is lifting at the edges because of moisture, an old sealer, or a profile that was not deep enough.

Before you sell a floor to a stranger:

- **Install at least three floors under someone who has done hundreds.** A manufacturer training class, an experienced installer, or a job with a coating company all work.
- **Coat your own garage and a few for friends or family at cost.** These become your first photos and reviews.
- **Write a prep checklist and use it every time.** Moisture test, contaminant check, grind, crack and joint repair, vacuum, then coat.
- **Know your system's limits.** Every product has minimum temperatures, recoat windows, and moisture limits on its spec sheet. Follow them.

## How do you get customers for a new concrete coating business?

This is where most new coating businesses stall. The floors are good, the price is right, and the calendar is empty.

Free channels come first: your Google Business Profile with real photos, posts in local community groups, and every contractor, realtor, and neighbor you know. They will get you a handful of estimates.

After that, the fastest way to create demand is Meta ads showing a real local before and after, sent to a landing page. The ads are only half of it. The other half is speed. A homeowner called within a minute answers about 90% of the time, and 60 to 70% of leads should turn into booked estimates when they are called that fast. Called the next morning, both numbers fall apart. The full breakdown is in [Meta ads for floor coating contractors](/blog/meta-ads-for-floor-coating-contractors) and [speed to lead wins floor coating jobs](/blog/speed-to-lead-wins-floor-coating-jobs).

That creates a real problem for a new owner operator. You cannot answer a lead in a minute while you are running a grinder. So you either hire someone to answer the phone, accept that a share of your leads will go cold, or pay a service that calls and books for you.

## What mistakes sink new concrete coating businesses?

- **Selling too many systems too soon.** Pick one system you can install perfectly and sell it with confidence.
- **Cutting corners on prep.** One failed floor costs more in callbacks and reviews than ten good ones earn.
- **Pricing to beat the cheapest quote.** New owners underprice to win jobs and then cannot afford to market or fix mistakes.
- **Skipping insurance and contracts.** Get general liability coverage and a written contract with a clear warranty from day one. Our [floor coating warranty guide](/blog/floor-coating-warranty-guide) covers what to promise and what to exclude.
- **Waiting to market until the first floors are done.** The first estimates take weeks to arrive. Start building the calendar while you are still training.

## Frequently asked questions

### How much does it cost to start a concrete coating business?

Most owner operators start a concrete coating business for $20,000 to $45,000. The grinder and dust extraction are the biggest single purchase. The low end assumes used equipment and a vehicle you already own. A franchise costs more because of the franchise fee and required startup investment.

### Is a concrete coating business profitable?

It can be. Garage floor coating typically sells for $5 to $8 per square foot, and a two person crew can finish a typical two car garage in a day or two. Profit depends on pricing, prep quality, and most of all on keeping the calendar full with qualified estimates.

### What is the best niche to start a concrete coating business in?

For most new owners, residential garage floors. The installs are fast and repeatable, homeowners decide quickly, and demand can be created with ads. Basements, patios, decorative floors, and commercial work make good additions once your prep and install process is solid.

### Should you start a concrete coating business independently or buy a franchise?

A franchise gives you a brand, training, and a system in exchange for an upfront fee and ongoing royalties. Going independent costs less and keeps all of your margin, but you build everything yourself. Becoming a certified installer for a coating manufacturer is a middle path that gives you training and product support without a royalty.

### Do you need a license to start a concrete coating business?

It depends on your state. Some states require a specialty contractor license for coatings, some include it under general contracting, and some only require a business license. Check with your state contractor board, form an LLC, and carry general liability insurance before you take a deposit.
