# Winter Marketing Plan for Floor Coating Contractors in Cold Markets

**Meta Description:** A month-by-month winter marketing plan for floor coating contractors in cold markets: what to run, what to pause, and when to restart ads before spring.

---

A winter marketing plan for a cold-market floor coating contractor has three parts: cut ad spend in December and January but never to zero, sell spring install dates and interior floors instead of unheated garages, and restart full spend 4 to 6 weeks before your first reliable install week. Florida, the Gulf Coast, and the Carolinas book year round. Everywhere else drops in winter.

## Key takeaways

- Florida, the Gulf Coast, and the Carolinas book year round; the Midwest, the Pacific Northwest, and the rest of the north are strong spring through fall and drop in winter.
- Cut winter ad spend by half to two thirds rather than pausing, because a paused account has to relearn in February while competitors are already running.
- Restart full spend 4 to 6 weeks before your first reliable install week so the first warm Saturday already has estimates on it.
- Sell what a cold-market homeowner can actually buy in January: a spring install date, a basement, a heated garage, or a commercial interior.
- Spend December and January on reviews, website fixes, crew training, and equipment, the four jobs that never get done in May.

## Why winter is slow for floor coating in cold markets

Winter is slow in cold markets because homeowners are not thinking about the garage and most systems cannot be installed on a slab near freezing. Our [seasonality benchmark](/floor-coating-benchmarks) is simple: Florida, the Gulf Coast, and the Carolinas book year round. Everywhere else is strong spring through fall and drops in winter unless the contractor can coat in cold conditions.

Two things drive the drop. Demand: nobody is cleaning out the garage in January in Minneapolis or Spokane. Chemistry: a cold slab slows cure, and an unheated Midwest garage is often below the range on a standard epoxy data sheet. Neither is permanent. For the full demand curve by season, read [when floor coating demand peaks and dips](/blog/best-time-of-year-for-floor-coating-jobs).

## What to run each month from November through February

Run a shrinking campaign through November and December, a small holding campaign in January, and a growing campaign in February.

### November: finish the fall push, then taper

November is the last month with natural residential demand in the north. Homeowners want the garage coated before they park inside for winter, so keep full spend through the first two weeks and make every ad about getting it done before the cold.

In the second half of the month, taper. Drop budget in steps and switch the creative from "before winter" to "book now, we install in spring." If you can coat in cold conditions, this is when your cold-weather ad goes live.

### December: cut spend, keep the pilot light on

December is the month to cut ad spend hard, but not to zero. A campaign that keeps running at a low daily budget keeps your pixel, your audiences, and the learning phase alive. Pause for ten weeks and you relearn in February while competitors are already spending.

Run one small campaign with two ads: a spring-date offer and an interior-floor offer. Call every lead within a minute, because each winter lead matters more. Our [phone answer rate benchmark](/floor-coating-benchmarks) is 90% when the lead is called within one minute, and that does not change because it is cold outside.

### January: sell spring dates and interior floors

January is the month to fill April. The homeowner who fills out a form in January is a planner. Give them a locked spring install date, a clear price per square foot range, and a small reason to book early, such as first pick of dates.

Keep the interior-floor ad running alongside it. Basements, heated garages, workshops, and commercial interiors are all installable in January because the slab is conditioned. Call past customers about a second space this month too.

### February: restart ahead of the thaw

February is when you turn the budget back up, and it is earlier than most contractors expect. Count back 4 to 6 weeks from your first reliable install week. In much of the Midwest that puts your restart in mid February for an early April start. In the Pacific Northwest it can be earlier.

The lead time is the pipeline. A form fill becomes an estimate in a few days, a signed job in a week or two, and an install a few weeks after that. Wait for the first warm day and your first full install week is in May. Start in February and it is early April. The detailed spring plan is in our [spring marketing plan for floor coating contractors](/blog/spring-marketing-plan-floor-coating-contractors); this post is the runway to it.

## How to market cold-weather capability without overpromising

If you can install in cold conditions, say so in your ads, but describe what you do rather than what the product guarantees. Some polyaspartic and polyurea systems cure at lower temperatures than standard epoxy; check the manufacturer's data sheet and stay inside the rated range. Market the process, not the chemistry:

- "We install in heated garages all winter."
- "We bring portable heat and warm the slab before we coat."
- "Some of our systems are rated for cooler installs. We check your garage first and tell you whether it can be done in January or should wait for March."

That last line books estimates. Homeowners in cold markets assume nobody can do the job in winter, so "maybe, let me check" gets the estimate. "Any temperature, no problem" gets the callback in March when the floor fails. Never quote a cure temperature from memory, and never promise a winter install on an unheated slab before you have seen it.

## Which winter offers actually book estimates

The winter offers that work sell something a cold-market homeowner can actually buy in January. Four hold up:

1. **Book now, install in spring.** The homeowner locks a week in March or April. You are selling certainty, not a discount. A small early-booking perk is fine; a deep discount is not necessary.
2. **Interior floors.** Basements, laundry rooms, workshops, and heated garages. The slab is conditioned, so the install is a normal job. This is the easiest winter revenue most contractors ignore.
3. **Commercial interiors.** Warehouses, shops, and showrooms are climate controlled, and many owners prefer a winter install because it is their slow season too. See [how residential contractors land commercial floor coating jobs](/blog/commercial-floor-coating-jobs-for-residential-contractors).
4. **Heated garage installs.** One line of ad copy that says "heated garage? we install all winter" pulls these homeowners out.

Skip the "winter special" with a big percentage off. It trains your market to wait for discounts and does not solve the real problem, which is that the slab is cold.

## What to do in winter versus what to stop doing

The whole plan fits in one table.

| Do this in winter | Stop doing this in winter |
|---|---|
| Run a small always-on campaign at a low daily budget | Pausing every ad from Thanksgiving to March |
| Advertise spring install dates and interior floors | Advertising unheated garage installs in January |
| Call every lead within a minute, because there are fewer of them | Letting winter leads sit because "nobody buys in winter" |
| Restart full spend 4 to 6 weeks before install season | Waiting for the first warm day to turn ads back on |
| Train the crew and service the grinders and vacs | Discovering the grinder is down on the first April job |

## How to use the slow season to get ready for spring

Use December and January for the work that makes spring more profitable: reviews, the website, crew training, and equipment.

**Reviews.** Your fall customers just got a new floor and they are happy. Ask every one of them in December. Our guide to [getting more Google reviews as a floor coating contractor](/blog/google-reviews-floor-coating-contractors) covers the ask and the timing.

**Website.** Shoot new photos and video of your best fall floors. Fix the phone number, the contact form, and the page speed. Make the form text you, not just email you, so you can call within a minute in March.

**Crew training.** Winter is the only time you can run a training day without losing a job. Practice the prep, the moisture test, the flake broadcast, and the topcoat on a sample slab.

**Equipment.** Service the grinder, replace diamonds, check the vac filters, and order flake and topcoat before spring lead times stretch.

## Frequently asked questions

### Should a floor coating contractor pause ads completely in winter?

No. A floor coating contractor in a cold market should cut ad spend sharply in December and January but keep one small campaign running. A paused account loses its learning and its warm audiences and has to rebuild in February while competitors are live. A low-budget always-on campaign keeps the pixel warm and still books spring dates.

### When should a cold-market contractor restart full ad spend for spring?

Restart full ad spend 4 to 6 weeks before your first reliable install week. For most Midwest contractors that means mid February for an early April start. The lead time covers form fill to estimate, estimate to signed job, and signed job to install. Waiting for warm weather pushes your first full install week into May.

### Can floor coatings be installed in a garage in winter?

Some floor coatings can be installed in winter, depending on the system and the slab. Heated garages and conditioned interiors are installable year round. Unheated garages depend on slab temperature and the manufacturer's rated range. Some polyaspartic and polyurea systems cure at lower temperatures than standard epoxy; check the data sheet and inspect the slab before promising a date.

### What is the best winter offer for a floor coating contractor?

The best winter offer for a floor coating contractor is a locked spring install date, not a discount. Homeowners who fill out a form in January are planners who want certainty and first pick of dates. Pair it with an interior-floor offer for basements, heated garages, and commercial spaces, and you have revenue now plus a full April.
