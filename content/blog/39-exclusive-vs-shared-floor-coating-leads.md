# Exclusive vs Shared Floor Coating Leads: Which One Actually Wins

**Meta Description:** Exclusive floor coating leads go to one contractor, shared leads go to three to five. What each costs, how they close, and which one wins per installed floor.

---

An exclusive floor coating lead is sold to one contractor. A shared lead is sold to three to five contractors at the same time, which means you are racing four other companies to the phone and then bidding against them on price. Shared leads look cheaper at $30 to $120 each and close at 5 to 12%. Exclusive leads cost more per unit and close far better, and the only comparison that settles it is cost per installed floor.

## Key takeaways

- Shared leads are resold to three to five contractors, so the first caller usually wins and everyone else paid for nothing.
- Contractors who track it honestly close 5 to 12% of shared leads, which puts a $50 shared lead near $625 of lead cost per installed floor.
- Exclusive leads remove the race, which protects your price as much as your close rate. You are not the third quote.
- Exclusivity at the lead level is not the same as exclusivity at the market level. Ask which one you are buying.
- An exclusive booked estimate is the strongest version: one contractor, one confirmed time, no bidding war.

## What is a shared lead?

A shared lead is a form fill sold as a commodity. A homeowner describes a garage floor project on a marketplace, and that form is sold to several contractors at once. Angi, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack, and the niche resellers all run some version of this.

The economics are straightforward and they are not in your favor. Five contractors buy the same homeowner. One of them reaches that person first and books the estimate. The other four paid full price for a voicemail. Then, because the homeowner now has three quotes, the job is priced against the cheapest bidder rather than against the quality of your prep.

**What it costs:** $30 to $120 per lead depending on the market and job type, sometimes with a setup fee in the low thousands.

**What closes:** 5 to 12% for contractors who measure it honestly. At $50 per lead and an 8% close rate, you are paying roughly $625 in lead cost per installed floor, before counting the hours your team spent chasing people who had already booked somebody else.

**When it works:** When you have a dedicated person calling back within two minutes, every time, including evenings. Shared leads reward speed more than any other source, because speed is literally the entire contest. If nobody is manning the phone, do not buy shared leads.

## What is an exclusive lead?

An exclusive lead is generated for you and sold to nobody else. In practice that almost always means paid social or paid search running to your offer in your service area, rather than a marketplace reselling a form.

**What it costs:** $15 to $45 per lead from well-run Meta ads, and $100 to $300 from Google Ads, using the ranges in our [lead cost and sources breakdown](/blog/garage-floor-coating-leads-cost-and-sources).

**What closes:** Far better, for two reasons that compound. First, you are the only company calling, so the answer rate and the booking rate are yours to win rather than to race for. Our [benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) show about 90% answer and 60 to 70% of leads becoming booked estimates when the call goes out inside a minute. Second, and this is the part contractors underrate, you are not automatically the third quote. You get to sell your prep, your system, and your warranty instead of defending your price against two companies the homeowner found ten minutes ago.

**The catch:** Exclusive leads arrive earlier in the decision. The homeowner saw a before and after of a garage in their town and raised their hand. They are interested, not shopping. That is great for margin and bad for anyone who takes four hours to call back.

## Exclusive lead or exclusive market?

This is the question that separates real exclusivity from the word "exclusive" in a sales deck, and most contractors never think to ask it.

**Lead exclusivity** means this particular form fill was sold only to you. It says nothing about whether the same provider is running campaigns for your direct competitor two towns over, bidding against you in the same auction, and selling them the next form fill.

**Market exclusivity** means the provider works with one floor coating contractor in your service area, full stop. Every homeowner the campaigns generate in that area goes to you, because there is nobody else to give them to.

Lead exclusivity without market exclusivity still leaves you competing with a company running the same creative, the same offer, and the same targeting, funded by the same agency. Ask providers directly: how many coating contractors do you work with in my market? Make them answer plainly, get it in writing, and be careful with any answer that includes the word "limited."

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Who else gets it | 3 to 5 contractors | Only you | Only you |
| Typical unit cost | $30 to $120 per lead | $15 to $45 per Meta lead | $125 to $199 per appointment |
| Who calls and books | You, fast, or you lose | You | The provider |
| What you pay for duds | Full price | Full price | Nothing |
| Pricing pressure | Bidding war | You set the frame | You set the frame |
| Typical close rate | 5 to 12% | 20 to 35% | 30 to 40% |

Cost per lead is the wrong column to read. Read the last row against the second row. A $50 shared lead at 8% is about $625 per installed floor. A $30 exclusive lead at a 65% booking rate and a 35% close rate is about $130 in media per installed floor, plus whatever you pay the person doing the calling. That labor is real and it is the honest asterisk on the comparison, but it does not close a gap that size.

## Which should you buy?

**Buy shared leads** if you have a dedicated setter who will call back in under two minutes every single time and you are comfortable winning on speed and price. Some contractors genuinely make this work. Most quietly lose money on it and never run the close rate.

**Buy exclusive leads** if you want margin protection and you have somebody who can call fast and follow up five times across three days.

**Buy exclusive booked estimates** if your crew is in the field all day, nobody is manning the phone at 7pm, and you would rather pay per confirmed appointment than per phone number. ## Frequently asked questions

### Are exclusive floor coating leads worth the extra cost?

Usually yes, because the comparison that matters is cost per installed floor rather than cost per lead. A $50 shared lead closing at 8% costs about $625 per installed floor, while a $30 exclusive lead that books at 60 to 70% and closes at 30 to 40% lands far below that, even after paying someone to do the calling.

### How many contractors get a shared floor coating lead?

Typically three to five. The marketplace sells the same form fill to multiple contractors at once, which is why the first company to call usually books the estimate and the rest pay for a voicemail.

### What is the difference between an exclusive lead and an exclusive market?

An exclusive lead is a form fill sold only to you. An exclusive market means the provider works with just one coating contractor in your service area, so no competitor is running the same campaigns against you. Ask which one you are buying, because the word "exclusive" gets used for both.
