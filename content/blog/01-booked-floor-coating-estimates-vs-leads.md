## Why do booked appointments beat raw leads for floor coating contractors?

Booked appointments win because the work that turns a lead into money happens after the form fill, and a raw lead leaves all of that work with you. You started your business to lay down epoxy and [polyaspartic](/blog/polyaspartic-vs-epoxy-garage-floors) finishes, not to chase form fills at 9pm.

The math on raw leads rarely works. You buy a list, you call, half don't answer, a third already booked someone else, and the rest were just looking. You burn hours dialing instead of quoting garage floors.

A booked appointment skips that. Someone else generates the demand, hits the phone the second the lead comes in, qualifies the job, and books the slot. You show up, walk the garage or basement, quote the system, and close. That's how contractors with full calendars operate. They don't sell leads to themselves. They buy [booked estimates](/floor-coating-leads).

## How does the pay-per-appointment model compare to a monthly-fee agency?

A monthly-fee agency bills you whether the calendar fills or not, and its job ends when a form gets submitted. A pay-per-appointment provider only gets paid when a real estimate lands on your calendar.

Most traditional [marketing agencies](/floor-coating-marketing-agency-alternative) keep part of your budget every month and ask for 3 to 6 months to "build momentum." When leads do come in, the chasing, dialing, and no-shows land on you.

| | Monthly-fee agency | Pay per appointment |
|---|---|---|
| What you pay for | A flat monthly fee, calendar full or empty | One flat fee per booked estimate, nothing for leads that never book |
| What you receive | Leads handed off after the form fill | Confirmed estimates on your calendar |
| Who calls the lead | You | The provider's calling team |
| Contract | Usually a long lock-in | No retainer, no monthly fee, no contract |
| Exclusivity | Often shared demand | One contractor per market |
| Slow months | Still cost full price | Cost nothing |

Say your average epoxy garage floor runs $4,000 and you close roughly one in three estimates you run. Every booked appointment on your calendar is worth chasing. The question stops being "how many leads did I buy" and becomes "how many estimates am I standing in front of this week." ## Why does speed to lead decide whether a lead ever books?

Speed to lead decides it because a homeowner who submits a form at 8pm is excited for about ten minutes, and the first contractor to reach them usually gets the appointment. Our [benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) show a 90% answer rate when the lead is called within one minute of submitting the form, and 60 to 70% of leads should become a booked estimate when follow-up runs at that speed.

A raw lead that sits for an hour is a cold lead. The full job after the click looks like this:

1. Contact the homeowner within a minute, before they call three other coating crews.
2. Qualify the job on the phone: garage, basement, patio, or showroom, plus size and timeline.
3. Book them into a slot that fits your route, on the spot.
4. Confirm at booking, remind at 24 hours and 1 hour, and have a person chase anyone who goes quiet. That system moves show rate from about 80% to about 95%.

A raw lead skips all of that and dumps the work on you. Read more on [why speed to lead wins floor coating jobs](/blog/speed-to-lead-wins-floor-coating-jobs).

## How should you evaluate a booked-appointment provider?

Judge a provider on who makes the call, how fast, whether the demand is exclusive, and whether the pricing is one clear fee per booked estimate. You wouldn't let a crew pour a coating system without checking their prep. Don't pick a provider on a vague pitch either.

**Red flags**

- They sell "leads" and go quiet about who actually calls and books them
- They guarantee a specific number of closed jobs (nobody controls your close rate but you)
- They won't tell you where the leads come from
- The contract has an early termination trap or a long minimum lock-in
- They share the same demand with multiple contractors in your market

**Green flags**

- They run the full job: generate the lead, contact it within a minute, and book the estimate
- Clear lead source (Meta ads, meaning Facebook and Instagram, to homeowners in your area)
- One flat fee per booked estimate and nothing for leads that never book
- One floor coating contractor per market
- References from contractors doing your kind of work
- Confirmed estimates on your calendar, not raw inquiries on a list

Ask them what share of their leads become booked estimates. If they can't answer, they aren't tracking it. Our post on [lead to appointment rate](/blog/floor-coating-lead-to-appointment-rate) covers what a healthy number looks like.
