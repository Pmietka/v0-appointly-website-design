# Why Floor Coating Contractors Are Ditching Marketing Agencies That Don't Book Jobs

**Meta Description:** Marketing agencies bill epoxy contractors for months with little to show. Why coating pros are switching to booked estimates from Meta ads instead of reports.

---

Floor coating contractors leave marketing agencies because they pay for activity, not booked estimates. A typical agreement bills a flat monthly amount for 12 months, whether the calendar fills or not, and most contractors can't tell if anything is working until the money is gone. The fix is to pay only when a homeowner is booked for an estimate.

## Key takeaways

- A 12-month agency contract at $3,000 a month costs $36,000 before you know whether a single estimate came from it.
- Pay-for-activity billing rewards reports and busy work; pay-per-booked-estimate billing rewards a homeowner on your calendar.
- Ask any agency how many booked estimates they expect in the first 30 days; if they can't answer, the incentive is wrong.
- A Meta ad, a call within about one minute, and a booked time slot fill a coating calendar in weeks, not months.
- Never sign a contract you can't leave within 30 days if the calendar isn't filling.

## Why does the pay-for-activity model fail coating contractors?

The problem isn't that marketing can't work. The problem is what you're paying for. A $3,000-a-month contract collects $36,000 a year whether your calendar fills or not. The agency is on the hook for activity: posts, settings changes, reports. Booked estimates are nice to have, not what they're paid to deliver.

In practice your account lands with a junior staffer handling 30 other clients. They tweak settings and push out generic content, and the monthly report shows metrics that look good on a chart but never put a crew on a driveway.

When you ask about leads, you hear "this takes time." Some of it does. But it shouldn't take 12 months to find out whether a strategy works. By month 12 you've spent $36,000 and you still may not know what moved.

## Why do agencies push long contracts?

Long contracts protect the agency, not you. If an agency delivered booked jobs, it wouldn't need a 12-month commitment. It would show you what landed on your calendar last month and let the results make the case.

The contract also traps you. Your schedule isn't filling, but leaving means an early termination fee or riding out the remaining months. You keep paying to "maintain your presence" while the crew across town is running estimates.

A contractor doing real revenue needs to know within a couple of weeks whether a channel is producing work. The pay-for-activity model can't tell you that for months.

## What are you actually paying for?

Most of it is generic work that every client gets:

- Blog posts nobody reads
- Website settings changes
- Low-value directory listings
- Generic posts on your social pages
- A monthly report full of vanity numbers

None of that is dishonest. It just isn't what fills a coating calendar. A sharp before-and-after photo in front of local homeowners beats a wall of generic content. Review count and trust matter more than another article. And the whole game is reaching a homeowner the moment they raise their hand and booking the estimate before anyone else calls.

A generalist agency sells generalist work because that's what it knows. It doesn't know what fills a coating contractor's calendar with [booked estimates](/floor-coating-leads), and it isn't paid to find out.

## Which agency pitches are red flags?

If you hear any of these on a sales call, slow down and ask what lands on your calendar this month.

| What they say | What it usually means |
|---|---|
| "We guarantee results in 90 days." | Nobody can guarantee that. |
| "We can't tell you how many leads you'll get." | Traffic and impressions are the goal. Whether anyone calls is your problem. |
| "A long-term commitment is required." | They aren't confident in near-term results. |
| "We charge for services rendered, not results." | They get paid whether your calendar fills or not. |
| "Our strategy is confidential." | It's the same playbook they run for 50 other clients. |
| "We'll focus on building your online presence." | Vague. Ask what a homeowner on your calendar this week looks like. |

You can run your own ads, but the ads are the easy part. The hard part is answering every lead within about a minute, every day, including evenings and weekends when homeowners fill out forms. Nobody does that from a job site with a grinder running. If you can't cover the phone that fast, hand the front end to someone who can and pay only for booked estimates.
