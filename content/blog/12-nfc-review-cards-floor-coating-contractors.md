# NFC Review Cards for Floor Coating Contractors: Turn Happy Customers Into Your Best Sales Tool

**Meta Description:** NFC review cards help floor coating contractors stack Google reviews fast and build the trust that closes estimates. How they work and how to use them.

---

NFC review cards are wallet-sized cards with a chip that opens your Google review page when a customer taps it with their phone. Hand one to the homeowner right after the final walkthrough, when they're happiest with the floor, and you collect a review in under 30 seconds instead of hoping they remember later. More reviews raise trust, and trust raises your close rate on every estimate.

## Key takeaways

- An NFC card opens your Google review link with one tap, so the whole review takes the homeowner under 30 seconds.
- Ask at the end of the final walkthrough, while the homeowner is looking at the finished floor; that is when they say yes.
- Most smartphones made since 2015 read NFC, and a QR code on the back of the card covers the rest.
- Handing a card to every customer can take you from 15 reviews to 45 in six months, which lifts close rate on every estimate you walk.
- Track your Google review count weekly; expect a visible bump within the first month if the crew hands out cards on every job.

## What are NFC review cards and how do they work?

NFC stands for Near Field Communication, the same tech behind Apple Pay and tap-to-pay credit cards. Your phone has an NFC chip inside it. So does the card.

For a coating contractor the process looks like this:

1. Hand a branded card to the customer at the end of the job.
2. The customer taps the card with their phone. Most phones made in the last five years read NFC.
3. Your Google review link opens on their screen.
4. They leave the review without typing a URL or searching for your business.

The whole thing takes under 30 seconds. Print the card with your business name, phone number, website, and a call to action like "Tap to leave us a review." It fits in a wallet, so the customer sees your name every time they open it.

## Why do Google reviews decide who closes the estimate?

[Google reviews](/blog/google-reviews-floor-coating-contractors) are the strongest trust signal a floor coating contractor has. Before a homeowner hands you a $4,000 garage floor, they pull up your Google Business Profile and look at three things: how many reviews, what rating, and what people actually said.

A coating contractor with 50 reviews at 4.9 closes more estimates than one with 5 reviews at 4.8, even with the same pitch. Reviews prove that real homeowners hired you and liked the result, which makes the next homeowner comfortable saying yes. More trust means a higher [close rate](/blog/how-to-close-more-floor-coating-estimates) on every estimate you walk.

Most coating contractors have barely any reviews. Plenty of local crews sit at 8 to 12. Get to 40 or 50 and the homeowner comparing you to the shop down the road feels a lot better about choosing you.

## How do you get customers to actually leave reviews?

Ask at the moment they're happiest, and make it a one-tap job. Asking feels awkward: the crew is loading up, the homeowner is heading inside, and standing on a fresh floor while someone types a review is uncomfortable. So most contractors don't ask, and unprompted reviews are rare.

The card removes the awkwardness. Hand it over and say, "We'd love to hear how the floor turned out. Tap your phone here, it takes 30 seconds." Because it's that easy, they do it.

The tap opens Google directly, so the review lands where homeowners look before they hire. Not Facebook, not Yelp. Google, right where your next customer is looking.

## Why do photos make reviews sell harder?

A review with a photo of the finished garage floor does three things. It reads as more credible than text alone. It shows the actual work, an oil-stained slab turned into a flake or metallic floor in a real home. And a profile full of real floor photos sells the next job before you arrive, because the homeowner has already seen proof after proof of clean work in homes like theirs.

Many NFC review systems let you prompt the customer to attach a photo. That gives you a steady stream of real before-and-afters that do the selling before you ever walk the garage.

## How does review count compound over time?

Every review makes the next one easier to earn and the next estimate easier to close. You finish a floor, hand over a card, and the customer taps it that evening. The review goes live on your Google Business Profile within a day or two. More reviews hold your rating at 4.9 and raise your total count, and both make the next homeowner more comfortable choosing you over the crew with twelve reviews and a stock photo.

More closed jobs means more floors, and more floors mean more chances to ask. The crew that's been in business ten years with 12 reviews keeps losing the trust battle to you, because you passed them in six months by collecting a review from every happy customer.

## Why do NFC cards beat text and email requests?

Text and email requests work, but the card wins on timing. A text asks the customer to do something later, and most forget. The card asks in the moment they first see the transformed floor, which is peak goodwill.

| Method | When the ask happens | Typical result |
|---|---|---|
| Text or email link | Later, whenever they see the message | Easy to forget, often no review |
| NFC card at the walkthrough | In the moment, looking at the new floor | Review on the spot or within days |

The card also feels less like a sales ask. It's physical, a little novel, and most people have never tapped one, so they try it. And it stays in the wallet as a reminder to leave the review and a built-in referral when a friend mentions their own ugly slab.

Those close rates are an example, not a promise. Our [benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) don't publish a single close rate because it depends on the product and the estimator's skill. ## How to roll out NFC cards in four steps

1. Design and order cards that carry your company name, phone number, website, and a clear call to action: "Tap to leave us a Google review."
2. Load the chip with a link that opens your Google review page. Some providers handle this. Others have you grab the review link from your Google Business Profile.
3. Train the crew to hand a card to every customer at the final walkthrough. This is the critical step. If the team forgets, the cards do nothing.
4. Track results. Watch your Google review count weekly. You should see a clear bump within the first month if a card goes to every customer.

## Frequently asked questions

### Will NFC review cards work if my customers have older phones?

Most smartphones made since 2015 read NFC, which covers the large majority of homeowners a floor coating contractor works for. For anyone whose phone can't tap, print a QR code or a short review link on the back of the card as a backup. Most customers tap, a few scan, and either way the review lands on your Google profile.

### Can competing floor coating contractors copy the NFC card idea?

Yes, any contractor can order NFC review cards, which is exactly why starting now matters. The coating contractor with 45 reviews beats the one with 12 every time a homeowner compares them, and by the time a competitor catches up you'll be at 70. Review count compounds, so the earlier you start handing out cards, the bigger your lead.

### What if customers leave negative reviews through the NFC card?

If you're laying clean, durable floors, the reviews will be positive. The problem most floor coating contractors have isn't bad reviews, it's no reviews at all. When a complaint does come in, treat it as information about something to fix, respond calmly on the profile, and keep collecting. One critical review among 40 good ones barely moves a rating.

### Do I need special software to set up NFC review cards?

No. The tap opens a plain link, whether that's your standard Google review link, a shortlink, or a QR fallback. No app is required on the customer's phone, and nothing runs on your end. The customer needs NFC on their phone, which is built into nearly every modern smartphone, and you need the review link from your Google Business Profile.

### How many Google reviews does a floor coating contractor need?

There's no magic number, but the gap between you and the next crew is what the homeowner notices. Many local coating crews sit at 8 to 12 reviews, so reaching 40 or 50 makes you the obvious choice on a comparison. Past that, keep collecting anyway, because recent reviews matter as much as the total and a steady stream shows you're still doing good work.
