# How to Cut No Shows on Floor Coating Estimates From 20% to 5%

**Meta Description:** Floor coating estimates no-show at 20% with no reminders and 5% with a system. The four-part confirmation process, message wording, and what a no show costs.

---

About 20% of booked floor coating estimates turn into no shows when nobody confirms or reminds the homeowner. With a four-part system, a confirmation at booking, a reminder 24 hours out, a reminder 1 hour out, and a person who triages homeowners who go quiet, the no-show rate drops to about 5%. On 20 booked estimates that is the difference between running 16 and running 19.

## Key takeaways

- Show rate runs about 80% with no reminders and about 95% with a confirmation and reminder system, so without one you lose one estimate in five.
- The system has four parts: a confirmation text at booking, a reminder 24 hours out, a reminder 1 hour out, and a person who calls anyone who stops replying.
- A no show costs a missed sale plus a two to three hour afternoon block and the drive time that could have gone to another homeowner.
- When a homeowner goes quiet, call first, then text, then offer a new time instead of cancelling.
- Calling a lead within one minute of the form raises show rate at the back end, because a homeowner who booked while excited remembers why they booked.

## What is a normal no-show rate on floor coating estimates?

A normal no-show rate is about 20% when the appointment is booked and then left alone, and about 5% when a confirmation and reminder system runs behind it. Those are the show rate numbers on our [floor coating benchmarks page](/floor-coating-benchmarks): roughly 80% show with no reminders, roughly 95% with the full system.

The gap is not about the homeowner's character. A homeowner who filled out a Facebook form on Tuesday night and agreed to Saturday at 10 has a week of work, kids, and other contractors between the booking and the visit. Without a nudge, some forget, some double book, and some assume you forgot about them.

Contractors with no system also rarely track no shows, so the missed estimates get filed under "bad leads" and the marketing gets blamed. The fix is cheaper than new marketing.

## What does a no show cost a floor coating contractor?

A no show costs you a job you might have closed plus a block of time you cannot get back. Take an illustrative slot: a two-hour afternoon window, 30 minutes of driving each way, and ten minutes ringing the bell of an empty house. That is three hours you could have spent grinding, coating, or standing in front of a homeowner who was home.

Now put a number on the job. Say your average garage runs $4,000 and, as an illustrative assumption, you close one in three estimates you actually run. Each estimate you show up to is worth about $1,300 in expected revenue. Four no shows a month on 20 bookings is roughly $5,300 in expected revenue gone, plus twelve hours of owner or crew time. With a system and one no show, the loss is about $1,300 and three hours.

## The four-part system that gets show rate to 95%

The system is four touches, in this order, every time. Skipping any one of them costs you a slice of the gain.

1. **Confirmation at booking.** The moment the homeowner agrees to a time, text the day, the time, the address, and what will happen at the visit. Ask for a one-word reply.
2. **Reminder 24 hours out.** The day before, restate the time and ask if it still works. This catches double bookings while there is still time to move them.
3. **Reminder 1 hour out.** Send a "heading your way" text with the time and the vehicle to look for. This catches the homeowner who forgot and is about to leave for the store.
4. **A person triaging the quiet ones.** Anyone who does not reply to the confirmation or the 24-hour reminder gets a phone call from a human. Automation sends the messages; a person rescues the appointments the messages could not.

The fourth part is the one contractors skip, and it is where most of the gain between 80% and 95% lives. Texts remind people who were going to show up anyway. Calls recover the ones who were drifting.

## Sample confirmation and reminder messages

Keep every message short, specific, and easy to answer. Swap in your details.

**Confirmation, sent right after booking**

> Hi [First name], this is [Your name] with [Company]. You're booked for a free garage floor coating estimate on [Saturday, May 9] at [10:00 am] at [Address]. I'll measure the floor, check the concrete, and show you flake samples. Takes about 30 minutes. Reply YES so I know this landed.

**Reminder, 24 hours out**

> Hi [First name], quick reminder that I'll be at [Address] tomorrow at [10:00 am] for your garage floor estimate. Still good? If something came up, just tell me and we'll find another time.

**Reminder, 1 hour out**

> Morning [First name], [Your name] here. On track for [10:00 am] today. I'll be in a [white truck with the Company logo]. Please make sure the garage is open or someone can let me in.

There is no price, no pitch, and no "let us know if you have questions" in any of these. Each one names the appointment, gives one detail that makes it real, and asks for a reply. ## How to triage a homeowner who goes quiet

When a homeowner does not reply to the confirmation or the 24-hour reminder, call them, then text them, then offer a new time. Do not cancel.

1. **Call first.** A ringing phone gets attention a text does not. Say who you are, name the appointment, and ask one question: "Is tomorrow at 10 still good for you?" Most quiet homeowners answer this call and say yes.
2. **Text if the call goes to voicemail.** Leave a ten-second voicemail and send a text that says the same thing, with a way to answer in one word.
3. **Offer a reschedule, not a cancellation.** If the time no longer works, respond with two new slots: "No problem. I've got Tuesday at 4 or Thursday at 10. Which is better?" A homeowner who reschedules still wants the floor. A homeowner you cancel on is a lead you paid for and threw away.
4. **Set a cutoff.** If you have called twice and texted twice by the morning of the appointment and heard nothing, treat the slot as open and offer it to another lead. Keep the quiet homeowner in your follow-up list; a fair share come back a week later.

The person doing this does not need sales training. They need a list of tomorrow's appointments, a phone, and 20 minutes each afternoon.

## What to do the morning of an estimate

The morning of an estimate, send the 1-hour reminder, check for a reply, and call anyone who has been silent since booking. Then decide before you drive.

If the homeowner confirmed at booking and again at 24 hours, go. If they went quiet at 24 hours and have not answered a call this morning, call once more before you leave the shop. If you still get nothing, move the slot to the next lead on your list and text the quiet homeowner two new times.

Two habits help here. Ask at booking whether the garage will be clear enough to see the slab. And ask who else needs to be there. An estimate where both decision makers are home shows up more reliably and closes better, which is covered in [how to close more floor coating estimates](/blog/how-to-close-more-floor-coating-estimates).

## Why speed to lead at the front raises show rate at the back

Calling a lead within a minute of the form does more than book more estimates; it books estimates that hold. Our benchmark answer rate is 90% when the lead is called within one minute. A homeowner who talks to you while the form is still open on their phone books because they want the floor right now, and that memory sticks.

A lead called the next afternoon has talked to two other contractors, half forgotten your company name, and agreed to a time mostly to get you off the phone. That appointment is the one that no-shows. The [speed to lead post](/blog/speed-to-lead-wins-floor-coating-jobs) covers the front end; the point here is that the front end and the back end are the same lever.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good no-show rate for floor coating estimates?

A good no-show rate for floor coating estimates is about 5%, which is what a confirmation at booking, a reminder 24 hours out, a reminder 1 hour out, and a person calling quiet homeowners produces. Without any of that, no shows run about 20%. Anything between the two usually means one of the four parts is missing.

### Should I call or text to confirm a floor coating estimate?

Text for the routine confirmations and reminders, and call when a homeowner goes quiet. Texts are easy to answer and do not interrupt anyone, so they get replies from people who were already planning to show up. A call is what recovers the homeowner who stopped replying, because a ringing phone gets attention that a third text does not.

### What should I do if a homeowner no-shows a floor coating estimate?

Text the homeowner from the driveway, say you are there, and offer two new times. Do not send a frustrated message. A large share of no shows are simple mistakes, and a homeowner who feels embarrassed rather than scolded will often rebook on the spot. If you hear nothing, keep them in your follow-up sequence and move on.

### Does charging for an estimate reduce no shows on floor coating jobs?

Charging for an estimate reduces no shows but also reduces bookings, and in a market where competitors give free estimates it usually costs more jobs than it saves. A confirmation and reminder system gets you to a 5% no-show rate without adding friction at booking. Keep the estimate free and put the effort into the reminders and the calls.
