# Polyaspartic Floor Coating Leads: How to Sell the One Day Floor in Your Ads

**Meta Description:** How to get polyaspartic floor coating leads by selling the one day install: ad creative, targeting, qualifying premium buyers, pricing vs epoxy, and lead costs.

---

The best polyaspartic floor coating leads come from ads that sell the outcome, a finished garage floor in one day that will not yellow, rather than the chemistry. Per lead they cost about the same as other Meta coating leads (typically $15 to $45 on your own ads), but the one day offer attracts homeowners who expect to pay for a better floor, which raises your average ticket and keeps your crew on one-trip jobs. The work is in the creative, the qualifying call, and setting price expectations before you arrive.

## Key takeaways

- Homeowners do not search for "polyaspartic." They want their garage back fast, a floor that stays clear in sunlight, and no peeling. Sell that.
- On Meta, the offer and the creative do the targeting. A one day install ad pulls a different homeowner than a "cheapest epoxy in town" ad.
- Qualify polyaspartic buyers on timeline, sun exposure, garage use, and budget awareness, not just on homeownership.
- Full polyaspartic typically installs at about $7 to $12 per square foot versus $4 to $7 for standard epoxy. Never put a price in the ad. Set the range on the call.
- Only promise one day if your crew can deliver it on that slab. A broken promise on the first job is worse than a two day quote.

## What is a polyaspartic lead, and how is it different?

A polyaspartic lead is a homeowner who responded to an offer built around a polyaspartic system, usually a full flake floor with a polyaspartic topcoat or a full poly base and top. The difference from a generic floor coating lead is what they already believe when you call them.

Someone who clicked "garage floors starting at $X" is shopping on price. Someone who clicked "your garage floor, finished in one day, park on it tomorrow" has already accepted that this is a professional system with a real install. That changes the call, the estimate, and the close.

Most homeowners call every floor coating "epoxy," and so do plenty of contractors. That is fine. You do not need them to know the word. You need them to want what the system does. The full comparison of the two chemistries, and how to explain it at the kitchen table, is in [polyaspartic vs epoxy garage floors](/blog/polyaspartic-vs-epoxy-garage-floors).

## Why sell the one day install as the offer?

Because it is the benefit a homeowner can feel before they ever see a sample. "UV stable" means nothing to a homeowner. "We are in and out in one day and you can park on it tomorrow" means everything.

It also does three jobs for your business at once:

1. **It sets up a premium price.** The homeowner connects speed and quality with a better product, which makes the higher number at the estimate less of a surprise.
2. **It filters out the bargain hunter.** People looking for the cheapest coat of paint on a slab scroll past an offer built on speed and durability.
3. **It matches how your crew makes money.** One-trip jobs mean less drive time, no return visit, and more installs per week. Across a week, a one day system lets one crew run four or five jobs instead of two or three.

Polyaspartic's fast cure is not marketing fluff. In a [Painting and Coating Industry article](https://pcimag.com/articles/102451-curing-what-ails-concrete-floors-with-polyaspartic-technology) by a coatings director at Covestro, one of the companies behind the chemistry, polyaspartic coatings are described as typically curing in 1 to 4 hours, holding color and gloss under UV, and being applicable below 50 °F. The author works for a supplier, so treat it as the manufacturer side of the story, and follow your own product's data sheet for cure times and temperature limits.

## How does the one day offer change your ad creative?

On Meta the creative is the targeting. Our [benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) show that an ad which shows a garage floor and speaks to a homeowner about their garage attracts homeowners. Change the offer and you change who raises their hand.

| Element | Generic coating ad | One day polyaspartic ad |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hook | "Garage floors starting at a low price" | "Your garage floor, done in one day" |
| Main visual | Single glamour shot of a glossy floor | Morning before, evening after, same garage, same day |
| Proof | Star ratings and "best in town" | Time-lapse of a real install, car parked on it the next day |
| Pain point | Ugly floor | Stains, peeling DIY kit, yellowed old epoxy, losing the garage for days |
| Call to action | "Get a free quote" | "See if your garage qualifies for a one day install" |

A few rules that keep this honest and effective:

- **Use your own jobs.** Real garages from your area beat stock photos every time, and homeowners can tell the difference.
- **Show the clock.** A short video with the start time and finish time of an actual install is the most persuasive piece of proof you have.
- **Name the old floor problem.** "Tired of the peeling kit from the hardware store?" speaks to a homeowner who already tried the cheap option.
- **Avoid claims you cannot back up.** "Lifetime," "20 times stronger than epoxy," and similar lines need a written warranty or manufacturer documentation behind them. If you cannot hand it to the homeowner, leave it out.
- **Refresh before it fatigues.** The same before and after ad stops working after a few weeks in most markets. Rotate new jobs in.

## How does it change targeting?

Less than most people expect, because on Meta you are mostly targeting with the creative. A few things still matter:

- **Service radius.** Draw it around the neighborhoods you want to drive to for a one day job. A one-trip install that is 75 minutes away eats the advantage.
- **Season.** Polyaspartic's wider install window means you can keep running offers into the shoulder seasons, when epoxy-only competitors slow down. If your product and setup can coat in the cold, say so in the ad.
- **Home type.** Single family homes with attached garages are the core. If your creative keeps attracting condo owners with shared garages, the creative is the problem.
- **Search.** On Google, "polyaspartic" is a niche term in most markets. Check real volume in [Google's Keyword Planner](https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7337243) and compare relative interest in your state with [Google Trends](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533) (remember Trends shows relative interest on a 0 to 100 scale, not raw searches). Most of your polyaspartic buyers will search for "garage floor coating," not the chemistry.

## How should the landing page match the offer?

Promise and page should be the same thing. If the ad says one day, the first screen of the landing page should say one day, show a same-day before and after, and then ask a short set of questions:

1. Which surface? (Garage, basement, patio, other)
2. How big? (One, two, or three car)
3. What is on it now? (Bare concrete, old coating or paint, DIY kit)
4. When do you want it done?
5. Contact details last.

The answers set up the call. If they say "old coating that is peeling," your caller already knows to mention that removal is part of the job. A native lead form that auto fills in two taps skips all of this, which is why our benchmarks favor a landing page.

## How do you qualify a polyaspartic buyer?

The basics are the same as any coating lead: owns the home, inside your area, wants professional work soon. A premium system needs a few more questions, because the worst outcome is driving to a homeowner who loved the ad and was never going to spend what the floor costs.

| Question | What a good answer sounds like | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| How soon do you want it done? | "Next few weeks," "before the holidays" | One day matters most to people with a reason to move fast |
| Does the sun hit the floor? | Open door, west facing, half the car parks outside | UV stability is the strongest reason to pay more |
| How do you use the garage? | Daily parking, gym, workshop, hangout space | Heavy use justifies the better system |
| What is on the floor now? | Bare, or a failed coating they want gone | Sets expectations on prep and price |
| Do you have a budget in mind? | Accepts the range when it is explained | Filters the cheapest-quote shopper before the drive |
| Who else needs to be at the estimate? | Both decision makers can be there | Premium jobs rarely close with one spouse missing |

The budget question is where most callers get nervous. Give the range plainly: "Full polyaspartic floors like the ones in the ad usually run in this range for a two car garage. Does that fit what you had in mind?" A homeowner who says yes is a strong appointment. One who says "I was hoping for a few hundred dollars" just saved you an afternoon. ## How should you position price against epoxy?

Never advertise the number. Advertise the outcome, then set the range on the call and present options at the estimate, best first.

These are typical installed ranges published elsewhere on this site, not quotes for your market:

| System | Typical installed price per sq ft | Two car garage (about 500 sq ft) | Install time |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Standard epoxy system | About $4 to $7 | About $2,000 to $3,500 | Usually two days |
| Epoxy base, polyaspartic topcoat | About $6 to $9 | About $3,000 to $4,500 | Faster than full epoxy |
| Full polyaspartic (base and top) | About $7 to $12 | About $3,500 to $6,000 | One day for a trained crew |

Our [benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) show most markets blending out to $5 to $8 per square foot, with saturated markets near the low end. The job-level margin math, including materials and labor on a flake and polyaspartic garage, is in [floor coating pricing and margins](/blog/floor-coating-pricing-and-margins).

At the estimate, anchor with the full polyaspartic system, walk down to the hybrid, then the budget epoxy, and tell the truth about each one. The homeowner who came in through a one day ad usually picks one of the top two.

## What do polyaspartic leads cost?

The lead costs follow the channel, not the chemistry. The typical ranges we use across this site:

- **Your own Meta ads:** about $15 to $45 per lead
- **Google Ads:** about $100 to $300 per lead
- **Shared marketplaces:** about $30 to $120 per lead, sold to several contractors

A premium offer can raise your cost per lead a little, because fewer people respond to a floor that is clearly not the cheapest. That is usually a good trade. Here is a hypothetical month with $1,000 in ad spend, to show the math rather than to predict your numbers:

| | Price-led epoxy offer | One day polyaspartic offer |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cost per lead (hypothetical) | $25 | $35 |

Fewer leads, more revenue, and less than half the crew time. That last row is the one owners underestimate. ## When is a polyaspartic offer the wrong move?

Be honest with yourself on three points before you build campaigns around it:

- **Your crew cannot reliably finish in one day yet.** Full poly takes skill. If you are still learning the product, sell the hybrid and build up.
- **Your market only buys on price.** In a saturated market where every quote is compared to the lowest, you may need a good, better, best offer rather than a single premium one.
- **Lots of your slabs need heavy repair.** Bad cracking, moisture, or a failing old coating can turn a one day job into two. Qualify for it on the call and say so at the estimate.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I get more polyaspartic floor coating leads?

Run Meta ads that sell the one day install with real before and after footage from your own jobs, send traffic to a landing page that repeats the promise, and call every lead within a minute. Homeowners rarely search for "polyaspartic," so creating demand on Meta usually beats waiting for searches on Google.

### Should I advertise polyaspartic or epoxy?

Advertise the outcome, not either word. A one day floor that will not yellow or peel is what homeowners want. Then recommend the right system at the estimate, which for most garages is full polyaspartic or an epoxy base with a polyaspartic topcoat.

### Are polyaspartic leads more expensive than epoxy leads?

The cost per lead depends mainly on the channel and creative, not the chemistry: typically $15 to $45 on your own Meta ads. A premium offer can raise cost per lead slightly because fewer bargain shoppers respond, but higher tickets and one-trip installs usually make cost per installed floor and revenue per crew day better.

### Should I put a price in my polyaspartic ads?

No. A price in the ad turns the conversation into a comparison with every cheaper quote. Set a clear range on the qualifying call so nobody is surprised, then present options at the estimate starting with the best system.

### How do I qualify a homeowner for a polyaspartic floor?

Ask about timeline, sun exposure, how they use the garage, what is on the floor now, and whether the price range fits. Confirm both decision makers can be at the estimate. Those answers separate a premium buyer from someone who liked the ad and wants the cheapest option.
