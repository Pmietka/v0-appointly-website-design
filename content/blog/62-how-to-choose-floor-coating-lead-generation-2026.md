The best lead generation partner for a floor coating contractor in 2026 is one that gets paid only when a real homeowner is on your calendar, not when a form gets filled out. Most contractors pick based on price per lead and end up paying for names that never pick up the phone.

At Appointly, we have booked over 1,000 floor coating appointments for epoxy, polyaspartic and polyurea contractors. This guide covers the 4 questions to ask any lead provider, and how each lead generation model stacks up.

## The 4 questions to ask before you hire a lead provider

### 1. Am I paying for leads or booked appointments?

A lead is a name and a phone number. An appointment is a homeowner expecting you at their door at a set time.

If you pay per lead, your team still has to call, text, chase and book every one. The lead looks cheap because you are doing the expensive part.

### 2. Is this lead exclusive to me?

Shared lead sites like Angi, HomeAdvisor and Thumbtack often send the same homeowner to 3 or 4 contractors at once. That turns every estimate into a race to the bottom. Whoever calls first and quotes lowest wins, and you are cutting your price before you have even seen the floor.

Exclusive leads mean you are the only company that homeowner is talking to, so you can sell on quality instead of price.

### 3. Who calls the homeowner, and how fast?

Homeowners fill out quote forms at night, on weekends, and while you are on another job. Many request quotes from several garage floor companies on the same night, and the company that reaches them first usually books the estimate.

Most coating owners cannot win that race on their own:

- **You are on a job site.** Grinding, coating and flaking all day leaves no room to call back in minutes.
- **Leads come in after hours.** Facebook and Instagram ads keep running after the shop closes.
- **One call is not enough.** Many homeowners need several calls and texts before they answer.

What good looks like:

1. A call within 5 minutes of the lead coming in.
2. A text right after any missed call.
3. Follow up on a set cadence for at least a week.
4. A confirmation the day before and the hour before every appointment.

At Appointly, we call every homeowner the moment they come in, before they can reach other companies. We qualify them, book the estimate onto your calendar and confirm it, so you only show up, bid and close.

### 4. What happens when an appointment is bad?

A fair policy means you never pay for an appointment you could not bid. This is the question most lead providers hope you skip, so get the answer in writing before you sign.

**Ask any provider to define these 4 things:**

- **What counts as a bad appointment.** Wrong service, not the homeowner, no real timeline, or outside your service area.
- **Who decides.** It should be you, because you were standing in the garage.
- **How it gets credited.** A credit you can see on your next invoice, not a promise on a call.
- **What happens with no shows.** Some providers bill you anyway. Ask how they handle it.

**How Appointly handles it:**

- **We qualify on intent first.** Before an estimate hits your calendar, we confirm the project goal, the look the homeowner wants and the material. No real timeline means we cancel it before you ever see it.
- **You grade every appointment.** After the estimate, you or your team grade it in the Appointly app with notes.
- **Grade it a C and you don't pay.** Unqualified homeowners, or a service you don't offer, are not billed.
- **No shows earn a credit.** Billing happens on booking, and a no show credits you toward your next appointment.

This guarantee came from running the business, not from a trend. You were at the estimate, so you know better than anyone whether it was real.

| Model | Examples | What you pay for | Who calls the lead | Exclusive to you |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lead marketplaces | Angi, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack | Each lead | You | Usually shared |
| Google Local Services Ads | Google LSA | Each lead | You | Yes |
| Retainer marketing agency | Local SEO and ads agencies | A monthly fee | You | Yes |
| Pay per lead Meta agency | Facebook and Instagram lead agencies | Each lead | You | Yes |
| Appointment booking agency | Appointly | Each booked estimate | The agency | Yes |

- **Lead marketplaces** send shared leads, so you race other crews before you even quote.
- **Google Local Services Ads** send exclusive calls from people already searching, but volume is capped by search demand in your area.
- **Retainer agencies** charge the same fee whether you get 2 estimates or 20. All the risk sits with you.
- **Pay per lead Meta agencies** hand you names and phone numbers. Your team still has to book every one.
- **Appointment booking agencies** run the ads, call the homeowner, qualify them and book the estimate. You only show up, bid and close.

## 3 more questions worth asking

1. **Who owns the ad spend and how is it reported?** You should see exactly what was spent, every week, with no markup.
2. **Is there a long term contract?** A partner confident in results should not need to lock you in.
3. **Can they show real numbers from contractors like you?** Ask for jobs closed and revenue, not impressions or clicks.
