# Floor Coating Lead to Appointment Rate: What Is Good and How to Raise It

**Meta Description:** Lead to appointment rate for floor coating contractors should be 60 to 70%. How to calculate it, why a low rate is a speed or creative problem, and the fixes.

---

Lead to appointment rate is the share of your leads that become a booked estimate with a date and time on your calendar. For floor coating contractors the benchmark is 60 to 70%. If 100 homeowners submit a form and 60 to 70 of them end up booked, the front of your funnel is healthy. A rate well below 60% points to speed or creative, not the market.

## Key takeaways

- Lead to appointment rate should run 60 to 70% for floor coating contractors, and you calculate it as booked estimates divided by leads received over the same 30-day window.
- A rate well below 60% is nearly always a speed to lead problem or a creative problem, so check answer rate first and homeowner share second.
- Calling every lead within one minute produces a 90% answer rate, and no other single change moves the booking rate as far.
- On an illustrative 50 leads a month, 40% books 20 estimates and 65% books about 32, which is about 12 more estimates on the same ad spend.
- Adding qualifying questions to the form, pausing ads, or blaming the market all make the rate worse, not better.

## How do you calculate lead to appointment rate?

Lead to appointment rate is booked estimates divided by leads received, over the same window, expressed as a percentage. If 40 homeowners submitted a form in June and 26 of them had an estimate booked on the calendar, the rate is 26 divided by 40, or 65%.

Three rules keep the number honest. First, count every lead that came in, including the ones you never reached and the ones you decided were not worth calling. Cutting "bad" leads out of the denominator hides the exact problem you are trying to find. Second, count a lead as booked only when there is a specific date and time on the calendar. "Said they'd call back" is not booked. Third, use a 30-day window at minimum, because leads booked on day three still count and a week of data is too small to say anything.

Run the calculation monthly, and split it by lead source if you have more than one. A 68% rate on Meta leads and a 35% rate on marketplace leads is two different problems that a blended 55% would hide.

## Why is my lead to appointment rate below 60%?

A rate well below 60% is a creative problem or a speed problem, not a market problem. Homeowners in every market fill out forms because they want their garage floor done. If fewer than six in ten book, either the wrong people are filling out the form or the right people are not being reached in time. The table below is the diagnostic.

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Leads rarely pick up, and you leave a lot of voicemails | You are calling too late, often hours after the form | Call within one minute, every lead, including nights and weekends |
| Leads pick up but say they already booked someone | Same as above; a competitor got there first | Same fix; speed to lead decides who the homeowner "chose" |
| Leads say they did not fill anything out, or are renters | The creative is attracting the wrong audience | Change the creative to speak to homeowners about their own garage |
| Leads are curious but not ready, "just looking" | The native lead form is too easy to submit | Send traffic to a landing page instead of the in-app form |
| Leads pick up, are qualified, but do not book | The caller is chatting instead of offering two specific times | Fix the call script; offer two slots and ask who else should attend |
| Rate is fine on weekdays and collapses on weekends | Nobody is on the phones when homeowners submit forms | Cover Saturday and Sunday; that is when a lot of forms come in |

Work the table from the top. Answer rate is the first thing to check because it is the easiest to measure and the biggest lever. If your answer rate is already high and the rate is still low, look at who is answering.

## What are the three levers that raise lead to appointment rate?

The three levers, in order of impact, are speed to lead, creative that attracts homeowners, and a landing page instead of the native lead form. Pull them in that order. Each one is on our [floor coating benchmarks page](/floor-coating-benchmarks), and every other number on that page moves when speed to lead moves.

### Lever 1: speed to lead

Calling the lead within one minute of the form produces a 90% answer rate. That is the single most important number in this whole discussion. A homeowner who just hit submit is sitting with the phone in hand, thinking about the floor, and picks up a number they do not recognize because they are expecting it. The same homeowner four hours later is at dinner, has talked to another contractor, and lets it ring.

A 90% answer rate does not mean a 90% booking rate, but it makes 60 to 70% reachable, because the person on the phone is warm and the call is short: confirm the property, confirm the project, offer two times, book one. Why the first caller wins is covered in the [speed to lead post](/blog/speed-to-lead-wins-floor-coating-jobs). The practical requirement is someone whose job is the phone, every day, including the Saturday afternoon when half the week's forms arrive.

### Lever 2: creative that attracts homeowners

With the right creative, 100% of your leads are homeowners. On Meta the creative is the targeting. The ad, not the audience settings, decides who stops scrolling and fills out the form. An ad that shows a real two-car garage in a neighborhood like the viewer's, with a real before and after and a plain offer of a free estimate, pulls homeowners. An ad that looks like a stock photo and a coupon pulls renters and people who tapped by accident.

If your callers keep hearing "I rent" or "I don't remember filling that out," the fix is not more qualifying questions. It is a different ad. Building creative that pulls the right person is covered in [Meta ads for floor coating contractors](/blog/meta-ads-for-floor-coating-contractors).

### Lever 3: a landing page instead of the native lead form

A landing page produces better lead quality than the native Meta lead form. The in-app form pre-fills the homeowner's name and number and submits in two taps, which is convenient and also why so many of those leads do not remember submitting. A landing page requires the homeowner to leave the feed, read a page about your floors, and type their details. That small effort filters for intent.

The trade is fewer leads at a higher cost each, and a much larger share of them that answer and book. Since you are measuring lead to appointment rate, not lead count, that is the trade you want.

## What not to do when your lead to appointment rate is low

Do not add qualifying questions to the form, do not blame the market, and do not pause the ads. All three feel like action and all three make the number worse.

**Adding qualifying questions.** Every extra field on the form cuts submissions, and it does not cut the people you were hoping to filter. Homeowners with real intent get impatient; people tapping by accident do not notice. Qualify on the phone in the first 30 seconds instead, where it costs nothing.

**Blaming the market.** "Nobody in this area is buying right now" is what a 35% rate sounds like from the inside. The homeowners are filling out the form, and that is the proof the market exists. The gap between the form and the calendar is in your process.

**Pausing ads.** Pausing to "fix things first" resets the algorithm's learning, stops the flow of leads you need to test the fix against, and usually means the fix never happens. Keep the ads running at a steady budget, change one thing at a time, and watch the rate for two weeks.

Rates above that are possible with fast calling and strong creative. Rates well below 60% point to a speed to lead problem or a creative problem rather than the market.

### Why do so many of my floor coating leads never answer the phone?

Most leads that never answer were called too late. Called within one minute of the form, about 90% of homeowners pick up, because they are still holding the phone and expecting the call. Called hours later, the same homeowner is busy, has talked to a competitor, or does not recognize the number. Speed is the fix, not a different voicemail.

### Does a landing page really beat the Meta lead form for floor coating leads?

Yes, on lead quality. The native lead form pre-fills the homeowner's details and submits in two taps, so a share of those leads do not remember submitting and never answer. A landing page makes the homeowner read about your floors and type their details, which filters for intent. You get fewer leads and a higher share of them that book.
