# Garage Floor Coating Leads: What They Cost and Where They Actually Come From

**Meta Description:** What garage floor coating leads cost from marketplaces, Google, Meta ads, and booked-appointment services, and which source actually turns into installed floors.

---

Garage floor coating leads cost roughly $30 to $120 each from shared marketplaces, $100 to $300 from Google Ads, and $15 to $45 from your own Meta ads, with wide swings by market. Cost per lead is the wrong number, though. Judge a source by its cost per installed floor, which depends on exclusivity, speed to lead, and whether 60 to 70% of leads become booked estimates.

## Key takeaways

- Shared marketplace leads go to three to five contractors at once, and contractors who track it honestly close 5 to 12 percent of them.
- Meta ads are the most scalable source, and with a call inside one minute, 60 to 70 percent of leads should become booked estimates.
- Compare sources on cost per installed floor, not cost per lead; a $50 shared lead at an 8 percent close costs about $625 per job.
- A booked-appointment service charges per confirmed estimate and nothing for leads that never book, which changes the denominator from phone numbers to appointments.

## What are you actually buying when you buy a lead?

A garage floor coating lead is a homeowner's name and phone number attached to some signal of interest. That signal ranges from a phone number typed into an ad to a confirmed Tuesday at 4 pm estimate with your company. Those are not the same product, and pricing them as if they were is how contractors overpay.

Sort every lead source by one question: how far along is the homeowner when you get them, and is anyone else getting them too? The ranges below are generic industry figures and published marketplace rates, not Appointly results.

## How much do shared lead marketplaces cost?

Shared marketplace leads run $30 to $120 each and are sold to several contractors at once. Angi, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack, and the niche lead resellers all work the same way. A homeowner fills out a form describing a garage floor project, and the marketplace sells that form to three to five contractors at the same time.

**What it costs:** $30 to $60 per shared lead for basic concrete coating requests, and $40 to $120 for larger or more specific garage floor projects, sometimes plus a setup fee in the low thousands.

**What you actually get:** A homeowner who is about to receive five phone calls. The first contractor to reach them usually gets the estimate. The other four paid for nothing.

**Close rate:** Contractors who track it honestly report closing 5 to 12 percent of shared leads. At $50 a lead and an 8 percent close rate, you are paying roughly $625 in lead cost per installed floor before counting the hours spent chasing. It only works if a dedicated person calls every lead back within two minutes, including evenings.

## How much do Google Ads and Google Business Profile leads cost?

A paid search lead for garage floor coating lands at $100 to $300 in most metros. A homeowner who types "garage floor coating near me" is as high intent as it gets. The problem is volume and cost, not quality.

**What it costs:** Google Ads clicks for garage floor coating terms commonly run $8 to $25, and it takes 8 to 15 clicks to produce one form fill or call. Organic clicks from your Google Business Profile are free, but every coating franchise in your area is competing for the three map spots.

**What you actually get:** Exclusive, high-intent leads who are also calling two or three other companies the same afternoon.

**Close rate:** 20 to 35 percent for contractors who answer the phone live. Much lower if the call goes to voicemail.

**When it works:** As a supplement. Search demand is capped by how many people are searching in a given week, and in a smaller market the well runs dry fast. Your [Google Business Profile and reviews](/blog/google-reviews-floor-coating-contractors) matter here more than ad budget.

## How much do your own Meta ads cost per lead?

Coating contractors running their own Meta ads with good creative and a short form typically see $15 to $45 per lead. Instead of waiting for someone to search, you show a homeowner a before-and-after of a garage in their town and ask if they want a free estimate. That creates demand, which is why it scales.

**What it costs:** $15 to $45 per lead with good creative and a landing page.

**What you actually get:** Exclusive leads, but earlier in the decision. Our [floor coating benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) show that when the lead is called within one minute, about 90 percent answer and 60 to 70 percent of leads should become a booked estimate. Called an hour later from a jobsite, far fewer answer, and the rest need texts and follow-up over several days.

**Close rate:** On the estimates you book, 40 to 60 percent close if the [estimate is run well](/blog/how-to-close-more-floor-coating-estimates). From raw lead to installed floor, contractors running their own ads usually land between 10 and 20 percent, because the follow-up falls apart while they are on a job.

**When it works:** When someone owns the phone. Meta ads are the source that punishes slow [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-wins-floor-coating-jobs) the hardest. We wrote a full guide to [Meta ads for floor coating contractors](/blog/meta-ads-for-floor-coating-contractors) and a breakdown of what a healthy [lead to appointment rate](/blog/floor-coating-lead-to-appointment-rate) looks like.

## What are the red flags when buying garage floor coating leads?

The biggest red flag is a provider whose job ends when the form is submitted.

- **"Exclusive" with an asterisk.** Ask how many contractors receive each lead and what happens if the homeowner submitted the same form on two sites.
- **Setup fees that dwarf the lead cost.** A $2,000 to $4,000 setup fee is a subscription in disguise.
- **No refund policy for bad contact info.** Wrong numbers, renters, and out-of-area addresses should never be billable.
- **Nobody calls the lead but you.** If the provider's job ends when the form is submitted, you are buying a to-do list.

### How much does a garage floor coating lead cost?

Shared leads from marketplaces like Angi and HomeAdvisor are worth it only if someone calls every lead within two minutes, including evenings. Each lead goes to three to five contractors, and the first to reach the homeowner usually gets the estimate. ### What percentage of floor coating leads should become booked estimates?

About 60 to 70 percent of floor coating leads should become booked estimates when the lead is called within one minute of submitting the form. Answer rates run near 90 percent at that speed. Contractors who call back an hour later from a jobsite book far fewer, which is why the same Meta campaign can look great for one company and useless for another.

### Is it cheaper to run my own Meta ads or buy booked appointments?

Running your own Meta ads is usually cheaper per lead, but only cheaper per installed floor if you can call every lead within a minute and follow up for days. Most owner-operators cannot do that from a jobsite, so 10 to 20 percent of leads become floors. A booked-appointment service charges per confirmed estimate, which removes the phone work and the leads that never book.
