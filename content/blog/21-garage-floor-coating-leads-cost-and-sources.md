# Garage Floor Coating Leads: What They Cost and Where They Actually Come From

**Meta Description:** What garage floor coating leads cost from marketplaces, Google, Meta ads, and booked-appointment services, and which source actually turns into installed floors.

---

## Every Coating Contractor Asks the Same Question

"Where do I get more garage floor coating leads?" is the question behind almost every conversation we have with a coating contractor. It is the wrong question, but it is the one everyone starts with, so let's answer it properly. Below is what each lead source actually costs, what a lead from that source is worth, and why the contractors who grow fastest stop buying leads and start buying booked estimates.

The numbers here come from running Meta campaigns for coating contractors in markets like Portland, Port St. Lucie, and Chicago, and from the rates lead marketplaces publish. Your market will move the figures up or down, but the ranking of the sources rarely changes.

## First, Define What You Are Buying

A garage floor coating lead is a homeowner's name and phone number attached to some signal of interest. That signal ranges from "clicked an ad and typed a phone number" to "requested three quotes on a marketplace" to "confirmed a Tuesday at 4 pm estimate with your company." Those are not the same product, and pricing them as if they were is how contractors overpay.

Sort every lead source by one question: **how far along is the homeowner when you get them, and is anyone else getting them too?**

## Source 1: Shared Lead Marketplaces

Angi, HomeAdvisor, Thumbtack, and the niche lead resellers all work the same way. A homeowner fills out a form describing a garage floor project, and the marketplace sells that form to three to five contractors at the same time.

**What it costs:** $30 to $60 per shared lead for basic concrete coating requests, and $40 to $120 for larger or more specific garage floor projects. Some niche resellers quote $41 to $99 per "qualified" lead plus a setup fee in the low thousands.

**What you actually get:** A homeowner who is about to receive five phone calls. The first contractor to reach them usually gets the estimate. The other four paid for nothing.

**Close rate:** Contractors who track it honestly report closing 5 to 12 percent of shared leads. At $50 a lead and an 8 percent close rate, you are paying roughly $625 in lead cost per installed floor before you ever count the hours spent chasing.

**When it works:** When you have a dedicated person calling leads back within two minutes, every time, including evenings. Most owner-operators cannot do that from the back of a truck, which is why marketplaces feel like a slot machine.

## Source 2: Google Search and Your Google Business Profile

A homeowner who types "garage floor coating near me" is as high intent as it gets. The problem is volume and cost, not quality.

**What it costs:** Google Ads clicks for garage floor coating terms commonly run $8 to $25, and it takes 8 to 15 clicks to produce one form fill or call. That puts a paid search lead at $100 to $300 in most metros. Organic clicks from your Google Business Profile are free, but you are competing with every coating franchise in your area for the three map spots.

**What you actually get:** Exclusive leads with strong intent. These homeowners are actively shopping, which also means they are calling two or three other companies the same afternoon.

**Close rate:** 20 to 35 percent for contractors who answer the phone live. Much lower if the call goes to voicemail.

**When it works:** As a supplement. Search demand for garage floor coating is capped by how many people are searching in a given week. You cannot scale it just by spending more, and in a smaller market the well runs dry fast. Your [Google Business Profile and reviews](/blog/google-reviews-floor-coating-contractors) matter here more than ad budget.

## Source 3: Running Your Own Meta Ads

Facebook and Instagram ads work differently. Instead of waiting for someone to search, you show a homeowner a before-and-after of a garage in their town and ask if they want a free estimate. This creates demand instead of harvesting it, which is why it scales.

**What it costs:** With good creative and a short lead form, coating contractors typically see $15 to $45 per lead. Poor creative or a long form pushes that past $80.

**What you actually get:** Exclusive leads, but earlier in the decision. Roughly half never answer the phone, and a good share of the rest are "just curious about pricing." Every one of them needs a fast call, a text, and follow-up over several days to become an estimate.

**Close rate:** On the leads you actually reach and book, 40 to 60 percent close if the [estimate is run well](/blog/how-to-close-more-floor-coating-estimates). But from raw lead to installed floor, contractors running their own ads usually land between 10 and 20 percent, because the follow-up falls apart while they are on a job.

**When it works:** When someone owns the phone. Meta ads are the highest-volume, most scalable source of garage floor coating leads in the country. They are also the source that punishes slow [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-wins-floor-coating-jobs) the hardest. ## The Comparison That Matters: Cost Per Installed Floor

Lead cost is a distraction. The number that decides whether a source is worth it is what you pay per job that actually gets installed. Using the mid-tier $3,500 garage floor we use throughout this site and the ranges above:

- **Shared marketplace lead:** $50 per lead, 8 percent close. About $625 in acquisition cost per job, plus dozens of unpaid calls.
- **Google Ads lead:** $175 per lead, 25 percent close. About $700 per job, exclusive, but limited volume.
- **Your own Meta ads:** $30 per lead, 15 percent close. About $200 per job, plus whoever is answering the phone all day.
- **Booked appointment:** One appointment fee per estimate, 50 percent close. Two appointment fees per job, and zero hours on the phone.

On a $3,500 job with a gross profit around $1,750 to $2,050, every one of these sources can be profitable. The difference is how much of your week each one eats and how predictable the volume is. The math on what a [booked estimate is worth](/blog/what-is-a-booked-floor-coating-estimate-worth) goes deeper on this.

## Red Flags When Buying Garage Floor Coating Leads

- **"Exclusive" with an asterisk.** Ask how many contractors receive each lead and what happens if the homeowner submitted the same form on two sites.
- **Setup fees that dwarf the lead cost.** A $2,000 to $4,000 setup fee is a retainer wearing a costume.
- **No refund policy for bad contact info.** Wrong numbers, renters, and out-of-area addresses should never be billable.
- **Nobody calls the lead but you.** If the provider's job ends when the form is submitted, you are buying a to-do list.
