# Best CRM for Floor Coating Contractors: What You Actually Need and Which Type Fits Your Shop

**Meta Description:** How to pick the best CRM for a floor coating or epoxy flooring business: the seven features that matter and how GoHighLevel, Jobber, and others compare.

---

The best CRM for a floor coating contractor is the one that texts every new lead within a minute, books the estimate straight onto your calendar, sends the quote and follows up on it without you remembering to, and asks for a review when the floor is done. For most one and two crew shops, that means either an all-in-one marketing CRM such as GoHighLevel or a field service platform such as Jobber or Housecall Pro. Larger multi-crew operations look at heavier platforms like ServiceTitan, and project-style shops sometimes fit JobNimbus.

**Disclosure:** Appointly Solutions uses GoHighLevel in its own operations. Every tool here is described by what its own website says, so confirm features and pricing with each vendor.

**How we wrote this:** every feature listed for a named tool comes from that company's own website as of October 2026. Software changes quickly, plans get renamed, and features move between tiers, so confirm anything that matters to you directly with the vendor before you sign up.

## Key takeaways

- A coating shop's CRM has one main job: make sure no lead, estimate, or open quote gets forgotten.
- Seven features matter most: speed to lead automation, two way texting, calendar booking, estimates, follow-up sequences, job photos, and review requests.
- Marketing CRMs are strongest before the sale. Field service tools are strongest after it. Pick based on where your business leaks.
- A solo owner-operator needs fewer features and more automation. A multi-crew shop needs dispatch, permissions, and reporting.
- No CRM answers the phone for you. The fastest automation in the world still needs a person to call the homeowner and book the estimate.

## What does a floor coating business actually need from a CRM?

Floor coating sales are simple on paper: a homeowner fills out a form, someone calls, an estimator measures the garage, a quote goes out, and the crew installs in a day. The money leaks between those steps. A CRM earns its cost when it plugs those leaks.

| Need | Why it matters in floor coating | What to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Speed to lead automation | Called within one minute, about 90% of homeowners answer. Hours later, many have booked someone else | Instant text on form submit, alert to whoever is on call, missed call text back |
| Two way texting | Many homeowners will not answer an unknown number but will reply to a text | Texts from a business number, every thread in one inbox, the whole team can see history |
| Calendar booking | Estimates need to fit the estimator's route, not just an open slot | Booking into your real calendar, confirmations, 24 hour and 1 hour reminders |
| Estimates and quotes | Good, better, best options raise the ticket | Multiple options on one quote, photos on the quote, e-signature or online approval, deposits |
| Follow-up sequences | Most unsold estimates are "not yet," not "no" | Automated texts and tasks over 30 days, stop when the homeowner replies or signs |
| Job photos | Before-and-afters are your best ads and your best sales tool | Photos attached to the job, easy to pull for ads and quotes |
| Review requests | Reviews drive both Google and the homeowner's decision | Automatic request when the job closes or the invoice is paid |

The first two rows matter more than the rest combined. Our [floor coating benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) show about 90% of homeowners answer when the call goes out inside a minute, and show rates move from about 80% with no reminders to about 95% with a confirmation, 24 hour and 1 hour reminders, and a person triaging. A CRM that automates the text and the reminders protects both numbers.

## What are the main types of CRM for coating contractors?

The tools contractors call a "CRM" fall into four groups. They overlap, but each is built around a different part of the job.

| Type | Built around | Strong at | Weaker at | Example tools |

| All-in-one marketing CRM | Leads and marketing | Instant texting, pipelines, automations, landing pages, review requests | Job costing, crew scheduling, field work | GoHighLevel |

## How do the well known tools compare?

Here is what each company says it offers on its own website, and where that lines up with a coating shop's needs. This is not a ranking. Each tool is good at what it was built for.

### GoHighLevel

**What it says it offers:** [GoHighLevel](https://www.gohighlevel.com/) describes itself as an "AI-powered business operating system." Its site lists a CRM with sales pipelines, a single conversation inbox for SMS, Messenger, Instagram, WhatsApp, and live chat, calendars with appointment reminders, workflow automations, missed call text back, reputation management with automated review requests, estimates and invoicing, websites, funnels, and landing pages, and AI tools for voice and conversation.

**Where it fits floor coating:** The front end. Instant texts on form submit, automated follow-up sequences, calendar booking with reminders, and review requests are exactly what this kind of tool is built for, and you can host your landing page in the same system.

**What to check:** Its [pricing page](https://www.gohighlevel.com/pricing) lists the Starter plan at $97 a month and the Unlimited plan at $297 a month, as listed on their site in October 2026, with a 14 day trial. The same page notes that usage based charges apply for things like phone numbers, text messages, emails, and premium AI. The plans are described as built for freelancers, marketers, and agencies, so expect to spend real time building pipelines and workflows yourself, or pay someone to.

### Jobber

**What it says it offers:** [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/features/) calls itself an all-in-one system for service pros. Its features page lists online booking, two way text messaging, automated quote follow-ups, automated visit and invoice reminders, a client hub where customers approve quotes and pay invoices, job details with photos, forms, and checklists, and automatic Google review requests. Reviews, email marketing, and an AI receptionist are listed as add-ons on most plans.

**Where it fits floor coating:** The back end, and a solid share of the front end. Quote follow-ups, the client hub, and job photos line up well with how coating estimates and installs run.

**What to check:** Jobber publishes plan prices on its pricing page and offers a 14 day trial. Check which plan includes the automations and add-ons you need, because those are where the cost moves.

### Housecall Pro

**What it says it offers:** [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/) says it is tailored for teams of 1 to 100+. Its site lists estimates and a sales proposal tool, scheduling and dispatching, online booking, automated follow-ups, review management, a jobsite photo app, consumer financing, payments and invoicing, and an AI customer service rep that answers calls and books jobs.

**Where it fits floor coating:** Similar ground to Jobber. Estimates with options, job photos, review management, and financing are all relevant to selling a $3,500 to $5,000 floor.

**What to check:** Housecall Pro lists its plan prices on its pricing page. Some features, including its sales pipeline, are listed as add-ons, so price the plan you would actually run, not the entry tier.

### JobNimbus

**What it says it offers:** [JobNimbus](https://www.jobnimbus.com/) describes itself as an all-in-one platform for roofing contractors, and lists concrete, decking and patio, painting, and remodeling among the industries it serves. Its site lists a contractor CRM, boards that show where each job stands, estimates, photo management through a CompanyCam integration, automations, and mobile apps.

**Where it fits floor coating:** Shops that run longer projects, commercial work, or multi-step jobs where a visual board helps. Built-in estimates and photo documentation are useful for coating.

**What to check:** Its pricing page shows "Request pricing" rather than public prices. The same page lists limits on active automations by plan, and describes its two way texting tool as part of a communications add-on. For a high volume residential shop, confirm how fast and how automated the first text to a new lead can be.

### ServiceTitan

**What it says it offers:** [ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/) calls itself software for commercial and residential trades. Its site lists call booking, dispatch, marketing, and reporting, with add-ons such as Pricebook Pro and Field Pro. The residential trades it names include HVAC, plumbing, electrical, garage door, and painting.

**Where it fits floor coating:** Larger operations with several crews, a call center or office staff, and a need for detailed reporting across technicians and locations.

**What to check:** Its pricing page lists packages with "Request Pricing" instead of public prices. For a one or two crew coating shop, ask how long setup takes and whether the features you would actually use justify the cost.

## Which CRM fits your shop size?

| Shop | What matters most | Type to look at first | Watch out for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Solo owner-operator, on the grinder most days | Instant text to new leads, missed call text back, reminders, simple quotes | All-in-one marketing CRM, or a field service tool with strong texting | Buying a big system you never finish setting up |
| One crew plus an office person or setter | Shared inbox, calendar booking, quote follow-ups, review requests | Field service tool, or marketing CRM plus a field service tool | Two systems that do not talk, so leads get typed in twice |
| Two to three crews | Scheduling across crews, job photos, permissions, estimator reporting | Field service tool with a marketing CRM in front if lead volume is high | Automations nobody owns once the person who built them leaves |
| Four or more crews, or several locations | Dispatch, reporting by estimator and location, call booking | Enterprise trades platform or a fully built out field service tool | Long setup and paying for modules you do not use |

A common setup for a growing coating shop is a marketing CRM for leads and follow-up, and a field service tool for quotes, scheduling, and invoicing once the homeowner signs. That works well when the two are connected so a booked estimate moves across automatically. If you have to retype every sold job, start with one tool and add the second only when it clearly pays for itself.

## How should you set up a CRM for floor coating?

Whatever you pick, build it around the steps of a coating sale.

**Pipeline stages:**

1. New lead
2. Contacted
3. Estimate booked
4. Estimate shown
5. Quote sent
6. Sold
7. Installed
8. Review requested
9. Not now (seasonal list)
10. Lost, with a reason

**Automations worth building first:**

- **Instant text on every new lead,** sent within seconds, with the call following right behind it. The wording is in our [floor coating lead follow-up scripts](/blog/floor-coating-lead-follow-up-scripts).
- **Confirmation and reminders** when an estimate is booked: a confirmation right away, then 24 hours and 1 hour before. This is the cheapest fix for [estimate no-shows](/blog/floor-coating-estimate-no-shows).
- **A 30 day follow-up sequence** for every quote that does not sign on the spot, with a reason-lost tag. Our plan for [following up on unsold floor coating estimates](/blog/follow-up-unsold-floor-coating-estimates) lays out the days and the texts.
- **Seasonal messages** to the "not now" list at the start of spring and fall.
- **A review request** the day the floor is finished, while the homeowner is still excited about it.

**Reports to check every week:** leads in, speed to lead, lead to estimate rate, show rate, close rate, and revenue per booked estimate. Those are the numbers in our [floor coating contractor KPIs](/blog/floor-coating-contractor-kpis) scoreboard, and a CRM set up with the stages above will give you all of them.

## What will a CRM not fix?

A CRM is a set of rails. It does not drive the train.

- **It does not call the homeowner.** An instant text helps, but most estimates get booked on a live phone call. Several tools now sell AI receptionists or AI call answering. Test them with your own leads before you trust them with your ad spend.
- **It does not fix a bad lead source.** If the leads are renters and do it yourselfers, faster texting just reaches them sooner.
- **It does not run itself.** Automations need an owner. Someone has to read the replies, move cards, and fix the sequence when it breaks.

If the honest answer to "who calls the lead within a minute" is "me, when I get off the job," the CRM is not your bottleneck. Staffing is. Our comparison of an [in-house appointment setter vs outsourced](/blog/in-house-appointment-setter-vs-outsourced) walks through those options.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best CRM for a small epoxy flooring company?

For a small epoxy flooring company, the best CRM is the simplest one that sends an instant text to new leads, books estimates with reminders, and follows up on open quotes automatically. An all-in-one marketing CRM like GoHighLevel or a field service tool like Jobber or Housecall Pro usually covers that. Pick the one you will actually finish setting up.

### Is GoHighLevel good for floor coating contractors?

GoHighLevel's site lists the features coating contractors use most before the sale: instant texting, pipelines, automations, calendars with reminders, landing pages, and review requests. Its plans are described as built for freelancers, marketers, and agencies, so expect a learning curve and setup time if you build it yourself. Many shops pair it with a field service tool for scheduling and invoicing.

### Do I need a CRM if I only run one crew?

Yes, even a simple one. One crew can still produce dozens of leads and estimates a month, and the open quotes and seasonal follow-ups are where the easy money hides. Without a system, they live in your text messages and get forgotten. Start with lead texting, reminders, and quote follow-up, and add more later.

### Should a floor coating company use Jobber or Housecall Pro?

Both list online booking, quotes, scheduling, job photos, reviews, and automated follow-ups on their websites, and both publish plan prices. The right one depends on how the quotes look to your homeowners, which features sit in the plan you can afford, and how easily it connects to wherever your leads come in. Take a free trial of each and build one real quote with your good, better, best options.

### What CRM features matter most for speed to lead?

An instant automated text when a form is submitted, an alert to whoever is on call, missed call text back, and one shared inbox so nobody replies twice or not at all. Those features matter because answer rates drop quickly after the first minute. Make sure business texting is properly registered with carriers, or your texts may not be delivered.
