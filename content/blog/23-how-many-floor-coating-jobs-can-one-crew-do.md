# How Many Floor Coating Jobs Can One Crew Do in a Month? (And How Many Estimates It Takes)

**Meta Description:** Capacity math for floor coating contractors: how many garage floors one crew installs a month, how many estimates that takes, and how to size marketing to it.

---

## The Question That Should Drive Your Marketing Budget

Most coating contractors size their marketing by what they can afford. The better way is to size it by what the crew can install. If a two-person crew can put down 12 garage floors a month, you need enough booked estimates to produce 12 signed jobs, and not much more. Anything past that is either a second crew or wasted spend.

This article works through the capacity math from the install day backward to the number of estimates you should be booking. The figures come from the coating crews we book for and from the pricing model we use across this site.

## Start With Install Days

A full flake [polyaspartic](/blog/polyaspartic-vs-epoxy-garage-floors) system on a standard two-car garage is a one-day install for a trained two-person crew: grind and prep in the morning, base coat and flake broadcast by midday, scrape and topcoat in the afternoon. Epoxy base coats add an overnight cure and turn it into a two-day job, which is the single biggest reason polyaspartic crews out-earn epoxy crews.

Working backward from a month:

- **Available workdays:** 20 to 22 weekdays, minus weather, holidays, and the days you spend on estimates.
- **Estimate days:** Most single-crew owners block one or two days a week for estimates and quoting, or run estimates in the late afternoon after an install.
- **Realistic install days:** 12 to 16 per month for a crew that is booked solid.

At one garage per install day, a one-crew coating business tops out around **12 to 16 garage floors per month** in season. Contractors who stack a small basement or patio onto a light day push a bit higher, but 12 to 16 is the honest planning number.

## Turn Install Days Into Revenue

Using the mid-tier $3,500 two-car garage:

- **12 floors per month:** $42,000 in revenue
- **16 floors per month:** $56,000 in revenue

With material, labor, and consumables at roughly $1,450 to $1,750 per job, that is **$21,000 to $32,000 in gross profit per month** for one crew running at capacity. Our [pricing and margins guide](/blog/floor-coating-pricing-and-margins) breaks the per-job numbers down line by line.

That is the ceiling. Most crews run well under it, not because they cannot install, but because the calendar has gaps.

## Now Work Backward to Estimates

Here is where the math gets useful. To install 12 floors, you need to close 12 estimates. How many estimates that takes depends entirely on close rate:

| Close rate | Estimates needed for 12 jobs | Estimates needed for 16 jobs |
|---|---|---|
| 30 percent | 40 | 53 |
| 40 percent | 30 | 40 |
| 50 percent | 24 | 32 |
| 60 percent | 20 | 27 |

A crew closing 30 percent needs twice as many estimates as a crew closing 60 percent to install the same number of floors, and every extra estimate is an afternoon not spent grinding. This is why we tell contractors that [closing better](/blog/how-to-close-more-floor-coating-estimates) is worth more than any marketing channel.

For exclusive, pre-qualified, booked estimates, 40 to 60 percent is a realistic close rate for a prepared crew. For shared leads and cold inquiries, it is usually 10 to 25 percent, and the estimate count balloons.

## Now Work Backward to Leads or Appointments

This is the step that decides your marketing budget.

**If you run your own Meta ads and answer your own phone:** Expect to reach and book roughly one estimate for every three to five leads, depending on how fast you call back. At 30 estimates a month for a 40 percent closer, that is 90 to 150 leads. At $15 to $45 per lead, plan on **$1,500 to $6,500 a month in ad spend**, plus a lot of phone time. Either way, the number you tell your marketing partner should be a number of **booked estimates per month**, and it should come from your install capacity, not from your bank balance.

## What Happens When Marketing Outruns the Crew

Booking 50 estimates a month for a crew that can install 12 floors creates three problems:

1. **Estimates get pushed.** Homeowners who wait two weeks for a quote cool off or call the next company.
2. **Installs get pushed.** Signed jobs scheduled six weeks out cancel at a much higher rate than jobs installed within two weeks.
3. **Reviews suffer.** A rushed crew leaves roller marks and misses edges, and one bad review costs more than the extra jobs were worth.

The fix is either a second crew or a smaller number. We would rather book a client 20 estimates they can run than 40 they cannot, which is why we ask for a target appointment count before launch and size the campaign to it. ## A Simple Capacity Worksheet

Fill this in for your own business:

1. **Install days per month you can actually run:** (usually 12 to 16)
2. **Jobs per install day:** (usually 1)
3. **Monthly job capacity:** line 1 times line 2
4. **Your honest close rate on booked estimates:** (30 to 60 percent)
5. **Booked estimates needed per month:** line 3 divided by line 4
6. **Add 15 percent for no-shows and reschedules:** line 5 times 1.15

Line 6 is the number to build your marketing around. For most single-crew coating contractors it lands between 20 and 35 booked estimates a month.

## Adding a Second Crew

When the first crew is installing 14 or more floors a month for three straight months, and you have a 20 percent cancellation rate or lower, you are ready to think about a second crew. Do not add the crew first and hope the estimates follow. Add the estimates first, watch the backlog stretch past three weeks, then hire. In our experience, a second crew needs its own [exclusive territory](/exclusive-floor-coating-leads) or a meaningfully larger service area to stay busy, because the first crew is already absorbing the demand in the original radius.
