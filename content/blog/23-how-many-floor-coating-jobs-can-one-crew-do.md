# How Many Floor Coating Jobs Can One Crew Do in a Month? (And How Many Estimates It Takes)

**Meta Description:** Capacity math for floor coating contractors: how many garage floors one crew installs a month, how many estimates that takes, and how to size marketing to it.

---

One trained two-person crew can install 12 to 16 garage floors a month in season, one polyaspartic flake floor per install day. At an illustrative 50 percent close rate and a 95 percent show rate, that takes 25 to 34 booked estimates a month. At a 60 to 70 percent lead to appointment rate, those estimates come from roughly 36 to 56 leads.

## Key takeaways

- A polyaspartic crew installs one two-car garage per day and realistically gets 12 to 16 install days a month after estimate days and weather.
- At a $3,500 average job, 12 to 16 floors is $42,000 to $56,000 in monthly revenue and $21,000 to $32,000 in gross profit for one crew.
- Divide job capacity by your close rate, then by your show rate, about 80 percent with no reminders and 95 percent with a reminder system, to get the booked estimates you need.

## Why should crew capacity set your marketing budget?

Size your marketing by what the crew can install, not by what you can afford. If a crew can put down 12 garage floors a month, you need enough booked estimates to produce 12 signed jobs, and not much more. Anything past that is a second crew or wasted spend. The conversion figures below are our published benchmarks; the dollar figures are illustrative.

## How many install days does one crew get in a month?

A booked-solid crew realistically gets 12 to 16 install days a month. A full flake [polyaspartic](/blog/polyaspartic-vs-epoxy-garage-floors) system on a standard two-car garage is a one-day install for a trained two-person crew: grind and prep in the morning, base coat and flake broadcast by midday, scrape and topcoat in the afternoon. Epoxy base coats add an overnight cure and make it a two-day job.

Start with 20 to 22 weekdays. Subtract weather, holidays, and the one or two days a week most single-crew owners spend on estimates, and you land at 12 to 16 install days. At one garage per install day, that is **12 to 16 garage floors per month** in season.

## How much revenue does one crew produce at capacity?

One crew at capacity produces $42,000 to $56,000 a month using the mid-tier $3,500 two-car garage:

- **12 floors per month:** $42,000 in revenue
- **16 floors per month:** $56,000 in revenue

With material, labor, and consumables at roughly $1,450 to $1,750 per job, that is **$21,000 to $32,000 in gross profit per month** for one crew running at capacity. Our [pricing and margins guide](/blog/floor-coating-pricing-and-margins) breaks the per-job numbers down.

## How many estimates does it take to fill the crew?

To install 12 floors you need to close 12 estimates, and how many estimates that takes depends on close rate. There is no single close rate benchmark, because it depends on the product and the estimator's skill, so treat the rates below as illustrative assumptions and plug in your own.

| Illustrative close rate | Estimates run for 12 jobs | Estimates run for 16 jobs |
|---|---|---|
| 30 percent | 40 | 53 |
| 40 percent | 30 | 40 |
| 50 percent | 24 | 32 |
| 60 percent | 20 | 27 |

A crew closing 30 percent needs twice as many estimates as a crew closing 60 percent, and every extra estimate is an afternoon not spent grinding, which is why [closing better](/blog/how-to-close-more-floor-coating-estimates) is worth more than any marketing channel. For exclusive, pre-qualified, booked estimates, 40 to 60 percent is a realistic assumption for a prepared crew.

## How many extra estimates do no-shows cost you?

No-shows mean booking more estimates than you plan to run. Our [floor coating benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) put show rate at about 80 percent with no reminders and about 95 percent with a confirmation at booking, reminders at 24 hours and 1 hour, and a person triaging homeowners who go quiet. A 40 percent closer who needs 30 estimates run must book 38 with no reminder system, or 32 with one. That gap is why the [no-show system](/blog/floor-coating-estimate-no-shows) belongs in the math.

## What happens when marketing outruns the crew?

Booking 50 estimates a month for a crew that can install 12 floors creates three problems:

1. **Estimates get pushed.** Homeowners who wait two weeks for a quote cool off or call the next company.
2. **Installs get pushed.** Signed jobs scheduled six weeks out cancel at a much higher rate than jobs installed within two weeks.
3. **Reviews suffer.** A rushed crew leaves roller marks and misses edges.

The fix is a second crew or a smaller number. We ask for a target appointment count before launch and size the campaign to it.

## How do you run the capacity worksheet for your own business?

Fill this in with your own numbers:

1. **Install days per month you can actually run:** usually 12 to 16
2. **Jobs per install day:** usually 1
3. **Monthly job capacity:** line 1 times line 2
4. **Your honest close rate on booked estimates:** illustrative range 30 to 60 percent
5. **Estimates you need to run per month:** line 3 divided by line 4
6. **Your show rate:** 0.80 with no reminders, 0.95 with confirmations, reminders, and a person triaging
7. **Booked estimates needed per month:** line 5 divided by line 6
8. **Leads needed per month if you run your own ads:** line 7 divided by 0.60 to 0.70

Line 7 is the number to build your marketing around. For a crew closing 40 to 60 percent with a reminder system in place, it lands between roughly 21 and 42 booked estimates a month. Track lines 4, 6, and 8 monthly with the other [floor coating contractor KPIs](/blog/floor-coating-contractor-kpis).

## When should you add a second crew?

Add a second crew when the first is installing 14 or more floors a month for three straight months, cancellations are 20 percent or lower, and the backlog has stretched past three weeks. Add the estimates first, then hire. A second crew usually needs its own [exclusive territory](/exclusive-floor-coating-leads) or a larger service area to stay busy.
