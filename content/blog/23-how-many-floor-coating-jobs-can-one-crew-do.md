# How Many Floor Coating Jobs Can One Crew Do in a Month? (And How Many Estimates It Takes)

**Meta Description:** Capacity math for floor coating contractors: how many garage floors one crew installs a month, how many estimates that takes, and how to size marketing to it.

---

One trained two-person crew can install 12 to 16 garage floors a month in season, one polyaspartic flake floor per install day. At an illustrative 50 percent close rate and a 95 percent show rate, that takes 25 to 34 booked estimates a month. At a 60 to 70 percent lead to appointment rate, those estimates come from roughly 36 to 56 leads.

## Key takeaways

- A polyaspartic crew can install one two-car garage per day and realistically gets 12 to 16 install days a month after estimate days, weather, and holidays.
- At a $3,500 average job, 12 to 16 floors is $42,000 to $56,000 in monthly revenue and $21,000 to $32,000 in gross profit for one crew.
- Divide job capacity by your close rate, then by your show rate, about 80 percent with no reminders and 95 percent with a reminder system, to get the booked estimates you need.
- With a call inside one minute, 60 to 70 percent of leads become booked estimates, so 30 estimates takes roughly 43 to 50 leads.
- Add a second crew only after the first has installed 14 or more floors a month for three straight months and the backlog stretches past three weeks.

## Why should crew capacity set your marketing budget?

Size your marketing by what the crew can install, not by what you can afford. If a two-person crew can put down 12 garage floors a month, you need enough booked estimates to produce 12 signed jobs, and not much more. Anything past that is either a second crew or wasted spend.

This article works through the capacity math from the install day backward to the number of estimates and leads you should be booking. The conversion figures come from our published benchmarks, and the dollar figures use the illustrative pricing model we use across this site.

## How many install days does one crew get in a month?

A booked-solid crew realistically gets 12 to 16 install days a month. A full flake [polyaspartic](/blog/polyaspartic-vs-epoxy-garage-floors) system on a standard two-car garage is a one-day install for a trained two-person crew: grind and prep in the morning, base coat and flake broadcast by midday, scrape and topcoat in the afternoon. Epoxy base coats add an overnight cure and turn it into a two-day job, which is the single biggest reason polyaspartic crews out-earn epoxy crews.

Working backward from a month:

- **Available workdays:** 20 to 22 weekdays, minus weather, holidays, and the days you spend on estimates.
- **Estimate days:** Most single-crew owners block one or two days a week for estimates and quoting, or run estimates in the late afternoon after an install.
- **Realistic install days:** 12 to 16 per month for a crew that is booked solid.

At one garage per install day, a one-crew coating business tops out around **12 to 16 garage floors per month** in season. Contractors who stack a small basement or patio onto a light day push a bit higher, but 12 to 16 is the honest planning number.

## How much revenue does one crew produce at capacity?

One crew at capacity produces $42,000 to $56,000 a month using the mid-tier $3,500 two-car garage:

- **12 floors per month:** $42,000 in revenue
- **16 floors per month:** $56,000 in revenue

With material, labor, and consumables at roughly $1,450 to $1,750 per job, that is **$21,000 to $32,000 in gross profit per month** for one crew running at capacity. Our [pricing and margins guide](/blog/floor-coating-pricing-and-margins) breaks the per-job numbers down line by line.

That is the ceiling. Most crews run well under it, not because they cannot install, but because the calendar has gaps.

## How many estimates does it take to fill the crew?

To install 12 floors you need to close 12 estimates, and how many estimates that takes depends on close rate. There is no single close rate benchmark, because it depends on the product you sell and the estimator's skill, so treat the rates below as illustrative assumptions and plug in your own.

| Illustrative close rate | Estimates run for 12 jobs | Estimates run for 16 jobs |
|---|---|---|
| 30 percent | 40 | 53 |
| 40 percent | 30 | 40 |
| 50 percent | 24 | 32 |
| 60 percent | 20 | 27 |

A crew closing 30 percent needs twice as many estimates as a crew closing 60 percent to install the same number of floors, and every extra estimate is an afternoon not spent grinding. This is why we tell contractors that [closing better](/blog/how-to-close-more-floor-coating-estimates) is worth more than any marketing channel. Coached estimators close meaningfully better, and the three objections that repeat are the one-day guys being cheaper, the spouse, and the homeowner who is just getting quotes.

For exclusive, pre-qualified, booked estimates, 40 to 60 percent is a realistic assumption for a prepared crew. For shared leads and cold inquiries, it is usually 10 to 25 percent, and the estimate count balloons.

## How many booked estimates cover no-shows?

Book more estimates than you plan to run, because not every homeowner shows. Our [floor coating benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) put show rate at about 80 percent with no reminders and about 95 percent with a confirmation at booking, a reminder 24 hours out, a reminder 1 hour out, and a person triaging homeowners who go quiet.

That gap is bigger than it looks. A 40 percent closer who needs 30 estimates run must book 38 with no reminder system, or 32 with one. Six extra booked estimates a month is a lot of ad spend or appointment fees to lose to empty driveways, which is why the [no-show system](/blog/floor-coating-estimate-no-shows) belongs in the math.

## What happens when marketing outruns the crew?

Booking 50 estimates a month for a crew that can install 12 floors creates three problems:

1. **Estimates get pushed.** Homeowners who wait two weeks for a quote cool off or call the next company.
2. **Installs get pushed.** Signed jobs scheduled six weeks out cancel at a much higher rate than jobs installed within two weeks.
3. **Reviews suffer.** A rushed crew leaves roller marks and misses edges, and one bad review costs more than the extra jobs were worth.

The fix is either a second crew or a smaller number. We would rather book a client 20 estimates they can run than 40 they cannot, which is why we ask for a target appointment count before launch and size the campaign to it. ## How do you run the capacity worksheet for your own business?

Fill this in with your own numbers:

1. **Install days per month you can actually run:** usually 12 to 16
2. **Jobs per install day:** usually 1
3. **Monthly job capacity:** line 1 times line 2
4. **Your honest close rate on booked estimates:** illustrative range 30 to 60 percent
5. **Estimates you need to run per month:** line 3 divided by line 4
6. **Your show rate:** 0.80 with no reminders, 0.95 with confirmations, reminders, and a person triaging
7. **Booked estimates needed per month:** line 5 divided by line 6
8. **Leads needed per month if you run your own ads:** line 7 divided by 0.60 to 0.70

Line 7 is the number to build your marketing around. For a crew closing 40 to 60 percent with a reminder system in place, it lands between roughly 21 and 42 booked estimates a month. Track lines 4, 6, and 8 every month alongside the other [floor coating contractor KPIs](/blog/floor-coating-contractor-kpis), because each one moves the budget.

## When should you add a second crew?

Add a second crew when the first is installing 14 or more floors a month for three straight months, your cancellation rate is 20 percent or lower, and the backlog has stretched past three weeks. Do not add the crew first and hope the estimates follow. Add the estimates first, watch the backlog stretch, then hire, and use our guide to [hiring and training a floor coating crew](/blog/how-to-hire-and-train-a-floor-coating-crew) so the second crew preps like the first. In our experience, a second crew needs its own [exclusive territory](/exclusive-floor-coating-leads) or a meaningfully larger service area to stay busy, because the first crew is already absorbing the demand in the original radius.
