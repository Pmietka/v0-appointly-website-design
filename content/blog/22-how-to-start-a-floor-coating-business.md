# How to Start a Floor Coating Business: Niche, Costs, Equipment, and Your First 20 Estimates

**Meta Description:** How to start a floor or concrete coating business: niche, independent vs franchise, startup costs, equipment, pricing, licensing, insurance, and a 90 day plan.

---

Starting a floor coating business takes $20,000 to $45,000 in year one, a planetary grinder with dust extraction, a few weeks of hands-on training, an LLC with general liability insurance, and a plan for booking the first 20 estimates. A two-person crew can install a flake garage floor in one day, charge $2,500 to $5,000 for it, and keep 50 to 60 percent as gross profit. The businesses that make it past year one are the ones that solve the calendar early.

## Key takeaways

- "Floor coating" and "concrete coating" cover garage floors, basements, patios and pool decks, decorative metallic floors, and commercial floors. Start in one, and for most people that is residential garages.
- You can start independent, buy a franchise, or become a manufacturer's certified installer. Each trades cost against control.
- Plan on $20,000 to $45,000 to start, with the grinder and dust extraction at $6,000 to $15,000 as the single most important purchase.
- Sell one system at launch, a full flake floor with a polyaspartic topcoat, priced at $5 to $8 per square foot depending on your market.
- Coatings fail because of prep. Install several floors under someone experienced before you sell your first one.
- Call every lead within one minute, because 60 to 70 percent of leads should become booked estimates at that speed.

## Why is floor coating a good trade to start right now?

Floor coating is a good trade to start because the job is fast, the ticket is high, and the result sells itself in one before-and-after photo. The equipment fits in a trailer and the training takes weeks rather than years. The part most guides skip is how you get the first 20 estimates on the calendar, so this one ends there.

## Which niche should a new coating business start in?

Concrete coating is a broad trade. Most businesses start in one of these and add others later:

| Niche | Typical customer | Why start here | Why wait |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Residential garage floors | Homeowners | One day installs, strong demand, easy to show in photos | Competitive in saturated markets |
| Basements and interior floors | Homeowners | Year round work, no weather risk | Moisture issues require more experience |
| Patios, porches, pool decks | Homeowners | Pairs well with garage jobs | UV and weather exposure need the right system |
| Decorative metallic epoxy | Homeowners, retail, offices | High ticket, visual portfolio | Skill heavy, easy to get wrong early |
| Commercial and industrial | Businesses, warehouses, shops | Large jobs, repeat work | Longer sales cycles, bids, bigger crews |

For a first year business, residential garage floors are the easiest place to start. The job is repeatable, the homeowner decides quickly, and a before and after photo does most of the selling. Sell one core product: a full broadcast flake system with a [polyaspartic](/blog/polyaspartic-vs-epoxy-garage-floors) topcoat. It installs in a day, resists hot tire pickup, and photographs well. One system, installed perfectly and priced with confidence, will outsell a menu of six. Basements, patios, and small [commercial floor coating jobs](/blog/commercial-floor-coating-jobs-for-residential-contractors) make good off season work later.

## Should you go independent, buy a franchise, or become a certified installer?

There are three common ways into the trade.

- **Independent.** You form an LLC, choose your coating system, buy your equipment, and build the brand yourself. You keep every dollar of margin and carry all of the risk of building a lead flow from nothing.
- **Franchise.** You pay an upfront fee and an ongoing royalty for a brand, a system, training, and a territory. It is the most structured path and the most expensive over time. We compare brands in [best garage floor coating franchises](/blog/best-garage-floor-coating-franchises) and break down the tradeoffs in [garage coating franchise vs going independent](/blog/garage-coating-franchise-vs-independent).
- **Certified installer for a manufacturer.** Many coating manufacturers train and certify independent installers on their system. You keep your own business and brand, and get hands on training and product support without a royalty. For someone new to concrete, this is often the best middle path.

| | Independent | Franchise | Certified installer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Upfront cost | Lowest | Highest | Low to moderate |
| Ongoing fees | None | Royalty and marketing fund | Usually none, you buy their product |
| Training | Find it yourself | Included | Included or low cost |
| Product choice | Any | Chosen for you | Usually tied to that manufacturer |
| Brand | Build your own | Theirs | Your own |

## What safety rules apply to grinding concrete?

Grinding concrete releases respirable crystalline silica, and OSHA's [silica standard for construction](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1926/1926.1153) applies the moment you have employees. Its Table 1 lists the controls for walk-behind and handheld grinders: a grinder with a shroud and a dust collector that meets the standard's airflow and filter requirements, or water delivered to the blade. It also bars dry sweeping and compressed air for cleanup when they would create exposure, and requires a written exposure control plan. Budget for the right vacuum from day one. It protects your crew, your homeowner's house, and you.

## How do you learn to prep concrete properly?

Learn prep by installing floors under someone who has done hundreds of them. Coatings fail because of prep, not product: moisture, contamination, or a slab not ground to the right profile. The slab looks fine, the coating goes down beautifully, and three months later it is lifting at the edges. Before you sell a single floor:

- **Install at least three floors under an experienced installer.** A manufacturer training class, an experienced installer, or a job with a coating company all work. Our guide to [floor coating training and certification](/blog/floor-coating-training-certification) compares the options.
- **Learn to read a slab:** moisture testing, spotting sealers and old coatings, identifying spalling that needs repair.
- **Write a prep checklist and use it every time.** Moisture test, contaminant check, grind, crack and joint repair, vacuum, then coat.
- **Know your system's limits.** Every product has minimum temperatures, recoat windows, and moisture limits on its data sheet. Follow them.

Your first year's reputation is built on floors that still look perfect two summers later. When you hire, our guide to [hiring and training a floor coating crew](/blog/how-to-hire-and-train-a-floor-coating-crew) shows how to pass that checklist down.

## How should a new floor coating business price jobs?

Price residential coating at $5 to $8 per square foot and adjust to your market. Our [floor coating benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) put saturated Florida markets closer to $5 and less saturated, wealthier metros closer to $8. An epoxy base with a polyaspartic topcoat is generally cheaper than full polyaspartic or polyurea, and decorative finishes price above the range.

A 500 square foot two-car garage at $7 per square foot is a $3,500 job. Material runs $700 to $1,000, labor for two people for a day is $600 to $900, and consumables are around $150. That leaves roughly $1,750 to $2,050 in gross profit. The full breakdown is in [floor coating pricing and margins](/blog/floor-coating-pricing-and-margins), and the line by line estimate is in [how to bid an epoxy floor job](/blog/how-to-bid-epoxy-floor-job).

## What paperwork, licensing, and insurance do you need?

You need an LLC, general liability insurance, whatever license your state requires, and a written contract with a clear warranty.

- **Form an LLC** and open a separate business bank account before you take a deposit.
- **General liability insurance** of at least $1 million is standard, and homeowners' associations and commercial clients will ask for a certificate. The SBA's guide to [business insurance](https://www.sba.gov/business-guide/launch-your-business/get-business-insurance) explains the common policy types, and our [floor coating business insurance guide](/blog/floor-coating-business-insurance) covers what a coating shop needs.
- **Licensing** varies. Some states require a specialty contractor license for coatings, some fold it into general contracting, and some require only a business license. Check with your state contractor board first.
- **Contracts and warranties:** Use a written agreement with scope, price, deposit, and a warranty that excludes moisture failures on slabs that fail a moisture test. Our [floor coating warranty guide](/blog/floor-coating-warranty-guide) covers what to promise and what to exclude.

## How do you build proof before you have reviews?

Make proof out of the work itself. Photograph every floor: before, during grind, flake broadcast, and finished. Ask every customer for a Google review while you are still standing in their garage, ideally with an [NFC review card](/blog/nfc-review-cards-floor-coating-contractors). Five reviews with photos will outperform a beautiful website with none. Coat your own garage, a family member's, and a friend's at cost in the first month. Those three floors are your portfolio.

## How does the season affect a new coating business?

Demand for garage floors peaks in spring and early summer, dips in the heat, and drops in winter everywhere the slab gets cold. Florida, the Gulf Coast, and the Carolinas book year round. Polyaspartic widens the winter window, but expect your calendar to swing. Start marketing in late winter so you enter [peak season](/blog/best-time-of-year-for-floor-coating-jobs) with a backlog, and use slow months for basements and commercial work.

## What mistakes sink new coating businesses?

- **Selling too many systems too soon.** Pick one system you can install perfectly and sell it with confidence.
- **Cutting corners on prep.** One failed floor costs more in callbacks and reviews than ten good ones earn.
- **Pricing to beat the cheapest quote.** New owners underprice to win jobs and then cannot afford to market or fix mistakes.
- **Skipping insurance and contracts.** Get coverage and a written contract with a clear warranty from day one.
- **Waiting to market until the first floors are done.** Start building the calendar while you are still training.

## What does a realistic first year look like?

A one-crew coating business that runs two to three garage floors a week at an average of $3,500 produces $28,000 to $42,000 a month in revenue during season, with gross profit around 50 to 60 percent before marketing and overhead. Most first-year owner-operators land well below that while they learn, then hit it in year two once the calendar is predictable.
