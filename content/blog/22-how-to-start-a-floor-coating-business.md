# How to Start a Floor Coating Business: Costs, Equipment, and Your First 20 Estimates

**Meta Description:** A practical guide to starting a garage floor coating business: startup costs, equipment, pricing, licensing, insurance, and how to book your first 20 estimates.

---

Starting a floor coating business takes $20,000 to $45,000 in year one, a planetary grinder with dust extraction, a few weeks of hands-on training, an LLC with general liability insurance, and a plan for booking the first 20 estimates. A two-person crew can install a flake garage floor in one day, charge $2,500 to $5,000 for it, and keep 50 to 60 percent as gross profit.

## Key takeaways

- Plan on $20,000 to $45,000 to start, with the grinder and dust extraction at $6,000 to $15,000 as the single most important purchase.
- Sell one system at launch, a full flake floor with a polyaspartic topcoat, and price it at $5 to $8 per square foot depending on your market.
- Call every lead within one minute, because 60 to 70 percent of leads should become booked estimates at that speed.

## Why is floor coating a good trade to start right now?

Floor coating is a good trade to start because the job is fast, the ticket is high, and the result sells itself in one before-and-after photo. The equipment fits in a trailer and the training takes weeks rather than years. The part most guides skip is how you get the first 20 estimates on the calendar, so this one ends there.

## What should a new floor coating business sell?

Sell one core product: a full broadcast flake system with a [polyaspartic](/blog/polyaspartic-vs-epoxy-garage-floors) topcoat for garages. It installs in a day, resists hot tire pickup, and photographs well. Basements, patios, and small commercial floors come later. One system, installed perfectly and priced with confidence, will outsell a menu of six.

## How much does it cost to start a floor coating business?

Most people start a coating business for $20,000 to $45,000, with the low end assuming used equipment and a vehicle you already own. Realistic ranges for a residential-focused startup:

- **Grinder and dust extraction:** $6,000 to $15,000. A quality planetary grinder with a HEPA vac is the single most important purchase. Rent for the first few jobs if cash is tight.
- **Hand tools and prep gear:** $1,500 to $3,000. Edge grinder, crack chasing tools, squeegees, spiked shoes, rollers, mixing drills, moisture meter, and PPE.
- **Trailer or van:** $5,000 to $20,000 used. Enclosed and lockable, so material stays out of temperature swings.
- **Initial material:** $2,000 to $4,000. Enough base coat, flake, and topcoat for your first three to five jobs.
- **Insurance, LLC, and licensing:** $1,500 to $4,000 in year one, depending on the state.
- **Training:** $0 to $3,000. Manufacturer training days are often free or cheap if you buy their system.
- **Marketing to get the first jobs:** $2,000 to $5,000. More on this below.

## How do you learn to prep concrete properly?

Learn prep by installing floors under someone who has done hundreds of them. Coatings fail because of prep, not product: moisture, contamination, or a slab not ground to the right profile. Before you sell a single floor:

- Install at least three floors under an experienced installer. Pay for the training if you have to.
- Learn to read a slab: moisture testing, spotting sealers and old coatings, identifying spalling that needs repair.
- Build a written prep checklist and follow it on every job, no matter how simple the floor looks.

Your first year's reputation is built on floors that still look perfect two summers later. When you hire, our guide to [hiring and training a floor coating crew](/blog/how-to-hire-and-train-a-floor-coating-crew) shows how to pass that checklist down.

## How should a new floor coating business price jobs?

Price residential coating at $5 to $8 per square foot and adjust to your market. Our [floor coating benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) put saturated Florida markets closer to $5 and less saturated, wealthier metros closer to $8. An epoxy base with a polyaspartic topcoat is generally cheaper than full polyaspartic or polyurea, and decorative finishes price above the range.

A 500 square foot two-car garage at $7 per square foot is a $3,500 job. Material runs $700 to $1,000, labor for two people for a day is $600 to $900, and consumables are around $150. That leaves roughly $1,750 to $2,050 in gross profit. The full breakdown is in our guide to [floor coating pricing and margins](/blog/floor-coating-pricing-and-margins).

## What paperwork, licensing, and insurance do you need?

You need an LLC, general liability insurance, whatever license your state requires, and a written contract with a clear warranty.

- **Form an LLC** and open a separate business bank account before you take a deposit.
- **General liability insurance** of at least $1 million is standard, and some homeowners' associations and commercial clients will ask for a certificate.
- **Licensing** varies. Some states require a specialty contractor license for coatings, some fold it into general contracting, and some require only a business license. Check with your state board first.
- **Contracts and warranties:** Use a written agreement with scope, price, deposit, and a warranty that excludes moisture failures on slabs that fail a moisture test. Our [floor coating warranty guide](/blog/floor-coating-warranty-guide) covers what to promise and what to exclude.

## How do you build proof before you have reviews?

Make proof out of the work itself. Photograph every floor: before, during grind, flake broadcast, and finished. Ask every customer for a Google review while you are still standing in their garage, ideally with an [NFC review card](/blog/nfc-review-cards-floor-coating-contractors). Five reviews with photos will outperform a beautiful website with none. Coat your own garage, a family member's, and a friend's at cost in the first month. Those three floors are your portfolio.

## How does the season affect a new coating business?

Demand for garage floors peaks in spring and early summer, dips in the heat, and drops in winter everywhere the slab gets cold. Florida, the Gulf Coast, and the Carolinas book year round. Polyaspartic widens the winter window, but expect your calendar to swing. Start marketing in late winter so you enter [peak season](/blog/best-time-of-year-for-floor-coating-jobs) with a backlog, and use slow months for basements and commercial work.

## What does a realistic first year look like?

A one-crew coating business that runs two to three garage floors a week at an average of $3,500 produces $28,000 to $42,000 a month in revenue during season, with gross profit around 50 to 60 percent before marketing and overhead. Most first-year owner-operators land well below that while they learn, then hit it in year two once the calendar is predictable. The biggest variable is whether estimates keep coming while you are on the job.
