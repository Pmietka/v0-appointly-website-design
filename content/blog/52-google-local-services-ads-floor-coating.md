# Google Local Services Ads for Floor Coating and Epoxy Contractors: How LSA Works and When It Pays

**Meta Description:** How Google Local Services Ads work for floor coating and epoxy contractors: pay per lead, the Google Verified badge, screening, credits, costs, and speed.

---

Google Local Services Ads (LSA) can work for floor coating and epoxy contractors, but only if the category is available where you operate and someone answers every call fast. You pay per lead rather than per click, you have to pass Google's screening to earn the Google Verified badge, and your ranking depends heavily on reviews and how responsive you are. Google's US category list includes "Flooring services," but no epoxy or garage coating category by name, so check eligibility for your exact business and service area before you plan around it.

## Key takeaways

- LSA charges per lead (a call, message, or booking), not per click. Your bid is the maximum you are willing to pay for a lead.
- Google now uses a single Google Verified badge. The old Google Guaranteed and Google Screened badges, and the money back guarantee tied to the Guarantee, are being phased out.
- Screening for flooring and general contractor categories can include background checks, business registration, liability insurance, and state licenses where local law requires them.
- Google says missed calls and slow responses can hurt your ad ranking. Responsiveness is not optional on LSA.
- LSA, Google Search ads, and Meta ads catch different homeowners. Judge all three by cost per booked estimate, not cost per lead.

## How do Google Local Services Ads work?

Local Services Ads show in their own unit at the top of Google Search when someone searches for a service in an area you cover. According to Google's [getting started guide](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en), the homeowner can call you, send a message, or book directly from the ad, and you get an email for each new contact. From there the lead is yours to convert.

The pieces that matter for a coating contractor:

1. **You pick a category, service area, and the jobs you do.** Your profile ties to your Google Business Profile, which Google requires to be public and verified before you can advertise.
2. **You set a budget and a bid.** Google's [ad ranking page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/7527305?hl=en) describes your bid as the maximum amount you are willing to pay for a lead. You may pay less.
3. **You pay per lead, not per click.** Google says you pay only for leads related to your business and the services you offer. A homeowner who scrolls past your listing costs nothing.
4. **Your ranking depends on more than money.** Google lists your bid, your responsiveness to customer inquiries, your review rating and count, your average response time, and the verification checks you have completed.

That is what makes LSA different from regular Google Ads: a higher bid alone does not buy the top spot if you miss calls.

## What happened to Google Guaranteed and Google Screened?

If you have read older articles about LSA, you have seen two badges: Google Guaranteed for home services and Google Screened for professional services. That is changing.

Google's [About Google Verified badge](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/9376654?hl=en) page says Google is simplifying advertiser badging with a single Google Verified badge for all advertisers. Existing advertisers who already completed verification get it automatically. New advertisers earn it by completing the screening and verification process. Google also says it is discontinuing the Money Back Guarantee that came with the Google Guarantee badge, with reimbursement requests accepted only for services booked before December 7, 2025.

For you, the practical takeaways are:

- The badge still signals that Google checked your business, and it still sits next to your listing.
- Do not advertise a "Google Guaranteed" money back promise to homeowners. Check the current wording on Google's help page before you put any badge language on your trucks, website, or estimates.
- Google's getting started page says providers who go live before finishing all requirements are placed below providers who have completed them. Finishing verification is worth the paperwork.

## What does Google check before a floor coating contractor can run LSA?

Google's [business screening and verification requirements](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/12174778?hl=en) list the checks by category. For "Flooring services" and "General contractor" in the US, the list includes:

| Check | What Google lists | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Background check | Business check and owner check | Marked "select users only" |
| Business registration | Business and business representative check | Marked "select users only" |
| Insurance | General liability insurance | Marked "select users only" |
| Licenses | Business license and owner license at the state level | Where applicable by local law |
| Google Business Profile | Public and verified profile | Required for all LSA advertisers |
| Reviews | Subject to category requirements | Listed as a go live requirement |

"Select users only" means Google does not apply every check to every advertiser. You find out what applies to you during onboarding. Have your liability certificate, business registration, and any state contractor license ready before you start, so a missing document does not hold up your listing.

Whether you need a contractor license for coating work depends on your state and sometimes your city. Check your state licensing board directly rather than assuming. If your state does not require one, Google's page says licenses are required only where applicable by local law.

## Is the floor coating category available in my area?

This is the question to answer before anything else, and the honest answer is: check for your business.

Google's US [getting started page](https://support.google.com/localservices/answer/6224841?hl=en) lists "Flooring services," "General contractor," "Painter," "Handyman," and "Foundations services" among its categories. It does not list epoxy, concrete coating, or garage floor coating as a category by name. Google also says LSA is available "only for certain service categories" and limits some categories to specific states, which tells you availability is not uniform.

So before you build a plan around LSA:

1. Go to Google's [Local Services Ads eligibility page](https://ads.google.com/local-services-ads/) and enter your business type and zip code.
2. See which categories and job types appear for your area, and whether garage floor coating or epoxy fits inside one of them.
3. If the closest fit is a broader flooring category, think about who else shows up there. Your listing may sit next to carpet, tile, and hardwood installers, and the homeowner searching "flooring near me" may not want a coating at all.

Google's [invalid leads troubleshooter](https://support.google.com/localservices/troubleshooter/11390630?hl=en) says it no longer credits leads for "job type not serviced" or "geo not serviced." That means if a homeowner calls about hardwood and you only do coatings, you may pay for that call. Set your job types and service area tightly.

## How much do Google Local Services Ads cost for floor coating?

We do not publish an LSA cost per lead benchmark, and we would be guessing if we gave you one. LSA prices move with your market, your category, how many other providers are bidding, and how you set your bid. Google shows you an estimate during setup, and that estimate for your zip codes is the most honest number you will get.

What we can give you is a way to judge it. Our [lead cost breakdown](/blog/garage-floor-coating-leads-cost-and-sources) puts Google search leads for coating terms at roughly $100 to $300 per lead, with exclusive Meta leads typically $15 to $45 and shared marketplace leads $30 to $120. Put your LSA quote next to those numbers, then run it through the only math that matters:

| Step | Example inputs (illustrative, not a benchmark) |
| --- | --- |
| Cost per LSA lead | Use the estimate Google shows for your area |
| Share of leads that are real coating jobs in your area | Track it for 30 days |
| Share of real leads that book an estimate | Our benchmark with fast contact: 60 to 70% |
| Your close rate on shown estimates | Your own number |
| Cost per installed floor | Cost per lead ÷ (real share × booked share × close rate) |

Worked example with made up inputs: if a lead costs $120, 80% are real coating jobs, 65% of those book, and you close 40% of shown estimates, your cost per installed floor is $120 ÷ (0.80 × 0.65 × 0.40), or about $577. Change any input and the answer moves a lot, which is why we push contractors to track [cost per qualified estimate](/blog/cost-per-qualified-floor-coating-estimate) instead of cost per lead.

### What about credits for bad leads?

Google's troubleshooter says leads determined to be invalid or low quality when the customer first makes contact are not charged, and some leads can be credited automatically later if Google's models judge them low quality. In most cases credits post within 30 days, and the original charge still shows on your invoice. For leads you think are wrong, Google points you to rate the lead in the Leads section. Google has changed its dispute process before, so check what your account shows today rather than relying on older blog posts.

## LSA vs Google Search ads vs Meta ads: which should a coating contractor run?

They do different jobs. LSA and Search both catch homeowners who are already looking. Meta finds homeowners who were not looking yet. We compare Search and Meta in depth in [Google Ads vs Meta Ads for floor coating](/blog/google-ads-vs-meta-ads-floor-coating). Here is where LSA fits.

| Factor | Google Local Services Ads | Google Search ads | Meta ads |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| You pay for | A lead (call, message, booking) | A click | Impressions or results, depending on setup |
| Homeowner intent | High, searching for a provider now | High, searching for the service now | Lower at first, interest sparked by a before and after |
| Volume limit | Search volume in your category and area | Search volume for your keywords | Budget and creative |
| Setup burden | Screening, documents, reviews | Keywords, ads, landing page | Creative, landing page, constant refresh |
| What controls ranking or delivery | Bid, responsiveness, reviews, verification | Bid and ad quality | Creative quality and audience response |
| Biggest risk | Paying for off target calls in a broad category | High cost per click on competitive terms | Leads cool fast if nobody calls right away |

The short version:

- **Run LSA** if it is available for your business in your area, your reviews are solid, and someone can pick up the phone during the day. It is a low effort way to catch searchers.
- **Run Search ads** if you want control over exactly which coating terms you show for, and your market has enough search volume to matter.
- **Run Meta** when you need more estimates than search demand in your area can supply. On Meta, the creative is the targeting, and a strong before and after reaches homeowners who never searched.

## How do you answer LSA leads fast enough?

Many LSA leads are phone calls, which sounds easier than a form. It is not, if your phone is in your truck while you are grinding a slab.

Google's ad ranking page says your responsiveness affects how likely your ad is to produce leads and that missed calls can hurt it. The getting started guide says the same: regularly failing to answer calls or respond to messages can affect your ranking. So a missed call costs you twice. You lose the homeowner, and you lose position for the next one.

What works:

1. **Route LSA calls to whoever is not on a job site.** If that is nobody during the day, that is the real problem to solve.
2. **Set your hours honestly.** If you cannot answer at 7 pm, do not show as open.
3. **Turn on message leads only if someone answers them fast.** Google suggests message and booking leads can help when you cannot answer the phone at night and on weekends. A message answered the next afternoon is a lost lead.
4. **Book the estimate on the first call.** The goal is a day and time on the calendar, not a phone quote. Our [lead follow up scripts](/blog/floor-coating-lead-follow-up-scripts) work the same for LSA calls.
5. **Ask every customer for a review.** Reviews feed LSA ranking directly. See our [Google reviews guide](/blog/google-reviews-floor-coating-contractors).

Speed matters for every channel. Our [benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) show about 90% of homeowners answer when the call goes out within a minute, and [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-wins-floor-coating-jobs) is the single biggest lever on how many paid leads become booked estimates. On LSA, the homeowner is often calling you, so the equivalent is simply answering live.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can epoxy flooring contractors use Google Local Services Ads?

Possibly. Google's US category list includes "Flooring services" and "General contractor," but no epoxy or garage coating category by name, and availability varies. Check eligibility for your business type and zip code on Google's Local Services Ads site to see which categories and job types are open to you.

### Is Google Guaranteed still available?

Google's help page says it is moving to a single Google Verified badge for all advertisers and discontinuing the Money Back Guarantee tied to the Google Guarantee badge, with reimbursement only for services booked before December 7, 2025. Check Google's current help page before using any badge language in your marketing.

### How much do LSA leads cost for floor coating?

It depends on your market, category, competition, and bid, and Google shows an estimate during setup. As a reference point, our lead cost breakdown puts Google search leads for coating terms at roughly $100 to $300. Track what share of your LSA leads are real coating jobs, because off target calls in a broad category raise your true cost.

### Do you get refunds for bad LSA leads?

Google says leads it judges invalid or low quality at first contact are not charged, and some can be credited automatically later, usually within 30 days. Google also says it no longer credits "job type not serviced" or "geo not serviced" leads. You can rate leads in your account, and you should check the current dispute options there.

### Is LSA better than Meta ads for floor coating?

They do different jobs. LSA catches homeowners already searching for a provider, limited by search volume in your area. Meta reaches homeowners who were not searching yet, limited mainly by budget and creative. Many contractors run both and judge them by cost per booked estimate.
