# How to Raise Your Average Floor Coating Ticket Without Discounting or Pushing

**Meta Description:** How floor coating contractors raise their average ticket without discounting or pushing: measure every surface, quote three options, sell stem walls, and book educated homeowners.

---

The fastest way for a floor coating contractor to grow revenue without buying more leads is to raise the average ticket. That does not mean pushing. It means measuring every surface the homeowner cares about, quoting three clear options instead of one number, offering stem walls and adjacent surfaces on every estimate, and sitting down with homeowners who already understand the product. Contractors who do those four things consistently see bigger jobs from the same calendar.

## Key takeaways

- Your marketing cost per job is roughly the same whether the job is $2,500 or $4,500, so every dollar added to the ticket drops almost straight to profit.
- Quote three options (good, better, best) instead of one price. Homeowners compare your options to each other instead of to the other contractor.
- Offer stem walls on every garage. It is the easiest add on in the trade and most homeowners have never heard of it.
- Walk the whole property. Porches, patios, basements, and a second garage or shop are jobs you only find if you look.
- Price per square foot is a bad opening line. Lead with the system and the result, then give the number.
- A homeowner who was educated on the first phone call picks the better system more often. That is a lead quality question, not a closing trick.

## Why does average ticket matter more than lead cost?

Because the cost to get you in the driveway barely changes with the size of the job.

A standard two car garage runs roughly 400 to 500 square feet. Our [benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) put garage floor coating at $5 to $8 per square foot depending on the market, so a typical two car garage lands somewhere between $2,000 and $4,000. Whether that homeowner picks the basic system or the full system with stem walls, you spent the same on ads, the same time on the phone, and the same drive to the estimate.

Here is a simple example. These are illustration numbers, not a client's results.

| | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jobs closed per month | 8 | 8 |
| Average ticket | $3,000 | $3,600 |
| Monthly revenue | $24,000 | $28,800 |
| Extra marketing spend | | $0 |

That is $4,800 more a month, close to $58,000 a year, without one more lead. Try getting that from cutting your cost per lead.

## Where do the extra dollars actually come from?

Five places, and most estimators are only using one or two of them.

### 1. Stem walls

Stem walls are the short concrete curb along the base of the garage walls. Coating them makes the floor look finished edge to edge and protects the part of the concrete that takes the most splash from salt and water.

Most homeowners have never heard the term. When the estimator points at the stained, chipped curb and shows a photo of a coated one next to it, a large share of homeowners say yes. It is a small line item on its own, and it adds up fast when it is on every estimate.

### 2. The system upgrade

Most contractors sell more than one system: a basic one, a full broadcast flake system, and a premium topcoat or polyaspartic option. The difference between them is durability, UV stability, and how long the floor looks new.

The homeowner cannot see those differences on a sample board. You have to explain them. If you need the talking points, [polyaspartic vs epoxy garage floors](/blog/polyaspartic-vs-epoxy-garage-floors) covers the real differences in plain terms.

### 3. Adjacent surfaces

While you are on site, walk the property. Front porch, back patio, pool deck, basement, laundry room, the walkway from the driveway to the door. A homeowner who is excited about the garage is often happy to do the porch at the same time, especially when you point out it saves a second visit.

Only quote surfaces your system and your warranty actually cover. Exterior surfaces get sun and weather the garage never sees.

### 4. The second space

Detached garages, workshops, a third bay, a golf cart garage, a barn. Ask the question: "Is there any other concrete on the property you would want done while we are here?" You will be surprised how often the answer is yes.

### 5. A longer warranty tier

If your supplier supports it, a premium system with a longer warranty is a natural top option. The homeowner is buying peace of mind, and the premium system usually carries more margin. The [floor coating warranty guide](/blog/floor-coating-warranty-guide) covers how to structure warranty terms you can actually stand behind.

A note on prep: crack repair, joint filling, and moisture mitigation are not upsells. If the slab needs them, they are part of doing the job right. Price them as clear line items and explain why, but do not treat them as a way to pad the ticket.

## How do you present three options at the kitchen table?

One price gives the homeowner two choices: yes or no. Three options give them a better question: which one.

| Option | What it includes | Who picks it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Good | Your standard system, floor only | The budget focused homeowner |
| Better | Full flake system, floor and stem walls | Most homeowners, when it is explained well |
| Best | Premium topcoat or polyaspartic, stem walls, longest warranty | Homeowners who plan to stay and want it done once |

A few rules make the three option quote work:

- **Build the options around the system, not discounts.** Each tier should be a real difference the homeowner can understand.
- **Recommend one.** "If this were my garage, I would do the middle one, and here is why." Homeowners want an expert opinion.
- **Keep the good option honest.** It should be a floor you are proud of. If it is a trap, the homeowner feels it.
- **Show the price for all three on one page.** Walking through them one at a time feels like a sales pitch.

## What pulls the average ticket down?

The same few habits show up in almost every contractor whose ticket is stuck:

- **Leading with price per square foot.** The moment the conversation starts at "$5 a foot," the homeowner compares you on price alone. Lead with the system and the result.
- **Quoting by text or over the phone.** A text quote is always for the cheapest version. You never get to show the stem walls or walk the property.
- **Discounting on the spot.** One "I can knock off 10%" teaches the homeowner that the first number was not real.
- **Skipping the walkthrough.** If the estimator only measures the garage, the porch and the shop never come up.
- **Low intent leads.** A homeowner who filled out a form on a marketplace to compare three prices is shopping the cheapest option by design. We cover why in [exclusive vs shared floor coating leads](/blog/exclusive-vs-shared-floor-coating-leads).

## How does the first phone call raise the ticket?

A homeowner who shows up to the estimate already understanding the product picks the better system more often.

That is the part of the ticket that gets decided before the estimator ever arrives. When the first call explains the difference between a basic coating and a full system, why stem walls matter, and why a professional install outlasts a hardware store kit, the homeowner is not hearing it for the first time at the kitchen table. They are confirming what they already decided they want.

## Which numbers should you track?

Average ticket on its own hides what is working. Track these every month:

1. **Average ticket per estimator.** If one estimator is $800 above the other on the same leads, you have a training opportunity.
2. **Stem wall attach rate.** What share of closed garages include stem walls?
3. **Option mix.** What share picks good, better, and best? If almost everyone picks good, the options are not being explained.
4. **Adjacent surface rate.** How many jobs include a second surface?

## Frequently asked questions

### What is a good average ticket for garage floor coating?

It depends on the market, the system, and the size of the garage. At $5 to $8 per square foot, a typical two car garage of 400 to 500 square feet lands between $2,000 and $4,000 before stem walls and add ons. Premium systems and larger garages run higher.

### What are stem walls on a garage floor coating job?

Stem walls are the short concrete curb along the base of the garage walls. Coating them makes the floor look finished from edge to edge and protects the concrete that takes the most splash from water and road salt. It is one of the easiest add ons to offer on every estimate.

### Should floor coating contractors give three options on every estimate?

Yes. A single price gives the homeowner a yes or no decision. Three options (good, better, best) built around real differences in the system let the homeowner choose between your options instead of comparing you to another contractor, and most homeowners pick the middle option when it is explained well.

### How do you raise your average ticket without being pushy?

Measure every surface, explain the real differences between systems, recommend the option you would put in your own garage, and let the homeowner choose. Pushy is discounting, pressure, and fake deadlines. Explaining what the homeowner is actually buying is just doing the estimate well.

### Does lead quality affect average ticket?

Yes. Homeowners who come from shared marketplaces are often shopping for the lowest price. Homeowners who were educated on the product during the first phone call arrive understanding the difference between systems and are more likely to choose the better one.
