# How Reviews and Your Google Profile Help You Close More Floor Coating Estimates

**Meta Description:** Your reviews and job photos close the floor coating estimates you already booked. A contractor's guide to building proof on Google that wins more jobs.

---

Reviews and a complete Google Business Profile help you close more floor coating estimates because plenty of homeowners look you up between booking and your arrival. A profile with 20 or more job photos, a rating above 4.5 stars, and a response to every review means you walk in already trusted. Reputation doesn't fill the calendar; it closes what's already on it.

## Key takeaways

- A booked homeowner often checks your Google Business Profile before you arrive, so the profile's job is closing, not lead generation.
- Aim for at least 20 photos, weighted toward before-and-after shots of real garage and basement floors.
- Ask for a review after every job with a direct link; most contractors get 1 review per 25 to 50 jobs, and asking every time beats that.
- Respond to every review, good or bad, because the next homeowner reads your responses too.
- A rating of 4.7 or higher across 30 or more reviews means one bad review barely registers.

## Why is your online reputation a closing tool?

Your reputation is a closing tool because the homeowner has usually booked you before they read it. When someone has an estimate on the calendar with you, plenty of them pull up your name on their phone before you arrive. The first thing they see is your Google Business Profile: your photos, your star rating, your reviews.

That moment decides a lot. A profile loaded with glossy before-and-after floors and a stack of real five-star reviews tells them they made a good call booking you. An empty or neglected profile plants doubt before you ring the doorbell.

This isn't about getting found. Getting in front of homeowners is the job of [Meta ads](/blog/meta-ads-for-floor-coating-contractors) and a fast call that turns the lead into a booked estimate. Your reputation's job is to make that booked homeowner confident enough to say yes when you quote the floor.

Our [benchmarks](/floor-coating-benchmarks) put no single number on close rate, because it depends on the product and the estimator's skill. But two of the three objections that repeat, "the one-day guys are cheaper" and "I am just getting quotes", are much easier to answer when the homeowner has already read 30 reviews from neighbors who paid your price and were glad they did.

## How do you claim and verify your listing?

Start by making sure you actually own your listing. Go to google.com/business and search for your company name. If a listing already exists (Google sometimes creates them automatically), claim it. If it doesn't, create one.

Google will ask you to verify the business. Depending on your area that's a postcard with a code, a video verification, or a phone call. Do it now if you haven't already. There's no shortcut here. Once verified, you own the listing and can edit everything about it, which is where you turn it into a trust asset.

## What should a complete profile include?

A complete profile fills every field, because a half-finished one looks sloppy to a homeowner checking you out. Most contractors skip this step or do it halfway.

- Business name. Make sure it matches your legal name exactly.
- Service area. List every city and area where you actually work, so a homeowner sees you cover their neighborhood.
- Phone number. Use the number customers call.
- Website. Link to your actual website, not a generic landing page.
- Business hours. Make them accurate. If you're closed Sundays, say so.
- Service categories. Pick "Flooring Contractor" or "Concrete Contractor" and any others that apply.
- Description. Write a short paragraph about your business. Mention epoxy floors, polyaspartic coatings, garage floor coatings, basement and patio floors, whatever you do.

Don't leave blanks. A complete, polished profile signals a real, established business, which is exactly what a homeowner wants to see before they sign.

## How many photos does a floor coating profile need?

Aim for at least 20 photos, and keep adding. Photos convince a homeowner you do beautiful work before you ever open your mouth, and floor coating is one of the most photogenic trades there is. Start with these:

- 3 to 5 photos of actual job sites. Show the crew grinding the concrete, the flake broadcast, and the glossy finished floor. Real work photos beat stock images every time.
- Before-and-after shots of a stained, cracked garage floor turned into a clean, glossy epoxy floor. Homeowners can't get enough of these.
- 2 to 3 photos of your equipment and truck, so they see you're established.
- 2 photos of your team. People buy from people.
- 1 to 2 photos of your shop or showroom floor.

Add captions so a browsing homeowner knows what they're looking at: "epoxy garage floor coating in [city]" or "[polyaspartic](/blog/polyaspartic-vs-epoxy-garage-floors) basement floor installation." A profile that's clearly active and full of recent work reassures a homeowner that you're busy and good at what you do.

## How do you get more Google reviews after every job?

Ask every customer, right after the job, with a direct link. Reviews are the single most powerful piece of social proof you have. If a homeowner sees 42 glowing reviews while deciding on your quote, the decision gets a lot easier. If you've got 3, they hesitate.

After you finish a floor, send the customer a text or email with a link to your review page. Don't make them search. Something like: "Hey John, thanks for letting us coat your garage floor. If you love how it turned out, we'd really appreciate a quick review on Google. Takes 2 minutes. [LINK]" A tap-to-review card left with the homeowner does the same job on the spot; see our guide to [NFC review cards for floor coating contractors](/blog/nfc-review-cards-floor-coating-contractors).

Most contractors get 1 review for every 25 to 50 jobs completed. If you're finishing 5 floors a month and you ask every time, you should get a new review every couple of weeks. That stacks up to a couple dozen a year, and the next homeowner you quote sees a wall of proof that you deliver.

Respond to every review, positive or negative. Say thanks. Answer questions. Handle a complaint with class. A homeowner reads those responses too, and seeing you take care of people closes the deal.

## Why do detailed reviews close more jobs than one-liners?

A detailed, specific review is far more convincing than a one-word "Great!" because the next homeowner sees themselves in the story. The more a customer describes (the stained slab you transformed, how clean your crew left the garage, how the floor has held up), the more the reader pictures their own garage.

When you ask for a review, nudge them toward specifics. "If you can mention what we did and how it turned out, that really helps other homeowners" goes a long way. Reviews from different neighborhoods also show a homeowner that people near them trusted you and were glad they did.

## How often should you post on your profile?

Post a finished job every week or two. A profile that hasn't been touched in a year looks abandoned, and fresh activity tells a homeowner you're a working, current business. Posts that work:

- "Just finished a 600 sq ft epoxy garage floor in [city]. Full flake system, polyaspartic topcoat, ready to drive on in 24 hours."
- "Cold weather is coming. Polyaspartic coatings cure fast even in lower temps, so there's no bad season to upgrade your garage floor."
- "New crew training this week. Getting faster and cleaner on every grind and broadcast."
- "Spring home improvement season is here. Ask us about your garage floor."

Don't sell hard. Educate, share wins, keep it real. When a booked homeowner checks you out, they should see a business that's clearly active and proud of its work.

## What happens when a booked homeowner looks you up?

When a homeowner with a booked estimate looks you up, the sequence that closes the job runs like this:

1. They get the appointment confirmation and, like most homeowners, search your name.
2. They land on a complete profile full of before-and-after floors.
3. They scroll a stack of detailed five-star reviews from real neighbors.
4. They see you responding to every one of them.
5. By the time you knock, they already trust you, so you're not selling from scratch.

That's the entire value of your reputation. It doesn't replace the [booked estimate](/floor-coating-leads), and it doesn't replace the [speed to lead](/blog/speed-to-lead-wins-floor-coating-jobs) that got the estimate booked. It makes the booked estimate easy to close.

## Frequently asked questions

### How quickly does a strong Google profile help a floor coating contractor close more jobs?

As soon as it's built. The moment a booked homeowner looks you up and sees real floor photos and real reviews, you're quoting from a position of trust instead of starting cold. There's no ranking to wait on, because the homeowner already has your name from the appointment confirmation and is searching for you directly.

### Do I need a website as well as a Google Business Profile?

It helps. A clean site that shows off your finished floors reinforces everything your profile says, and some homeowners click through to it from the listing. You don't need anything fancy. A fast page that loads well on a phone, a gallery of glossy garages and basements, and a simple way to request a quote is enough to show you're real.

### What is a good Google review rating for a floor coating contractor?

Anything above 4.5 stars reassures a homeowner, and 4.7 or higher is the target. Volume matters as much as the average. A single bad review stings if you only have 5 reviews, but once you reach 30 or more, one bad one barely moves the rating and the next homeowner reads it as an outlier.

### How should I respond to a negative review?

Respond quickly, calmly, and in public. Thank the customer, acknowledge the problem plainly, say what you did or offered to fix it, and invite them to call you directly. Never argue. The reply isn't really for that customer; it's for the next homeowner reading your profile before an estimate, who wants to see how you handle it when a floor goes wrong.

### Should I manage my Google profile myself or hand it off?

If you can spend 30 minutes a week on it, do it yourself, because nobody knows the jobs and the customers better than you. If you can't, hire someone to keep it active with fresh photos, posts, and review responses. Either way, don't let it go stale. A neglected profile costs you closes on estimates you already paid to book.
